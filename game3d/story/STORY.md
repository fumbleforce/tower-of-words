# Day one

The intro day only. Dialogue data: train.js, gate.js, office.js, transitions.js (format in FORMAT.md). How people talk: VOICE.md. Engine needs: REQUESTS.md. Critic scores: REVIEW.md. About 15 to 20 minutes.

## Premise

Eric, a tired Nordic IT engineer, starts a support contract: he's here to help keep Amakawa's ancient systems running, because the company refuses to replace any of them. He's based in the IT support room on B2 with a small team. He barely speaks Japanese. Only Mio on the team speaks some English, and she's busy. The island's old systems, installed in the nineties, answer to spoken commands (kotodama), but only when Eric says them. Nobody knows why, Eric included.

## Language on day 1

- Mio speaks loose, imperfect English (VOICE.md). Kenji has a few words. Everyone else speaks Japanese. Lines Eric can't follow are marked `overheard: true`: they show and sound as gibberish, with only the words he has learned (and loanwords he'd catch by ear) shown clearly. Dialogue and gestures carry what he needs; narration only covers what the scene can't show.
- Each new word ends with Eric typing its romaji (`{ do: 'type' }`). Choice buttons are for real choices.
- Greetings (from Mio's lesson on the train): おはようございます (ohayō gozaimasu, good morning), よろしくおねがいします (yoroshiku onegaishimasu, nice to meet you), すみません (sumimasen, excuse me / sorry). He says them to people, and each person reacts in their own way. That reaction is the day-1 bonding.
- Commands (kotodama), four on day 1: 待って (matte, wait), 開けて (akete, open), 動いて (ugoite, work), and one from the lunch partner. Japanese, reading and English always shown together.

## The day

1. **Train (commute).** Eric sits next to Mio, coming back from a night at her mother's on the mainland with a bag of pickles. She sees B2 on his card: he's the support contractor Mori told them about, first day, from Norway, and he speaks no Japanese. She doesn't love strangers (calls him 外人 instead of his name), but she'd rather not watch him embarrass B2, so she teaches him on the ride. おはようございます: he tries it on her, then on the cat (his first use of the Say button). よろしくおねがいします: they did just meet. At the station, すみません gets a woman out of the aisle. Everyone gets off; from the platform Eric and Mio see the sleeping man still alone in the car, and the announcer says the train is going back to the mainland. The doors close in steps; Mio yells 待って and nothing happens; Eric types it and the doors freeze with the kotodama effect. The man stumbles out, the doors shut, the train leaves. Mio says doors don't do that and asks him not to tell anyone. Her phone buzzes (a server is down) and she runs.
2. **Gate.** The guard, Mr. Ishibashi, says おはようございます to people coming in for work. Greeted, he points Eric to the reader. The card is red until nine; the guard points at the clock, then at the bench. At 8:52 the man from the train (Mr. Hamada, Accounts) bursts in and the gate counts his briefcase as a second person. He begs it, 開けて. The guard calls the gate company and gets hold music. Mio texts. Two ways through: say 開けて with Hamada (the gate bursts open; the gate company picks up just then; the guard stares), or すみません and a mime for the guard (lift the case over your head; the guard laughs, Hamada thanks you, the guard waves you through).
3. **Lift.** Two people from Sales talk in Japanese; Eric catches "コンサルタント" and "B2". They see his card and stop.
4. **Office, morning.** Mori meets him at the lift with a formal bow and leads him in; よろしくおねがいします matters to him. Kenji, in broken English, confesses he borrowed Eric's chair, which is now in the machine room with the cat on it. Mio gives him his first repair request: the B2 copier, which Mori opened in 1996. Mori says 動いて to it, as he has for thirty years. Eric says it, and it works. Mori puts a finger to his lips.
5. **Lunch (the one choice).** Eat with Mio in the machine room (English, the only real conversation of the day; she has told the station it was the sensor; she asks him to try 止まって on a rack alarm), or with Mori in the kitchenette (Lillehammer 1994, tea; he teaches 入れて to the thermos pot, which fills all seven cups).
6. **Afternoon.** One gift: a drink from the vending machine, which sticks on the first coin until 動いて. The words echo once (Mori tries 入れて on his cup, Mio tries 止まって on her phone). At 17:40 Emi, the team lead, comes down from head office: she won a parts budget by promising B2 can keep every machine running for ten years, "with the right contractor".
7. **Evening.** Mio names what she's seen, word by word, and asks how he's doing it. Then the station's repair request about the 8:40's doors arrives, and it's assigned to Eric. She tells him to go down to the station tomorrow morning. End.

## People Eric meets

- **Mio** (25): the team's programmer and the only one with English. Dry, low-energy, busy; keeps strangers at a distance and calls Eric 外人. Mori quietly corrects her to 外国の方 (the polite word). A flicker of warmth by the end of the train ride, no more. Likes canned coffee.
- **Mr. Ishibashi** (64): the gate guard. Strict, fair, polite Japanese. Likes a proper おはようございます.
- **Mr. Hamada** (54, Accounts): falls asleep on trains, late through every gate, says すみません constantly.
- **Mr. Mori** (58): a former manager, now on the team. Formal, kind, makes the tea. Opened the copier repair request in 1996. Likes corn soup.
- **Kenji** (21): the team's newest engineer, two months in. Keen to help, easily distracted, a bit sheepish about the chair; tries his school English on Eric. Likes melon soda.
- **Emi** (32): B2's team lead, native English. Spent day one upstairs winning a parts budget by promising more than B2 can do.
- **Tama**: a calico cat who rides the monorail and gets off wherever she likes.

Register: casual inside the team, polite set phrases with the guard and Mori. No em dashes.
