# Day one

The intro day only. Dialogue data: train.js, gate.js, office.js, transitions.js (format in FORMAT.md). Engine needs: REQUESTS.md. Critic scores: REVIEW.md. About 15 to 20 minutes.

## Premise

Eric, a tired Nordic IT engineer, starts a contract to upgrade Amakawa's IT. He's based in the IT support room on B2 with a small team. He barely speaks Japanese. Only Mio on the team speaks some English, and she's busy. The island's old systems, installed in the nineties, answer to spoken commands (kotodama), but only when Eric says them. Nobody knows why, Eric included.

## Language on day 1

- Mio speaks short, plain English. Everyone else speaks Japanese. Lines Eric can't follow are marked `overheard: true`: they show and sound as gibberish, with the words he knows shown clearly. Narration and gestures carry what he needs.
- Greetings (from Mio's lesson on the train): おはようございます (ohayō gozaimasu, good morning), よろしくおねがいします (yoroshiku onegaishimasu, nice to meet you), すみません (sumimasen, excuse me / sorry). He says them to people, and each person reacts in their own way. That reaction is the day-1 bonding.
- Commands (kotodama), four on day 1: 待って (matte, wait), 開けて (akete, open), 動いて (ugoite, work), and one from the lunch partner. Japanese, reading and English always shown together.

## The day

1. **Train (commute).** Eric sits next to Mio, coming back from a night at her family's on the mainland with a bag of her mother's food. She sees his contractor card: same team. She doesn't love strangers: short answers, headphones half on. She calls him 外人 (gaijin, foreigner; blunt) instead of his name, all day. But a contractor who embarrasses himself embarrasses B2, so she gives him a quick lesson on the ride: the three greetings, when to use which, and "if you're lost, say すみません and point". At the station a sleeping man is about to miss his stop; Mio mutters 待って at the closing doors as a joke, Eric repeats it, and the doors wait. Mio: "That doesn't work. It never works."
2. **Gate.** Mio's phone buzzes (a server alarm) and she runs ahead. Eric's card isn't live until nine. The guard, Mr. Ishibashi, only speaks Japanese. Eric uses his greetings. Mr. Hamada, the sleeping man, runs in late and the new gate shuts on his briefcase; he begs it: 開けて. Eric can say it too. Ways through: wait for nine, Hamada signing him in, or 開けて.
3. **Lift.** Two people from Sales talk in Japanese; Eric catches "コンサルタント" and "B2". Nobody explains.
4. **Office, morning.** Mori, the oldest on the team, meets him at the lift with a formal bow; よろしくおねがいします matters to him. Kenji, casual, has borrowed Eric's chair. Mio gives him his first ticket: the B2 copier, which has been eating paper since 1996. Mori says 動いて to it out of politeness, as he always has. Eric says it, and it works.
5. **Lunch (the one choice).** Eat with Mio in the machine room (English, the only real conversation of the day; she teaches 止まって when a rack alarm goes off), or with Mori in the kitchenette (almost no shared language, tea; he teaches 入れて to the thermos pot). Reaching the first bond step plays that scene.
6. **Afternoon.** One gift moment: a drink from the vending machine for someone. Each person answers in their own way.
7. **Evening.** Mio, leaving: "外人. The doors this morning. The copier. How?" End.

## People Eric meets

- **Mio** (25): the team's programmer and the only one with English. Short, dry, busy; keeps strangers at a distance and calls Eric 外人. Mori quietly corrects her to 外国の方 (the polite word). A flicker of warmth by the end of the train ride, no more. Likes canned coffee.
- **Mr. Ishibashi** (64): the gate guard. Strict, fair, polite Japanese. Likes a proper おはようございます.
- **Mr. Hamada** (54, Accounts): asleep on every train, late through every gate, says すみません constantly.
- **Mr. Mori** (58): a former manager, now on the team. Formal, kind, makes the tea. Likes corn soup.
- **Kenji** (29): the team's other engineer. Casual, a bit sheepish about the chair. Likes canned coffee.
- **Tama**: a calico cat who rides the train to work.

Register: casual inside the team, polite set phrases with the guard and Mori. No em dashes.
