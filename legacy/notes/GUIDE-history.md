## Current focus: low-poly Mio (paused 2026-09-27)

Jørgen paused the hand-built low-poly Mio after round 14b. What still holds (the pause, Meshy parts, no shrinking of generated meshes) is in GUIDE.md.

- Flat-shaded characters are built by hand in code (Jørgen, 2026-09-27). Shrinking or recolouring a generated mesh (TRELLIS etc.) is out: "simplifying an inherently broken model is a stupid way to go about it." His target is legacy/side/flat/ref/mio-lowpoly-ref.png: chibi Mio about 2.3 heads tall, faceted hair with a bun and teal-green bits, flat eyes behind square black glasses, teal headphones, lanyard with ID card, dark oversized hoodie, cargo trousers with teal tabs, teal and white sneakers, rigid limbs, run/idle/crouch, shown in an isometric street corner. This replaces "never hand-code characters" for flat-shaded 3D (that rule was about pixel art). Copy the reference as it is unless he says otherwise.
- Low-poly Mio notes (Jørgen, 2026-09-27, on round 5): "she looks emaciated": the face must be round and full, chibi-style. The hair is one continuous shell "like an angular helmet with flaps", with the fringe and side locks as flaps of it, never separate bangs or strands.
- Round 6 verdict (Jørgen, 2026-09-27): "6 at this point, the style is there, but reads like lego, using square blocks instead of fewer, purposeful planes." Flat-shaded models are built from fewer, deliberate planes where the form turns (planar analysis), with tapered prisms and wedges instead of boxes. This applies to the whole figure and to world props.
- Round 9 verdict and new reference (Jørgen, 2026-09-27): round 9 was "a lot worse… triangles facing inward giving her a bunch of dents, totally not usable". He made a simplified reference, legacy/side/flat/ref/mio-simplified-ref.png, which is now the target: two buns, few big flat planes (not perfect squares), and "the lighting and coloration gradients do a lot of the work". Build method from round 10: each part is a convex hull of a few hand-placed key points, so it can't have dents, with a check that every face points outward, a gentle top-to-bottom colour gradient per part, and soft light.
- Round 12 notes (Jørgen, 2026-09-27): don't taper the underside of the chin toward the neck; the bottom of the head is smooth and curves out to the sides. The front bang is one squarish block, never spiky shards. Eyes are drawn as smooth 2D (SVG-like) shapes on the face, not modelled in 3D. The torso narrows at the waist and widens toward the chest and hips. Don't over-think the clothes: the hoodie "is literally just an asymmetric cube", and legs are longer cubes with rounded edges, so use tens of vertices per piece. Only the head and hair get more shape.
- Modular characters (Jørgen, 2026-09-27, round 12): build the base body, hair, clothes and props as separate parts, each matched to its own reference (legacy/side/flat/ref/mio-part-body.png, mio-part-hair.png, mio-part-clothes.png), then assemble. The same system is for the rest of the cast later.
- Round 10 notes (Jørgen, 2026-09-27): the hair is too tall and reads as a very tall head; the back of the head looks cut off under the hair (the skull must fill out behind the ears down to the nape); the body edges are too square (bevel the corners).
- Round 8 notes (Jørgen, 2026-09-27): better, "but not cute chibi yet". No nose or mouth (the cute look is without them). Eyes a little less tall, all dark with a highlight and no white, with short brows above. Glasses less tall, softer colour, and an extra angle where the bridge meets the frame. One larger centre bang instead of many small ones, and hair that isn't lopsided. Body: fewer divisions and triangular cuts rather than square ones (the main difference from the reference), and limbs that taper unevenly top to bottom instead of square blocks. Softer colours overall.

- Difficulty (Jørgen, 2026-09-26, playtest 5, both builds): "both games as they stand are way too hard, they dont ease you into anything, japanese everywhere that you are expected to know, you constantly have to tediously click every single word all the time." Playtest 4 said the same, so this comes before looks and before new places. Rules:
  1. English carries the text. Narration, UI and what people say are in English, and only the words he is learning or knows appear in Japanese inside the English line. Their reading and meaning show right there the first few times, so nothing needs a click to understand.
  2. Start with almost no Japanese and add one new word at a time, where it's used. The first command is one word, shown with its meaning before a gate asks for it. Longer sentences come one piece at a time.
  3. Support fades as a word is learned (meaning first, then reading), and lines slowly show more Japanese, so he sees the shift. That is the sense of mastery.
  4. Voices stay in Japanese. Katakana always shows its reading.
  5. Help gives extra detail only and is never needed to follow the story or pass a gate.
  6. No clicking for the sake of clicking.
  7. A cold-player test at his level (mid-N5, weak katakana) comes before he plays anything.
  8. Easing in still asks for recall (cold-player test 01, 2026-09-27: the eased room printed every answer in romaji, so the player copied instead of remembering and it was too easy). Romaji only the first time a word appears. After that, help comes only when he's stuck, in steps (the chip glows, the voice replays, then the full sentence). Every attempt, even a wrong one, visibly does something.

Keep the setting (the company island, the cast, kotodama, casual speech first, rising in the company). ## Learning rules (VN era)

Replaced where they conflict by "A 3D RPG first, learning second" in docs/game/setting.md; how words show and count is in docs/game/words.md.

- Reverse language learning (Jørgen's idea, 2026-09-25): assume the player knows everything. Text shows as natural Japanese. Tap a word once: the kana reading appears above it. Tap again: the English appears below. The player's level is learned from what they tap during play (no test), and unknown words and kanji are reintroduced gradually later. Soft adaptation: if a player taps readings for nearly everything, start showing readings by default for those kanji. This replaces the up-front level check and simplifies the opening: it only has to teach the tap.
- Word display: above your level = kana only; learning = kanji with a reading above; known = kanji alone. Readings go romaji, then kana, then none. Never show a reading above text that is already kana.
- Listen first: sound and text belong together (karaoke-style follow-along), not a separate listen step and read step. No blur effects.
- Comprehension checks must use a different mode from the input (heard Japanese → answer in English or by acting). Never let the player match kanji shapes.
- A word counts as known only after unaided success on separate days. English is hidden by default; revealing it counts as a lookup.
- Reply options: the option text and the button that selects it are separate (2026-09-25). Tapping the text only gives word help; a distinct select button, spaced away from the text, commits. Nothing gets chosen by accident.
- Every word, including words inside options, can be tapped for its meaning without triggering the option.
- Selecting an option reads it aloud (player voice, at a lower volume). Replays play slightly slower.
- Scroll up or PageUp goes back through the dialogue (VN backlog).
- Three separate progression tracks: vocabulary, grammar and letters (kana and individual kanji). Track each per player. Kanji the player can't read yet are shown as kana automatically, so a known word can still appear in kana while its kanji is unknown.

## Process: VN-era verdicts and ownership

- The main thread coordinates and never blocks on waiting; long work goes to background subagents with clear file ownership: the builder owns legacy/game/; the art agents own art/ and add images to legacy/game/img; the content writer owns content/.
- Playtest 3 verdict (2026-09-25, phone): "Your whole approach and QA is wrong." Characters drawn on top of each other; the phone onboarding was a language dump that didn't read as a phone and explained nothing; no exposition (who am I, where, why); the elevator was all Japanese with no explanation.
- Playtest 4 verdict (2026-09-25, train opening): "interesting", inconsistent, dialogue not too bad. But: tapping on parts of the text is very fiddly; the Japanese is too advanced for him, so he presses Meaning all the time; he feels he can click any option at random every time (choices have no stakes and don't test understanding); not engaging; no feeling of mastery.
- What that means for design: (1) word help needs a bigger, less fiddly target than tapping parts of a line (e.g. tap the whole line to reveal readings, then the words); (2) the default text must sit at his level so Meaning is the exception, not the routine; (3) choices must depend on understanding the line, with visible consequences, or they're just clicking; (4) build a sense of mastery: things that were hard become easy and the game shows it.
