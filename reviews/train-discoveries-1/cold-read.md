# Train encounters: cold read

Claude, 2026-09-29. Read as a first-time player against GUIDE (Writing), docs/game/setting.md, cast.md, places.md, stories/mio-train.md and sleeping-man.md, VOICE.md and the current train.js. Drafts not edited.

## Across all six

1. **The Japanese is blurred in the game.** systems.md (Overheard Japanese) blurs every line Eric hasn't learned, and the current `phone_girl` line is `overheard: true`. Played as it stands, the player sees gibberish plus Eric's English. Aoi's joke, the youth's "first goal", the music player's "I recorded it myself" and the bun woman's "tired of company snacks" all live in the Japanese. draft-notes.md flags this, but it decides whether any of these work. It has to be settled before the pick (English shown for passenger lines, or each moment readable from the staging alone). Judged by staging alone, only the youth (photo with score), Hamada (buzz and tap) and the reader (printout) carry.
2. **Same shape six times.** Each one goes: the passenger shows a thing, Eric says something in English, the passenger replies, and Eric closes on a quip ("I'll try not to make the score any worse.", "I think I'm going to need an older book.", "I'll leave you to it.", "This is very welcome."). If a player clicks four of them in a row, Eric's closing jokes become a tic. Let two or three end on the passenger instead.
3. **Eric's English gets answered.** Mio says on this same ride, "Nobody speaks English. Even at the supermarket." Aoi answers "Is that bad?" with a precise "ううん！窓が好きなだけ。" A reply to a loanword ("Windows 95?" / "うん。会社の。") is fine; a reply to a full English question isn't.
4. **"Yesterday" twice.** The youth's "昨日の試合" and the music player's "昨日、自分で録ったんだ" come back to back. Day 1 is a Thursday. An island company match on Wednesday, then the same man riding back from the mainland on Thursday morning, is a small leap nobody explains. Not a commute problem, but worth one change (e.g. the match was on the mainland).
5. **Commute:** no findings. Nothing says every morning or riding to work. The bun woman's "駅で" is the mainland station, which is fine.
6. **Past day 1:** mostly clean. The youth is the exception (below).

## train-aoi

7 beats, plus the seat nod. Could be 6.

- **Interesting: yes, the best of the set.** It has a real turn (she has just said "not the basement" when the basement guy turns up), and "窓が好きなだけ" is a warm, funny recovery. Its setup is already in the game.
- **Overlap with Mio's reveal** (train.js `sit`): Mio's "Eh... B2? You're going to B2?" is her big surprise. If Aoi goes first, the player has already had "I'm in IT, on B2." / "あっ。……ごめん。", so Mio's reaction becomes the second one. Either accept that as a setup and let Mio's line shift slightly, or keep Eric from saying B2 here (for example he shows his card and she reads it).
- **Order when she's the first passenger:** "She gives a small apologetic bow before returning to her call." comes first, then "the passenger nods toward [the seat]". She has just gone back to her phone, and now she turns to nod. On the game's very first click, the goal ("Sit down") arrives after seven beats. Put the nod inside the bow ("she bows, and nods you toward the free seat") so it reads as one gesture.
- "あ、あなたも新人？": あなた to a stranger reads textbook. "もしかして、新人さん？" is more like her.
- Eric's "I'm in IT, on B2." answers a question he can't understand. It works only if the player reads it as Eric guessing from her face. Fine if the staging shows her looking at his card or lanyard.

## train-bun

5 beats. Could be 4.

- **Interesting: mildly.** It's a kind gesture with no turn. It is nicer than "いい天気ですね", but it's the weakest of the six.
- **Staging:** places.md has her "seen from behind" on the near bench. Opening the bag, offering the cake and opening the other one all need her to turn and face the camera. That's three actions for one click. Cut "She opens the other cake for herself."
- **Overlap with Mio's pickle:** Mio offers a pickle if warmed twice. Two strangers handing Eric food on one short ride blunts Mio's offer, which is a bond reward.
- "I haven't had breakfast. This is very welcome." is written, not spoken. "This is very welcome" is a thank-you card. Something like "Oh, I skipped breakfast. Thanks."
- Eric knows ありがとう (his own `jp_one` choice: "ありがとう, that's about it"). He says "Thank you" in English to someone who doesn't speak it. Using the one word he has would read naturally and needs no lesson.
- "会社のお菓子、もう飽きちゃって。" leans on something the player hasn't seen: what are "company snacks"? With the English shown it reads as a hint that everything here is company-made, which is nice. Without it, it's nothing.

## train-youth

5 beats. Could be 4: "やっと一点取れた" and "初めて決めたんだ" say the same thing.

- **Interesting: yes, on the picture.** Proud of 1–6 is a good small character beat, and the photo carries it without the Japanese.
- **Invents an IT department.** "IT 1–6 ACCOUNTS" plus Eric's "I'm starting in IT today" tells a first-time player this man is Eric's colleague. setting.md and cast.md have one IT: support, the B2 team of five. The notes say "IT outside the already-established B2 team", which the player can't know, and which adds a department the docs don't have. Use another team name (Sales, General Affairs), or drop Eric's IT line.
- **Points past day 1:** "I'll try not to make the score any worse." implies Eric will be playing for the team. That's a future event, which the notes themselves rule out.
- **Staging:** he's "seen from behind" (places.md). Turning round and holding the phone out so the player can read a score is fine as one beat, but the score has to fill the frame.
- An English "IT 1–6 ACCOUNTS" label on a Japanese company match is contrived for Eric's sake. Accept it or make it a scoreboard with numbers only.

## train-music

6 beats. Could be 5.

- **Contradicts cast.md:** `music` is the "Girl with headphones" (cast.md, both label columns). The draft uses "he" and "him" throughout. Pick one; cast.md is canon until changed.
- **Interesting: yes, quietly.** "音、漏れてた？" is the most natural line in the set, a real train moment, and "まだ下手だけど" gives the person a shy edge.
- **Leap:** "Oh, that's you playing." How does Eric know? He can't understand "自分で録った", and a recording on a phone doesn't show who's playing. Show a video of her playing, not an audio file, or cut Eric's line and end on her embarrassment.
- "Only a little." answering "音、漏れてた？" is fine: the lifted headphone and the tinny sound make the question guessable.
- Staging is at the limit: headphone lift, a sound loop, phone out, rewinding, headphone back. Drop the rewind.

## train-reader

6 beats including the kept title line. Could be 5.

- **Interesting: yes.** It's the only one that ties to Eric's job, and "I think I'm going to need an older book." is a fair joke for him.
- **Overlap with Mio:** her `sit` line, "Amakawa never replaces anything, so, um... some of them are older than me", is the reveal. If the player clicks the reader first, Windows 95 has already told them. Keep it a small setup (he looks at the book and sighs at his old printout), or accept that Mio's line lands second.
- **Staging too much for one click:** take the printout out of the book, hold it beside a picture, make a Win95 taskbar readable, then tap the Amakawa name. Four props and gestures, and a taskbar is a detail most players won't read on a phone screen. Cut the tap on the Amakawa name (the rack's luggage tag already does the company name, and so does Mio). Make the printout a big, obviously old grey window.
- "Windows 95?" works: it's a loanword and a picture, so "うん。会社の。" follows.

## train-hamada

4 beats plus the kept sticky-note line. Could be 3.

- **Interesting: the gag is good, the lines aren't.** Silencing a phone without waking up is a funny, readable picture, and it sets up the platform: his alarm won't save him. But he still says nothing, which is exactly Jørgen's complaint. cast.md says he "apologises constantly". A sleep-mumbled "すみません…" as he taps it quiet would be in character and funny, and Mio teaches すみません at the doors later. Hearing it first, blurred, is harmless.
- Eric's two lines ("Excuse me. Was that your alarm?" / "All right. I'll leave you to it.") add nothing, and you don't question a sleeping stranger. Cut both or keep one small aside. With them gone it's two beats and better.
- "Alarm" in Eric's line vs "reminder" in the title and notes. An alarm mid-crossing, before the stop, is odd timing. Say which it is (12F 9:00 reminder, which fits the sticky note).
- Also make sure `asleep` / greeting still gives the zzz, and that this node's nod doesn't read as him being awake enough to be greeted.
