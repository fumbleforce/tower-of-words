Voice sheet

Who Mio is lives in her bio, notes/character-backgrounds/mio.md, and in cast.md. The general dialogue rules are in game3d/story/VOICE.md. This page is how she sounds, for anyone writing a new line for her.

How she talks

Mio is the only person on B2 who speaks English with Eric, and she learned it from games, forums and programming. It comes out loose and a little wrong in the same places every time. She drops an article ("island has no food"), puts "also" at the end ("tomorrow also"), says "like" and "um" while she looks for a word, and gives up on a sentence with "so..." when she stops caring how it ends. Her lines are medium length and run on with "and then" or "but". She almost never makes a neat short sentence unless she is cutting someone off.

Her Japanese is casual and plain. She says 森さん and uses the polite forms she needs with people from upstairs, but she gives a small nod where they bow, and she tells Eric that ohayō without the gozaimasu is fine for her. When she teaches him a word she explains it the way a gamer explains a mechanic: what it does, when to use it, and one joke at his expense.

Her fillers are あー, えっと, "mm" and "okay". "Honestly" and "basically" come from the internet. "Mm." on its own is a full answer.

She never says she is glad, never thanks someone at length, never praises Eric like a teacher, and never explains her own feelings. She does not list three things, and she does not give orders in fragments.

What she wants and avoids

After work she wants to be left alone with her games. At work she wants the old servers to keep running without anyone upstairs noticing her. The thing she cares about most is the tactics game she is making and has shown nobody. She avoids meetings, bowing, people turning up without a message, and anything that would put a fault on her list because someone else reported it.

She deflects with work or with her mother. If a question gets too close, she answers a different, safer one ("Logs. You want to read them?") or brings up the pickles. If someone keeps pushing, she puts her headphones back on.

She opens up about games. A specific question about one she likes, such as why she lost or how a system works, gets three times the words she gives anything else, and she forgets to be tired. She also warms to people who leave her alone first. Sitting near her without asking anything does more than any question.

Under stress

Her sentences stop halfway ("No, no, not now..."). She answers without looking up and keeps typing. When she is embarrassed she ends the subject with "...Anyway" and a job for the other person ("Anyway, go."). When something breaks, she takes it on herself first and says it is on her list. When she is interrupted at a bad moment she can be rude ("What.") and does not always apologise later.

Eric at the start and later

On day 1 he is the new guy. She calls him 外人 (gaijin) without meaning anything by it, teaches him the greetings because B2 would look bad if he couldn't say them, and leaves him on the platform the moment her phone buzzes. She doesn't use his name until he has eaten lunch with her.

Later she uses his name, sends him texts first (lower case, no full stops), and leaves a space for him at the rack. She asks him to try a command because she wants to see it work, and she keeps his secret because it would go on her list otherwise. She still leaves his messages unanswered for a day or two and is surprised when he minds. If he turns up at her door without a message, she opens it halfway.

Example lines

B2, someone from upstairs stands at her desk while she reads a log.

"Mm, one second... okay, what is it. Is it the copier? It's always the copier."

Eric knocks on her dorm door in the evening without messaging first.

"Ah... you could message, you know. I'm not really dressed for people. What do you need?"

Lunch in the machine room, Eric asks about the old game on her second monitor.

"It's from like 1994. You draw the map yourself, and if you draw it wrong you just die in a corridor. Honestly it's really good, nobody makes them like that anymore."

The morning after a late night.

"I didn't sleep. I lost to a guy who only builds archers, which shouldn't work, so I played him again. Four times."

Eric mentions a message she left unanswered for two days.

"Oh. I saw it. I was going to answer, I just... okay, I'm answering now, so."

In the lift, a manager bows to her deeply.

"あ、どうも。" Then, to Eric: "I'm not doing the whole bow, it's eight in the morning."

Testing a command with Eric.

"Can you say it again, but quieter? I want to know if it's the volume or, um, you."

Turning down drinks.

"I said I'd help a friend with the last boss on Thursday. It's Thursday, so... I'm not coming."

Lines she would not say

"I just really need my alone time, you know?"

She never names her own need. She shows it by closing the laptop or putting her headphones on.

"Servers. Logs. Coffee. That's my whole morning."

This is the row of fragments VOICE.md bans. Mio would say "I have to check the servers and I haven't even had coffee yet."

"Great job! See, you're getting the hang of it."

She doesn't cheer or teach like a guide. A good attempt from Eric gets "Mm, okay, not bad."

Drift to watch

Her day 1 lines on the train and in the office are the reference Jørgen liked. Some later lines (days 2 to 5 and the shared Chat menu) lost her slips and became polite and warm, for example "That was a good guess. Just ask if you're not sure, okay?" Keep the dropped articles, the trailing "so..." and the dryness on every day.

Voice sheet

Who he is lives in his bio and cast.md. This page is how to write him. Jørgen's direction: "kenji is a mild confused lamb, somewhat clumsy." He is funny because of what happens to him, never because he is slow. He follows fine once someone explains properly, but he rarely asks.

How he talks

Kenji speaks softly and a little too quickly when he wants to be helpful, then slows down when he realises he has lost the thread. He builds a sentence one piece at a time, adds the detail he has just remembered, and sometimes never reaches the end. He is mild. He sounds pleased rather than excited, and an exclamation mark should be rare. Most of the current lines ("Sit, sit!", "Welcome food!") are brighter than he is now.

In Japanese he is casual with Eric and anyone his age, and uses 僕, never 俺. With Mori and Emi he switches to careful です and ます, which comes out stiff, and he sometimes slips back into casual halfway through and corrects himself. His fillers are えっと and あれ？. When he is trying to remember something he says it out loud with っけ (何階だっけ). When a senior gives him an instruction he says はい, はい before they have finished.

His English is what he kept from school. He uses the present tense for everything ("Yesterday I go"), drops "is" and articles, and builds the sentence noun by noun ("Paper. For the printer upstairs. Third floor, I think."). When a word is missing he goes back to the noun he has and points at the thing. He likes practising on Eric and ends a sentence with "right?" to check it landed. Keep his broken English loose and running on. A row of single nouns as a punchline ("Machine room. Chair. Cat.") is the clipped rhythm the guide bans, even when a learner says it.

He apologises quickly and quietly: ごめん to Eric, すみません to seniors, "sorry" in English, and usually only once, because he is already trying to fix whatever it was.

What he wants and avoids

He wants to be sent out on a job alone and trusted with it, and one day to own a proper workbench. He wants people to keep asking him to help.

He avoids asking the same question twice, telling anyone a job went wrong, and saying no to anyone who sounds disappointed. He gets lost when several people talk at once.

Things that steady him are one instruction at a time, something written down, someone showing him with their hands, and a melon soda in his hand. The old space programmes he builds models from steady him too. On that subject his sentences get longer and surer, because he knows he isn't wrong.

His tells under stress

  • He says 大丈夫 or "it's okay" too fast, before anyone asked. Eric learns 大丈夫 on day 2, so the player can start to hear this.
  • He repeats the last word someone said, as if holding on to it.
  • He agrees with whoever spoke last.
  • His hands get busy. He straightens his tie, which still ends up crooked, or tightens his grip on whatever he is carrying.
  • He moves too quickly in a tight space and catches a chair or a doorframe.

Eric, at first and later

At first Eric is a relief. He is the first person on B2 who is newer than Kenji, and the first who understands less Japanese than he does. Kenji plays the senior gently and offers what he is sure of, like where the good tape is or which vending machine has melon soda. For the same reason he hides his mistakes from Eric more than from anyone, because Eric is the one person he can look capable in front of.

Later he tells Eric first when something has gone wrong, and asks him to come along while he asks again. Further on he can say no to Eric, kindly and with a reason, and he starts to notice when Eric is nodding along without understanding, because he knows the look.

Example lines

B2, day 1. Mori has just given him a long instruction in polite Japanese.

「あ、はい、はい。…はい。」 Then, quietly, to Eric: "He says something with paper. I go and look."

Day 2, at his desk. Eric asks whether the floor-4 printer job is done.

"Yes, it's okay, it prints now. It prints one page and then it stops, but... one page is printing. So."

At the vending machine, can in hand.

"My mother calls every Sunday. The first question is always shirts, did you wash. I say yes. Then I wash them, so after, it's true."

In the dorm commons, a half-built model ship on the table.

"This one is from the second series. In the second series the ship has four engines, not three, so I wait before I glue this part. Many people make it with three. It's wrong."

Two clubs have both asked him for six o'clock.

「大丈夫です、大丈夫です。両方できます。」 To Eric, after: "Both say six. That is... the same six, right?"

Later, standing by Eric's desk with a cable in his hand.

"I said it was finished. It is not finished. Can you look before I tell Mio-san?"

Later still, when Eric asks him to come along to something.

"Sorry, today I can't. I said yes to Mori-san first, and this time I want to finish one thing properly."

Lines he would not say

"Mistakes are just lessons, right?"

He has no distance from his mistakes yet. This is a fortune-cookie line, and it lets him off a hook he is still on.

"I'm so stupid, I always ruin everything!"

He is embarrassed and says sorry once. Loud self-pity is the over-the-top anime reaction Jørgen dislikes, and it turns him into a joke.

"I'm afraid I misunderstood what Mori-san wanted, so I'll check with him."

The English is far past his level, and checking with Mori right away is exactly what he can't make himself do.

Voice sheet

How Kuro talks and behaves in a scene. Who she is (38, she/her, head office receptionist) is in cast.md and her life is in her bio. The general dialogue rules are in VOICE.md. This sheet is a proposal and nothing in it is in the game yet.

How she talks

Kuro speaks slowly and keeps her sentences short. She leaves a pause after a question and holds eye contact until the other person answers. She uses few fillers. Where Mio says えっと and trails off, Kuro waits and lets the silence push for her.

Her Japanese stays in polite です/ます form at the counter, and she uses that politeness as pressure. Her requests come as ～てください or ～ましょう with the decision already made: 空けておいてください, お店は私が予約しますから. She asks questions she already knows the answer to, ending in ～ですよね or ～んですか. Off duty, with someone she has decided she wants, she drops to plain form and leaves the さん off his name.

Her English is the English of a hotel front desk: "Please wait here", "Two people?", "Six o'clock, yes?". She has nothing beyond that, so she bends those phrases to her own use. An invitation sounds like a booking she is confirming, and a compliment sounds like a guest check. The short English makes her bossier, because she has no softeners to reach for.

She asks personal questions early and watches what they do to his face: whether anyone is waiting for him in Norway, or why he works late. She remembers names, faces and clothes, and she brings up yesterday's shirt. If he flusters she asks a second question. If he answers plainly she moves on to something more personal.

What she wants and avoids

She wants to be wanted and to choose what happens next: the restaurant, the time, the second drink. She wants her clothes noticed, and she asks if he has noticed when he hasn't said anything. She avoids talk about money and who pays, and she avoids asking for permission. She never says she is lonely or that she likes someone. She says what they will do on Friday instead.

What knocks her off balance

Three things throw her. One is a direct question back ("And you? Is anyone waiting for you?"). Another is a plain, specific compliment on her suit with no flirting in it. The third is someone who says no calmly and leaves. She goes quiet for a moment, answers in her reception voice as if he were a visitor, and then takes back control by setting a time or a place.

Under stress

After she sends an invitation she checks her phone and puts it face down on the counter, then turns it over again. If there is no reply she may send something cruel late at night. The next morning she treats him with full visitor politeness, as if they had never spoken. When she is jealous she asks who he was with and pretends she only knows because reception sees everything. Anyone who snaps their fingers at her gets made to wait while she finishes writing.

Eric at the start and later

At the start Eric is a new face at her counter, and she uses the counter on him. She makes him say good morning first, remembers his name the next day, and books him for the pool the way she would book a guest. She is testing whether he flusters or pushes back.

Later, if he pushes back, she gets more interested and more possessive. She chooses the izakaya, orders his second drink before he finishes the first, and minds who he walks in with. If he only ever agrees, she gets bored and lets him wait at the counter like anyone else. Her rewritten bond steps are still open under #357.

Lines she would not say

  • 「あらあら、また迷子ですか？」 (Ara ara, lost again?) This is the stock teasing older woman from anime. Kuro doesn't play a type and doesn't make Eric small or cute, because she wants someone who can stand up to her.
  • "Only if you want to, of course. No pressure!" She never leaves the decision open. She states the plan and watches whether he argues.
  • "Some doors only open for people who knock twice." She talks about Friday night and the restaurant, never about life in general.

Voice sheet

Who Rei is lives in her bio and cast.md: forty-one, Sales, grew up on Amakawa, plays club tennis by the sports grounds. This page is how she talks. The general rules are in VOICE.md.

How she talks

Rei speaks in whole sentences, usually two at a time: the first says what she has decided and the second tells you what to do about it. She is brusque because she leaves out the cushioning. Her sentences are still complete. "You can stand by the line and tell me if these go in" is her. "Line. Watch it." is not.

She often asks a question and answers it before you can ("Can you see the T from there? You can, you're standing on it."). Once she thinks she knows where your sentence is going, she finishes it for you, and sometimes she gets it wrong and carries on anyway. She counts everything out loud: serves in, days until an order ships, how many times you've asked. Her lines get long only after a loss, when she walks through the whole match to show whose fault it was.

Japanese. Polite です/ます with strangers, customers and seniors, but without the softeners: 〜てください where others would say 〜ていただけますか, and no ちょっと or もしよければ in front. With her Sales juniors she switches to plain 〜て and 〜といて. With the classmates she grew up with she is casual and uses their old nicknames, even in a meeting. On court she talks to herself in Japanese, counting and saying もう一度 before the next serve.

English. Conversational and practical, learned on overseas customers. Short complete sentences joined with "and" and "so", no idioms. When she loses a word she uses a plainer one or shows you. Her tennis words (the T, the alley, the deuce side) run ahead of Eric, and she assumes he knows them. She phrases her orders as "You can...", so they sound like offers. When someone makes a joke she doesn't follow, she goes flat and steers back to the subject.

Orders and criticism. She says what went wrong and what to do next in the same breath, with the facts: "You were on the middle line again. I said take the alley." She doesn't ask whether you're willing to help. She assumes it and starts explaining the job.

She never says "would you mind", "if it's no trouble", "sorry, take your time", or "I was wrong" while the thing is still going on. She never uses Mio's internet fillers ("honestly", "basically", "like"), sales patter, or anything mysterious.

Wants and avoids

She wants the management job, a doubles partner who is good enough to keep up, and her customers left in her hands. She avoids holidays, stopping a match for her right ankle, admitting a plan was wrong in front of the people she made follow it, and anyone who interrupts the last chapters of a crime novel.

Under stress

  • When her ankle hurts, she says 大丈夫です before anyone asks and moves her weight to her left foot. She walks to the ball basket slowly and pretends to be choosing balls.
  • When her plan is failing, she gives her reasons for keeping it and gets more detailed about what everyone else should do differently.
  • When she loses, she spends the evening explaining the match point by point and names whoever was at fault.
  • When she is out of her depth in English, her sentences get shorter and she starts pointing.

Eric, at the start and later

At first Eric is the new foreigner on B2 and a pair of hands. She uses him without asking, switches to English because she won't wait for his Japanese, and brushes off anything he learned in Norway. His dry jokes fall flat with her and she says so.

Later she tells him why as well as what. She trusts him with something she would normally keep, such as setting up the laptop for a customer visit or calling the lines on match point. She may give him a nickname, which is how she treats the people she counts as hers. She still argues when he expects an equal say on court. If she admits a mistake, it is to him alone, days later, and in passing.

Example lines

  1. Sales floor. A junior hesitates before calling a customer back. 「考えてる時間があるなら、先に電話して。断られたら、そのとき考えればいいでしょ。」 (If you have time to think, call first. If they say no, you can think then.)
  2. A meeting. An old classmate, now in Logistics, presents the delivery numbers. 「タコちゃん、その数字、先週のと違うよね。」 (Tako-chan, those numbers are different from last week's, aren't they?) He hates the nickname.
  3. Eric suggests a fix from his old job. "That probably worked in Norway. This customer has been ordering the same valves since 1998, and he wants the same person to call him about them."
  4. An overseas customer makes a joke on a video call. "I don't understand the joke. Can we go back to the delivery date?"
  5. Club doubles, Eric drifts to the middle. "You were standing on the middle line again. I told you to take the alley, so take the alley. If they hit it down the middle, that one's mine."
  6. Her partner wants to stop because she's limping. 「大丈夫です。」 Then, to Eric, "It always does that. We're one game from the set, so you can serve."
  7. Her apartment, Eric knocks while she's reading. "I've got eleven pages left and I already know who did it. Can it wait eleven pages?"
  8. Weeks later, on the sea terrace. "The new toss was a mistake. I kept it for two weeks because I'd told the whole club about it. Don't repeat that on Sunday."

Lines she would not say

  • "Tennis is a war. Pick a side." She explains things practically. This is the clipped, quotable line the game bans, and she doesn't think in slogans.
  • "Sorry, sorry, I always rush people! Take your time." She doesn't apologise for her pace. If she slows down, she just does it and keeps talking.
  • "You were right all along, my plan was wrong." She doesn't concede while the match is going. The furthest she goes in the moment is "Fine, we'll try yours," and she will mention the score if it fails.
