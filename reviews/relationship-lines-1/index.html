Mio, Kuro and Rei, step by step

These are plans for Mio's, Kuro's and Rei's relationship lines, steps 1 to 5, written from their approved voice sheets and bios. Each step says what earns it in play, the public scene that marks it, how she treats Eric from then on, and its Japanese focus. Each voice agent read its character's plan and its changes are worked in.

Nothing here is built yet. Mio's step-2 and step-3 lunch scenes exist and stay. The built step-2 and step-3 scenes for Kuro and Rei were written for their earlier personalities, so the plans rewrite or replace them.

Following your rule, kisses, dates and sex happen only on these lines, gated on her step. Each plan has one friendship-or-romance choice, and the answer is saved in a new flag (romance_mio, romance_kuro, romance_rei) that only that choice sets. The private scenes check the step and that flag. They are written separately and only named here.

After your answer, the public step scenes get written and built in step order, and the private scenes get connected to the steps and the new flags.

Steps 1 to 5

Mio wants to be left alone with her games and to keep her servers off other people's lists. Eric gets close to her by leaving her alone first, asking before he touches her racks, sending a message instead of turning up, and asking real questions about the games she plays. Her line moves from the machine room to her own room, and the last thing she lets him see is the tactics game she has shown nobody.

Step 1: the new guy

Earned on day 1 on the train, when they introduce themselves. Catching her lunch bag and eating lunch with her in the machine room both count, and she remembers them. She calls him 外人 (gaijin) until he has eaten lunch with her once. After that she uses his name.

Step 2: a space at the rack

She reaches it at 6 points. Points come from greeting her in casual Japanese (she said ohayō without the gozaimasu is fine), coffee from the machine, asking before he opens a rack, and staying quiet while she works. The built lunch scene marks it, on weekdays from day 5 in the machine room. She moves a cable so he can sit beside the rack and lets him stay while she eats. She still answers his questions with work, and she still leaves his messages for a day.

Step 3: she goes out for lunch

At 14 points, the built scene plays at weekday lunch. She is checking a front vent before she eats. Eric offers to check it or tells her to go and eat, and she hands him her checklist for that one row. She comes back and says she actually got outside, "like before lunch was over. That never happens." Afterwards she eats at the east-coast bench on weekdays, and she starts texting him first, in lower case with no full stops.

Step 4: the last boss

At 24 points, on a Saturday or Sunday evening, she texts him first: "theres a boss i lost to all week / need someone who doesnt talk the whole time / you free saturday". If he replies, he can come to her dorm room. If he turns up without replying, she opens the door halfway ("Ah... I texted you, you know. You could just answer. I'm not really dressed for people, so... next weekend, okay?") and the scene waits for next weekend.

In her room she gives him the folding chair and a spare controller, and she explains the boss the way she explains a word: what it does, when it does it, and one joke at his expense. Her work phone buzzes in the middle of the fight. It is a routine alarm on a server that is still running. She looks at it, puts it face down and says it can go on tomorrow's list. It is the first time Eric has seen her leave a working machine alone.

They win, or they lose and she wants one more try. Then comes the choice. Eric can say he will come back next weekend for the next boss, which keeps it a friendship. Or he can tell her he didn't come for the boss. She goes quiet, then answers in one loose run-on sentence that makes it clear she means yes: "Mm. ...Okay. Then next Saturday also, and, um, we don't have to play anything, so..." On the friendship answer she sends him home before midnight, because she has a match online. On the romance answer she doesn't mention the match.

The Japanese focus is game talk she says out loud while playing: 右, 左, まだ, もう一回 and 次. The player hears each one with the action on screen and types もう一回 when they want the retry.

The answer is saved in a new flag, romance_mio, which only this choice sets.

Private: on the romance answer, the private scene for step 4 opens (her first time). It is written separately and is gated on step 4 and that answer.

Step 5: the game nobody has seen

Step 4 and this scene. On a weekend evening after step 4, she texts him: "come over later / not talking about work tho". With the game she plays closed, there is a second window she has always minimised when someone came in. This time she opens it herself. It is her tactics game: the combat runs, the art is grey boxes. She asks him to play the first map and say honestly what is wrong with it, and she argues with every note he gives. Then she writes them down.

On the friendship path, he becomes the one person who plays her builds, and she sends him a new one every few weeks with no message attached. On the romance path, the same scene ends with her bringing her mother's pickles out of the fridge, the thing she always complains about, and offering them: "My mother sent too much again, like always. Eat some, okay? ...I'm not doing my match tonight, so you can stay." When she says yes to something that matters, she does it by giving up a game. Friendship players get one more chance here to say he would like more than that, with the same clear answer as step 4, and that answer sets romance_mio too.

The Japanese focus is the game's own menu, in katakana: スタート, セーブ, ユニット, ターン. Her menu is the first place where the player reads katakana for something they want to do.

Private: on the romance path, the private scene for step 5 hangs here (the second time).

After step 5

She still leaves his messages for a day or two and is surprised when he minds. She still guards her racks. She brings enough food for two on the days her mother has sent too much.

Her line runs from the reception counter to the pool, the izakaya and her own dining table, and every invitation on it comes from her.

Step 1: one more foreigner

Earned on day 3, when she gives him her name. Before that she is the receptionist he passes each morning. On day 2 an English greeting gets "Good evening." and nothing else, and a Japanese one gets her brushing the dust off his sleeve and teaching him あなた. She remembers which of the two he chose.

Step 2: she chooses his seat

She reaches it at 6 points. Points come from greeting her in polite Japanese every working morning, using the words she taught him back to her, saying something about her suit, giving his own opinion when she asks for it (she asks and takes his answer, as with the label printer), and turning up at Saturday swimming. Walking past the counter without a word costs nothing, but she says so the next morning ("今日は言ってくれないんですか").

The scene is the built Saturday one at the gym after swimming, rewritten. She no longer asks which seat he wants. She pats the one beside hers by the window and tells him to sit there. Then she asks him a personal question, 「ノルウェーに、待ってる人、いるんですか。」, and watches his face. If he answers plainly, her next question is more personal. If he gets flustered, she asks a second one. From now on she touches his arm when she talks to him at work.

Step 3: six o'clock at the pool

At 14 points, and once Eric knows 私, あなた, 六時 and 二人, the counter scene from her sample plays on a working evening. She points at the clock, says 明日、六時, writes 18:00 プール on a note and says 二人, 私とあなた. Eric can agree to six or point at the seven on the clock because he works late. If he points at seven, she argues once (「七時？ ……六時のほうが、すいてますよ。」), then crosses out the time and writes his. She likes him more for it.

The scene ends at the pool the next evening, where she sets the pace and swims beside him. It is not a date yet. This replaces the built equipment scene, which was written for the earlier Kuro who wanted to stop being treated as staff.

The Japanese focus is the time and people the booking needs: 六時, 二人 and 大丈夫, all heard, typed and used in one exchange. 七時 shows clear because he points at the clock as she says it.

Step 4: Friday night

At 24 points, at the counter on a working morning, she tells him: 「金曜の夜、空けておいてください。お店は私が予約しますから。」 This is the friendship-or-romance choice, and it is hers to offer and his to answer.

He can accept, and she says she will tell him the time later. He can ask for Saturday instead, and she argues once (「土曜？ ……金曜のほうがいいんですけど。」) and then takes it (「じゃあ、土曜。私が予約し直します。」). Either of those is a date. Or he can say no calmly and go to work. That throws her. She answers in her reception voice as if he were a visitor (「かしこまりました。いってらっしゃいませ。」), and the next morning she takes control back by setting something else: 「土曜日、泳ぎましょう。そのあと一杯。お店は私が決めます。」 That keeps the line going as a friendship.

On the date path the public scene is the dinner at the izakaya on the shop street. She orders his second drink before he has finished the first, and asks what he noticed about her that evening. Near the end she slips into plain form once, still with the さん on his name. She doesn't say she likes him. She tells him what they are doing next Friday: 「来週の金曜も、ここ。……空けといて。」

The Japanese focus is ordering: もう一杯, 同じの and すみません, which she says for both of them until he says one himself.

Private: on the date answer, the private scenes for step 4 open: the booking at the counter and the date that follows it. Both are written separately and gated on step 4 and that answer.

Step 5: her table

Step 4 and this scene. On a Friday or Saturday evening she invites him to her apartment on the east side. Clothes cover every surface in the bedroom behind a half-open door, but the dining table is clear, with takeaway on proper plates and a bottle of wine she chose. She has an old romantic drama on and complains about what the heroine decides. Eric can agree with her or tell her the heroine was right. She likes it better when he disagrees, and she says so by refilling his glass.

On the date path she leaves the さん off his name for good when they are alone, and she tells him about the seafront apartment she wants one day, the first time she has told anyone from work. She talks about the place and never the saving: 「いつか、海の見える部屋に住むの。ベランダで、こうやって食べたいな。」 On the friendship path she invites him the same way, as her one guest who argues back about the drama, and she still decides what they eat. With him she keeps です/ます and his さん, and she goes plain only when she complains at the screen (「なんでそっち選ぶの。」).

The Japanese focus is plain-form Japanese. On the date path she switches from です/ます to plain form with him. On either path the player hears the same few things said both ways.

After step 5

At work she stays polite and touches his arm. If he doesn't answer a message she may send something cruel late at night and treat him like a visitor the next morning. If he comes in laughing with someone, she asks who it was.

Rei wants a doubles partner who does what she says and is good enough to win, and somebody around to boss. She never admits a weakness or a mistake. Eric gets close to her by turning up on Sunday, standing where she tells him, calling her lines honestly, and not mentioning her ankle. She softens slowly, and it shows as attention, never as an apology. Her line runs from the tennis court to the Sales floor and ends at her own dining table.

Step 1: the new foreigner from B2

Earned on day 4 at the tennis club, when she introduces herself and tells him and Aoi to take a spare racket each. She switches to English because she won't wait for his Japanese. She remembers whether he watched her serve or hit with Aoi.

Step 2: she calls on him first

She reaches it at 6 points. Points come from Sunday tennis, doing a drill her way, polite Japanese, and repair requests from her team's machines. Joking with her earns nothing, because she doesn't follow the joke.

The scene is the built Sunday one, rewritten. She no longer offers to keep the explanation shorter. She tells him to serve again and throw the ball higher, counts his serves out loud, and tells him which of them were in. From now on she calls on him before anyone else at the court.

Private: her terrace scene hangs on this step. It has no romance in it and is written separately.

Step 3: the drill

At 14 points, the built doubles drill plays at Sunday tennis, rewritten. She puts him on the deuce side, then crosses into his half and takes a ball that was his. Eric can call "mine" on the next ball and stand his ground, or let her take it. If he stands his ground, she makes it sound like her plan all along: "Then it's yours. That's why I put you in the alley. Hit it properly." She doesn't say she was wrong. The next Sunday she runs the drill with his half left alone and doesn't mention it. If he let her take it, she wins the point and tells him he was standing in the wrong place. Both complete the step. She remembers which one he did.

This replaces the built version, where she says "I got there before I thought about it". That line has her admit a mistake, and she never does.

The Japanese focus is court talk: the line calls イン and アウト, which the player reads in katakana, and もう一度, which she says to herself before a serve and the player types to ask for the ball again.

Step 4: match point

At 24 points, at a Sunday club match against her old classmate's pair. She tells Eric to call the lines from the bench, which she trusts nobody else with. Late in the set her right ankle goes. She says 大丈夫です before anyone asks and moves her weight onto her left foot.

Then her own shot lands close to the opponents' baseline. Eric can call it out, which is honest and costs her the game, or let it go. Either way she plays on and finishes the set. After the match she goes through it point by point. She never praises him. If he called it, she states it as a fact (「あそこはアウト。ボール一個分。」) and moves straight on to her partner's mistakes, and the next Sunday she puts him back on the bench to call lines. If he let it go, she tells him "That was out. Next time, call it." Calling it honestly is what she wanted from him. Then she gives Eric a nickname, which is how she marks him as one of hers (the nickname is chosen when the scene is written), and tells him to come to Sales on Tuesday to set up the laptop for her biggest customer visit.

The Japanese focus is her count of games. Points in Japanese tennis are called in katakana English (フィフティーン, サーティ, デュース), but she keeps the game count out loud in Japanese numbers (ゴー・ヨン), and the player follows the set by it.

Step 5: a second corner of the table

Step 4 and this scene. After a Sunday match she tells him to come to her place by the sports grounds and fix her home printer. Her dining table is covered in customer files, with one corner cleared for her plate and a crime novel open near the end. Eric can fix the printer quietly or talk while she reads. If he talks, she tells him to wait until she has finished the chapter. Then she clears a second corner of the table for him without saying anything, and puts two plates on it.

This is where the friendship-or-romance choice falls. She tells him to keep Saturday free (「土曜、空けといて。」) and says she will tell him where they are going. Eric can answer as her regular partner, for tennis and errands, or ask her plainly whether that is a date. She doesn't look up from the plates, and she is short with him for asking: 「デートに決まってるでしょ。七時に寮の前で待ってて。遅れないでよ。どこに行くかは、そのとき言うから。」 Either answer completes the step. The answer is saved in a new flag, romance_rei, which only this choice sets. On the date path she still decides where they go and still gives the orders, and the warmth shows in what she tells him about her work. On the friendship path she keeps him as her doubles partner and the one she sends on errands, and she tells him why as well as what, now and then.

The Japanese focus is plain Japanese orders: 空けといて, 待ってて and 遅れないで, the forms she has used on her team all along and now uses on him.

No private scene hangs on steps 3 to 5 yet.

After step 5

She still never admits a mistake. When she has got something wrong, she changes it the next week and is shorter with him if he brings it up. She still gets angry when he expects an equal say, and she still calls on him first.
