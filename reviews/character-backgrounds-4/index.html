Mio

Mio is twenty-five and spends most of her free time playing games. She likes being alone and can happily go a whole weekend without speaking to anyone. She dislikes people turning up unannounced and prefers them to send a message, even if she takes a while to reply. At work, she gets annoyed when people interrupt her and is sometimes rude to them.

Mio has pale skin, light tan eyes and dark green hair with brighter green underneath. She keeps her hoodie on even in the office and often looks tired. She sometimes stays up playing until three, especially after losing a game she thinks she should have won.

Growing up on the mainland, Mio shared a small bedroom with her younger sister. Their mother expected the door to stay open. Mio was glad to have a room of her own when she moved to the island, and to be left alone after work. Her mother still sends food home with her after visits. Mio complains about carrying it, then eats it for the rest of the week.

In her room on the island, two monitors take up most of the desk beside her narrow bed. She keeps a folding chair for visitors, though she rarely has anyone over. Most evenings she plays turn-based strategy games or old dungeon crawlers, often with the sound off. Online, she uses a name nobody at work knows. She is much more talkative with other players and frequently argues with them.

Mio learned English through games, forums and her programming work on B2. She prefers maintaining the old servers to dealing with visitors upstairs and would dislike a promotion that put her in meetings. She wants to release a small tactics game and has already programmed the combat, though she keeps changing it. The artwork is unfinished, and she has not shown the game to anyone. If she has a visitor while she is working on it, she closes it before they can see.

Mio has a few friends she enjoys playing with and is willing to spend an evening helping them through a difficult game. She still wants plenty of time to herself. When a friend needs more attention than she wants to give, she sometimes leaves their message unanswered for days. She expects them to be pleased to hear from her when she finally replies and is surprised if they are still upset.

Kenji

Kenji is twenty-one and started his first job on B2 two months ago. He is eager to help and often agrees to do something before he understands what he has been asked. When he realises he has misunderstood, he is embarrassed to ask again. Sometimes he guesses what he should do and ends up taking the wrong equipment to the wrong floor.

Kenji has an open smile, untidy black hair and a tie he rarely gets straight on the first try. He is pudgy and rather clumsy, often bumping into chairs as he squeezes past them. He drinks melon soda throughout the day and has been banned from the machine room since he spilled some there.

Before moving to the island, Kenji lived with his parents above their small bicycle shop on the mainland and helped out in the shop. His father was patient when he needed a repair explained several times, but customers sometimes became irritated with him. He preferred working in the back, where he could finish one thing before someone asked for another. He took a vocational computing course before applying for the island job. Leaving home was his idea, although his mother still phones to ask whether he has washed his shirts.

Kenji spends much of his time in his dorm room building plastic spacecraft from old science-fiction television programmes. There are several unfinished models among the carefully sorted parts, and a pile of laundry beside the bed. He takes his time over the models and finds the printed instructions easier to follow than several people talking at once. He also knows the programmes well enough to talk about them without worrying that he has got something wrong.

Kenji wants to become an engineer who can be sent out on a job alone. His first large purchase would be a proper workbench for his room. For now, he is so worried about making mistakes that he sometimes says everything is fine when a job has gone wrong. Colleagues have had to stay late finishing jobs he said were done. He is quick to apologise but worries that they will stop trusting him to do the work.

Kenji is grateful to anyone who takes the time to explain things without making him feel stupid. He listens patiently and tries to be just as patient when someone needs his help. He finds it difficult to refuse a favour, especially when the other person sounds disappointed in him.

Kuro

Kuro is thirty-eight and flirts openly with people she finds attractive. She speaks in a low, unhurried voice and enjoys asking personal questions, especially when she can see that she is making someone flustered. She has no difficulty asking for a date and usually assumes she will choose the restaurant. Once there, she may order another drink for her guest without asking whether they want one.

Kuro wears her black hair in a high bun, with a blunt fringe and long locks beside her face. She has dark eyes and wears black trouser suits that she chooses carefully for their fit. She likes it when someone notices the effort she has made with her clothes. At reception, she remembers visitors' names and faces and pays attention to their moods. Her limited English makes it difficult to say much to visitors from abroad.

Kuro spent most of her twenties at a mainland hotel's reception desk. Shared flats and changing shifts made it difficult to have anyone over. She accepted the job on the island because it came with her own place and regular hours. Although she had little interest in the company at first, she enjoyed knowing who was arriving and who was being kept waiting. If a visitor snaps their fingers at her, she sometimes makes them wait even when she could help them straight away.

Kuro lives in a small staff apartment overlooking the east side of the island. Her bedroom is crowded with clothes, but she keeps the dining table clear so she can have people over. She finds cooking boring, so she prefers to order takeaway and serve it on proper plates with a bottle of wine she has chosen. She swims regularly, even when she has nobody to go with, and likes a long bath afterwards. At home she watches old romantic dramas and complains about the decisions the heroines make.

Kuro wants one of the island's expensive seafront apartments by the time she is forty-five, with a balcony large enough for dinner guests. She is saving for it and would be pleased to have a wealthy partner, but would resent being told what to do because they paid for things. She used to live with a boyfriend, but they separated after repeatedly arguing about money. She still describes him as stingy and usually leaves out how often she hid purchases from him.

Kuro checks her phone repeatedly after sending an invitation. If no answer comes, she may send something cruel and regret it in the morning. She gets jealous even when she has promised nothing herself. She is attracted to people who stand up to her, but she still wants to decide what they do together.

Rei

Rei is forty-one and works in Sales. When people are unsure what to do, she usually starts telling them without waiting to be asked. She is impatient with anyone who takes too long to answer, and she argues openly with colleagues who question her instructions.

Rei has steel-grey eyes and wears her silver-grey hair in a ponytail. She dresses in a carefully fitted light suit and sits upright even when she is resting. She plays tennis with much less restraint, serving hard and complaining loudly when a match goes badly. Her right ankle still aches after long matches because of an old injury. Sometimes she tries to hide her limp so her partner will not suggest stopping.

Rei grew up on Amakawa, where her mother worked in payroll and her father maintained the lifts. After school, she joined the company in order processing and worked her way into Sales. Several former classmates still work nearby. She knows their old nicknames and sometimes uses one in a meeting, even when it embarrasses them.

Before meeting a customer, Rei reads their previous orders and prepares for the objections they usually raise. She speaks conversational English with overseas clients but can be unfriendly when they make jokes she does not understand. She wants a management job and resents having to compete for one with people who have only recently joined the company. She has worked nowhere else and often dismisses what they learned in other jobs. She also finds it difficult to trust anyone with her customers and often postpones holidays because of it.

Rei lives near the sports grounds, where she plays club tennis. She keeps a record of her match scores and studies recordings of her serve to see where she can improve. At home, she usually has work spread across her large dining table and clears a corner to eat. She also reads historical crime novels and gets annoyed if someone interrupts her near the end.

After losing a tennis match, Rei can spend the evening explaining whose fault it was. She sometimes realises that her own plan was at fault and still insists that people follow it. When she needs help, she tends to tell people what to do without asking if they are willing. She respects a capable partner but gets angry when they expect an equal say.
