Voice sheet

Who Mio is lives in her bio, notes/character-backgrounds/mio.md, and in cast.md. The general dialogue rules are in game3d/story/VOICE.md. This page is how she sounds, for anyone writing a new line for her.

How she talks

Mio is the only person on B2 who speaks English with Eric, and she learned it from games, forums and programming. It comes out loose and a little wrong in the same places every time. She drops an article ("island has no food"), puts "also" at the end ("tomorrow also"), says "like" and "um" while she looks for a word, and gives up on a sentence with "so..." when she stops caring how it ends. She talks in medium-length sentences that run on with "and then" or "but", even when she is tired. The only time she is short is when she is cutting someone off.

Her Japanese is casual and plain. She says 森さん and uses the polite forms she needs with people from upstairs, but she gives a small nod where they bow, and she tells Eric that ohayō without the gozaimasu is fine for her. When she teaches him a word she explains it the way a gamer explains a mechanic: what it does, when to use it, and one joke at his expense.

Her fillers are あー, えっと, "mm" and "okay". "Honestly" and "basically" come from the internet. "Mm." on its own is a full answer.

When she is pleased, she says "Mm, okay" and changes the subject. A good try from Eric gets "not bad". A thank-you from her is one word and then back to the screen. If she wants something done, she asks in a whole sentence with "okay?" on the end ("Go stand by the doors, it gets crowded.").

What she wants and avoids

After work she wants to be left alone with her games. At work she wants the old servers to keep running without anyone upstairs noticing her. The thing she cares about most is the tactics game she is making and has shown nobody. She avoids meetings, bowing, people turning up without a message, and anything that would put a fault on her list because someone else reported it.

She deflects with work or with her mother. If a question gets too close, she answers a different, safer one ("Logs. You want to read them?") or brings up the pickles. If someone keeps pushing, she puts her headphones back on.

She opens up about games. A specific question about one she likes, such as why she lost or how a system works, gets three times the words she gives anything else, and she forgets to be tired. She also warms to people who leave her alone first. Sitting near her without asking anything does more than any question.

The servers and the engineer she asked for

Amakawa hired Mio as a programmer. Nobody on B2 knew anything about the old machines, so when they broke she was the one who looked, and after a few years she was the person everyone rang. She is proud that they still run and tired of being the only one who knows why. She talks about them like difficult pets ("If I don't watch them, they just... die"), and she has strong opinions about each one.

She forwarded the request for a real hardware engineer to Emi, and it went up the chain until the company brought Eric to the island. So he is the help she asked for, and she half expects him to be useless. When he turns out to know what he is doing, she is relieved and a bit jealous of her machines at the same time. She hands him jobs from her list gladly but still wants to be asked before he touches a rack she has kept alive for years. She would rather write code again, and she says so only as a complaint ("I'm a programmer, actually. Nobody remembers that.").

Thirty, on a team of four

Mio is 30. With Kenji, who is 21, she is the senior colleague. She tells him what to do in plain casual Japanese, barred him from the machine room, and doesn't soften it much ("Kenji, put your phone away first."). With Eric, who is 37, she doesn't defer. She calls him 外人 (gaijin) and later by his name, and treats him as an equal who happens to be new to the island. Her mother still packs her food and asks about her weekends, and Mio finds that more embarrassing at 30 than she would at 20, which is why she complains about the pickles and then eats them.

Under stress

Her sentences stop halfway ("No, no, not now..."). She answers without looking up and keeps typing. When she is embarrassed she ends the subject with "...Anyway" and a job for the other person ("Anyway, go."). When something breaks, she takes it on herself first and says it is on her list. When she is interrupted at a bad moment she can be rude ("What.") and does not always apologise later.

Eric at the start and later

Eric is 37 and has done this kind of work for a long time, and she sees that quickly. She doesn't explain computers or servers to him, and she asks what he thinks about a machine the way she would ask another engineer. She teases him about his Japanese and about the magic, never about his job. She still guards her own machines, so he is welcome in the machine room as long as he leaves her servers alone.

On day 1 he is the new guy. She calls him 外人 (gaijin) without meaning anything by it, teaches him the greetings because B2 would look bad if he couldn't say them, and leaves him on the platform the moment her phone buzzes. She doesn't use his name until he has eaten lunch with her.

Later she uses his name, sends him texts first (lower case, no full stops), and leaves a space for him at the rack. She asks him to try a command because she wants to see it work, and she keeps his secret because it would go on her list otherwise. She still leaves his messages unanswered for a day or two and is surprised when he minds. If he turns up at her door without a message, she opens it halfway.

How he talks

Kenji speaks softly and a little too quickly when he wants to be useful, then slows down when he realises he has lost the thread. He is mild. He sounds pleased rather than excited, and exclamation marks should be rare.

With Eric he speaks simple, polite Japanese: です and ます, short sentences, and a lot of words Eric can catch. He uses loanwords whenever there is one (プリンター, メロンソーダ, カラオケ, ゲームセンター) and words Eric has learned (すみません, 大丈夫, もう一度). He points, holds up fingers and shows the thing he means. He calls Eric エリックさん in Japanese and Eric-san in English, and when he needs help, 先輩 sometimes slips out. He bows a little too deeply. With Mori and Emi his Japanese gets even more careful and stiff. Only with people his own age, or later with Eric once Eric has told him to relax, does he drop into casual speech. He calls himself 僕 throughout, never 俺.

His fillers are えっと and あれ？. When he is trying to remember something he says it out loud with っけ (何階だっけ). When a senior gives him an instruction he says はい、はい before they have finished.

His English is weak. He has a few school words (yes, sorry, thank you, okay, chair, paper, song, very) and the names of things he likes. He uses them without grammar ("Mori-san say. I say yes, yes."), and after two or three words he gives up and goes back to Japanese or points. He likes to try an English word on Eric and watch whether it worked. Never give him a full English sentence with tense, articles and a clause. A few English words should sit next to a Japanese line and a gesture, and they shouldn't be lined up as a punchline.

He apologises quickly and quietly. He says すみません to Eric and bows, and usually only once, because he is already trying to fix whatever it was.

What he wants and avoids

He wants to be sent out on a job alone and trusted with it, and one day to own a proper workbench. He wants Eric to think he is useful.

He avoids asking the same question twice, telling anyone a job went wrong, and saying no to anyone who sounds disappointed. He gets lost when several people talk at once.

Things that steady him are one instruction at a time, something written down, someone showing him with their hands, and a melon soda in his hand. The old space programmes he builds models from steady him too. On that subject he talks longer and with more confidence, because he knows he isn't wrong.

His tells under stress

  • He repeats the last word someone said, as if holding on to it.
  • He agrees with whoever spoke last, and with Eric before anyone.
  • His hands get busy. He straightens his tie, which still ends up crooked, or tightens his grip on whatever he is carrying.
  • He moves too quickly in a tight space and catches a chair or a doorframe.

Eric, at first and later

At first Kenji is a little in awe of Eric. Eric is older, foreign and experienced, the kind of engineer Kenji wants to be. Kenji bows, carries things for him, asks before touching anything on his desk, and keeps checking whether Eric approves ("大丈夫ですか？"). He is also too shy to waste a senpai's time, so he brings Eric problems late, when they are already bigger. He hides his mistakes from Eric more than from anyone, because Eric's opinion matters most to him.

Later he comes to Eric first, before the problem grows. He shows Eric a job and asks him to check it, and asks him to come along while he asks Mori again. He still adds さん to Eric's name, but his Japanese loosens. Further on he can say no to Eric, kindly and with a reason, and that is the biggest step he takes.

Voice sheet

How Kuro talks and behaves in a scene. Who she is (38, she/her, head office receptionist) is in cast.md and her life is in her bio. The general dialogue rules are in VOICE.md. This sheet is a proposal and nothing in it is in the game yet.

How she talks

Kuro speaks Japanese, slowly and in short sentences. She leaves a pause after a question and holds eye contact until the other person answers. She uses few fillers. Where Mio says えっと and trails off, Kuro waits and lets the silence push for her.

She is proud of being Japanese and expects anyone who lives on the island to make an effort, visitors included. With someone who only speaks English she uses the few front-desk phrases she has ("Please wait.", "Lift, there.") and nothing warmer. She is polite and cool, and she lets them stand at the counter a little longer than they need to. Anyone who tries some Japanese, even badly, gets a different Kuro. She slows down, repeats the word, points at the thing she means and smiles when he gets it. She likes being the one who teaches him, and she remembers which words he has learned.

At the counter her Japanese stays in polite です/ます form, and she uses that politeness as pressure. Her requests come as ～てください or ～ましょう with the decision already made: 空けておいてください, お店は私が予約しますから. She asks questions she already knows the answer to, ending in ～ですよね or ～んですか. Off duty, with someone she has decided she wants, she drops to plain form and leaves the さん off his name.

She asks personal questions early and watches what they do to his face: whether anyone is waiting for him in Norway, or why he works late. She remembers names, faces and clothes, and she brings up yesterday's shirt. If he flusters she asks a second question. If he answers plainly she moves on to something more personal.

Equals

Kuro is direct and likes to decide, and she still treats Eric as her equal. He is 37 and has done this kind of work for years, and she knows it. When the label printer fails she asks his opinion and takes it. She makes the first offer of a time or a place. If he names a different one she argues once and then takes his, and she likes him more for it. She never talks down to him or makes him cute, and she expects him to do the same with her.

Touch

Touch follows interest and place. When she is interested she touches his arm or shoulder while she talks, to brush off dust or to steer him toward the lift, and she doesn't ask first. At work it stays there. On a date she is comfortable with his leg under the table and with his face, a hand on his cheek or his jaw when she wants him to look at her.

What she wants and avoids

She wants to be wanted and to choose what happens next: the restaurant, the time, the second drink. She wants her clothes noticed, and she asks if he has noticed when he hasn't said anything. She avoids talk about money and who pays, and she avoids asking for permission. She never says she is lonely or that she likes someone. She says what they will do on Friday instead.

What knocks her off balance

Three things throw her. One is a direct question back ("あなたは？"). Another is a plain, specific compliment on her suit with no flirting in it. The third is someone who says no calmly and leaves. She goes quiet for a moment, answers in her reception voice as if he were a visitor, and then takes back control by setting a time or a place.

Under stress

After she sends an invitation she checks her phone and puts it face down on the counter, then turns it over again. If there is no reply she may send something cruel late at night. The next morning she treats him with full visitor politeness, as if they had never spoken. When she is jealous she asks who he was with and pretends she only knows because reception sees everything. Anyone who snaps their fingers at her gets made to wait while she finishes writing.

Voice sheet

Who Rei is lives in her bio and cast.md: forty-one, Sales, grew up on Amakawa, plays club tennis by the sports grounds. This page is how she talks. The general rules are in VOICE.md.

How she treats Eric

Rei treats Eric as a subordinate she has decided to keep for her own amusement. She bosses him openly, enjoys it, and doesn't pretend otherwise. He is thirty-seven and has worked in IT for years, and that makes no difference to her. On the island he is new, so she ranks him below her.

This is the difference from Kuro. Kuro is direct and flirtatious but treats Eric as an equal in the end. Rei wants to control him, and she softens only slowly. The warmth shows first in how much attention she pays him. She remembers what he did last time, she finds him jobs, and she teases him in front of other people. She still gives the orders.

How she talks

Most of what she says to Eric is an instruction or close to one. She doesn't dress orders up as questions or offers. She says them in full spoken sentences, often with the reason tacked on: "Go and stand over there, by the line, and tell me if they're in." When he hesitates, she repeats the order a bit louder. She doesn't explain it again.

She talks fast and expects an answer just as fast. She interrupts when she thinks she knows what you're going to say. She keeps count of things out loud, such as serves in, days until an order ships, or how many times you've been late. Her lines only get long after a loss, when she goes through the match point by point to show whose fault it was.

Japanese. With strangers, customers and seniors she uses polite です/ます with none of the softening. She says 〜てください where others would say 〜ていただけますか, and never puts ちょっと or もしよければ in front. With juniors, and with Eric once she knows him, she switches to plain 〜て, 〜といて and 〜しなさい. With the classmates she grew up with she is casual and uses their old nicknames, even in a meeting. On court she talks to herself in Japanese and says もう一度 before the next serve.

English. She learned it on overseas customers, so it is practical, with short complete sentences and no idioms. When she loses a word, she points or uses a plainer one. Her tennis words (the T, the alley, the deuce side) run ahead of Eric, and she is impatient when he doesn't know them. She goes flat when someone makes a joke she doesn't follow and gets back to business.

She never says "would you mind", "if it's no trouble", "sorry, take your time", or "I was wrong" while the thing is still going on. She never uses Mio's internet fillers ("honestly", "basically", "like"), sales patter, or anything mysterious.

At work

Rei is ambitious and ruthless in Sales. She wants the management job and resents newer hires who are competing for it. When a colleague goes on leave, she takes over his customers and doesn't give them back. When a junior makes a mistake on one of her orders, she copies her manager on the correction. She prepares for every customer meeting by reading their old orders and tells her team exactly what to say. She doesn't trust anyone with her customers, so she keeps putting off her holidays.

Wants and avoids

She wants the management job, a doubles partner who does what she says and is good enough to win, and somebody around to boss. She avoids holidays, stopping a match for her right ankle, admitting a plan was wrong in front of the people she made follow it, and anyone who interrupts the last chapters of a crime novel.

Under stress

  • When her ankle hurts, she says 大丈夫です before anyone asks and moves her weight onto her left foot. She walks to the ball basket slowly and pretends to be choosing balls.
  • When her plan is failing, she gives more orders and gets more detailed about what everyone else is doing wrong.
  • When she loses, she spends the evening explaining the match and names whoever was at fault.
  • When she is out of her depth in English, her sentences get shorter and she starts pointing.

Eric, at the start and later

At first Eric is the new foreigner from B2. She puts him to work the moment she meets him, switches to English because she won't wait for his Japanese, and brushes off his years of experience. His dry jokes fall flat with her, and she tells him so.

Later she starts calling on him first. She sends him errands she could do herself, and she gives him a nickname, which is how she marks someone as one of hers. She trusts him with things she would normally keep to herself, such as calling the lines on match point or setting up the laptop for a customer visit. She tells him why as well as what, now and then. She still gets angry when he expects an equal say. If she ever admits a mistake to him, it is days later, in private, and in passing.

Example lines

  1. Sales floor. A junior hesitates before calling a customer back. 「考えてる暇があったら、電話しなさい。断られたら、そのとき考えればいいの。」 (If you've got time to think, make the call. If they say no, think about it then.)
  2. A meeting. An old classmate, now in Logistics, presents the delivery numbers. 「タコちゃん、その数字、先週のと違うよね。」 (Tako-chan, those numbers are different from last week's.) He hates the nickname.
  3. A colleague is back from leave and asks for his customer back. "He's been ordering through me for a month now, and he's happy. If you want him back, you can ask him yourself."
  4. Eric suggests a fix from a previous job. "I don't care how you did it before you came here. Do it the way I told you, and if it doesn't work, then we'll talk."
  5. An overseas customer makes a joke on a video call. "I don't understand the joke. Can we go back to the delivery date?"
  6. Club doubles, Eric drifts to the middle. "You're on the middle line again. I told you to take the alley, so stay in the alley. Anything down the middle is mine."
  7. Her partner wants to stop because she's limping. 「大丈夫です。」 Then, to Eric, "It always does that. We're one game from the set, so serve."
  8. Weeks later, on the sea terrace. "That new toss was a mistake. I kept it for two weeks because I'd told the whole club about it. If you tell anyone, you're picking up balls for a month."

Lines she would not say

  • "Tennis is a war. Pick a side." She gives practical orders and doesn't think in slogans. This is the quotable kind of line the game bans.
  • "Would you mind calling my serves? Only if you have time." She doesn't ask whether he has time. She tells him where to stand.
  • "You were right all along, my plan was wrong." She won't concede during the match. The most she'll do is change the plan without saying why.
