# Mini stories and jobs

Written 2026-09-27 while Jørgen was away. He asked for "some mini stories we can put in the game, relationship progression and such, ideas on jobs. Text only, set a limit yourself." The limit I set: twelve stories of 350 to 600 words, a jobs section of about 1,500 words, and under 9,000 words in all.

Nothing here is approved. It follows GUIDE.md: English carries the text, one or two new Japanese items per beat, casual speech for the first half of the game, choices that test understanding, and the magic kept secret. Every story assumes day 1 has happened, so the player has already used kotodama alone in the copy room and knows that things take commands literally.

## How to read the stories

The stages are the ones in island/private/rewards/CONCEPTS.md: 1 colleague, 2 friend, 3 close, 4 romance. The last beat of each story lets the player choose romance or friendship, and both paths keep paying.

Each beat names a place on the island and a moment of play, then lists:
- Japanese: the new items, with kana, romaji and meaning. Anything marked review was taught earlier.
- Play: what the player does.
- Unlocks: the next stage, a perk, a place, an item or a reward picture.

Reward pictures are named only by their concept ID from CONCEPTS.md or the round-27 and round-28 folders. Where nothing fits, the beat says "new reward" with a neutral title. This file stays public, so it doesn't describe any picture.

The player is the Nordic IT hire in Planning 7 (企画室7), the basement team on the closure list. Emi leads the team and appears in some beats, but she has no story here.

# Part A: mini stories

## 1. Rei: between floors

Rei (26) runs the top Sales team on the fifth floor, and Sales wants Planning 7's budget. She's polite to the player's face and talks fast and exactly, so her beats test listening.

1. Colleague. The lift, a morning in week 1.
Rei holds the doors with one hand and doesn't look up from her phone: 「何階？」 He answers by pressing a button. B2 gets a short nod. Pressing 5 takes him up to Sales with her, where she walks off and he rides back down ten minutes late for Emi.
   - Japanese: 何階 (なんがい, nangai): which floor?
   - Play: a question answered by acting, on the lift panel.
   - Unlocks: stage 1 and her contact in the phone. After this she presses B2 for him without asking.

2. Friend. Planning 7, week 2.
Rei comes down to the basement for the first time. Sales needs ten years of order data from a database only Mio can open, and Rei leaves a note on Emi's desk: 「これ、明日まで。」 Mio asks him when it's due: today, tomorrow or Friday. With "tomorrow" the file is on Rei's desk at nine. With "Friday" Rei comes back down herself, cold, and the next beat waits a week.
   - Japanese: 明日 (あした, ashita): tomorrow; まで (made): by, until.
   - Play: a choice that depends on reading her note.
   - Unlocks: stage 2. The Sales floor without a visitor pass. When her team isn't around, Rei switches to casual speech with him.

3. Friend. The lift, a later morning.
Alone with her on the way up, he wants five minutes to ask what Sales wants with B2. He tells the panel 「止まれ」, the word painted on the road by the station. The lift stops between floors and takes it literally, so the fan and the air conditioning stop as well. 「待て」 only holds the doors at the next floor, and she walks out. After twenty minutes she has told him about the budget meeting, and she asks 「……わざと？」 (on purpose?).
   - Japanese: 止まれ (とまれ, tomare): stop.
   - Play: a spell, with weaker results for partial commands.
   - Unlocks: rei-lift. The full budget note, which he can take to Emi. Rei's suspicion +1.

4. Close. The emergency stairwell.
Rei loses the quarter's top ranking to another team and climbs twelve floors of stairs so nobody sees her face. He finds her under the green exit light. 「大丈夫。……一人にして。」 If he understands and leaves, she texts 「ありがと。」 that night. If he sits down beside her, she gets up and goes, and the stage waits.
   - Japanese: 一人にして (ひとりにして, hitori ni shite): leave me alone.
   - Play: a choice where understanding means doing what she asked.
   - Unlocks: stage 3. rei-stairs. The stairwell becomes a place to find her at lunch.

5. Romance. The company trip (社員旅行) to a hot-spring town.
On the first night the outdoor bath is so full of steam that nobody can see the view. 「晴れろ」 clears it, and everyone's cover with it, and Rei is the only other person in the water. Later, on the hotel balcony, she holds up a bottle: 「飲まない？」 A beginner reads ない as "don't drink" and says no, and she drinks alone. It's an invitation, and saying うん opens the romance route. Declining keeps them friends, and she asks again on the next trip.
   - Japanese: 晴れろ (はれろ, harero): clear up; 飲まない？ (のまない, nomanai): want a drink? (〜ない？ as an invitation).
   - Play: a spell, then a choice.
   - Unlocks: stage 4 or friendship. rei-onsen, rei-balcony. Perk: Rei passes Planning 7 the Sales requests that pay rank.

## 2. Mio: three hundred cans

Mio (25) is Planning 7's programmer. She's very good, low on energy, and uses one word where other people use five. Planning 7 is the only job she has kept, and the closure scares her more than she shows. She's where tech words and slang come from, and she hates keigo.

1. Colleague. The vending machine in the B2 corridor.
The machine has eaten her coins again. She kicks it, says 「……出せ。」 and gives up. If she's in the office, 「出せ」 returns her coins and she finds them on her desk. With her standing there, 「全部出せ」 empties the machine: three hundred cans, her coins, and one coffee she holds up without expression (「……出しすぎ。」).
   - Japanese: 出せ (だせ, dase): give it out; 全部 (ぜんぶ, zenbu): everything.
   - Play: a spell where it matters who is watching.
   - Unlocks: stage 1. mio-cans for the 全部 version, which costs suspicion +1. From then on, a can on his desk every morning.

2. Friend. The server room in August.
The B2 air conditioning dies and the server room reaches 38 °C. Mio sits on the floor by the racks with an ice lolly, watching a build she can't afford to lose: 「暑い……」 「動け」 gets a weak breeze. The full 「部屋を冷やせ」 brings the room to a steady 20, and she stays to watch the build finish with him.
   - Japanese: 暑い (あつい, atsui): hot (weather, a room); 冷やせ (ひやせ, hiyase): cool it.
   - Play: a spell that needs the object and the verb.
   - Unlocks: stage 2. mio-server. Mio starts sending him tech words, which become commands for monitors and routers.

3. Friend. The dorm laundry at 2 a.m.
Her hoodie is in a coin dryer that takes the coins and does nothing. 「……直して。」 He tells it 「パーカーを早く乾かせ」, and it takes 早く literally: full heat and full spin, until it walks across the floor and she climbs on top to hold it down. 「動け」 alone gives a normal sixty-minute cycle and she goes to bed.
   - Japanese: 乾かせ (かわかせ, kawakase): dry it; 早く (はやく, hayaku): quickly.
   - Play: a spell.
   - Unlocks: mio-dryer. The laundry as a late-night place to find her.

4. Close. The convenience store, the night before a release.
Mio is buying energy drinks for the whole night. At the fridge, without turning round, she says 「……一緒に、やる？」 Saying うん costs him the evening slot, which was Emi's bar night. Staying gets the scene where she says what Planning 7 means to her, and the release goes out clean in the morning.
   - Japanese: 一緒に (いっしょに, issho ni): together.
   - Play: a choice that costs time.
   - Unlocks: stage 3. mio-konbini. Perk: Mio takes one tech request off the board each week.

5. Romance. Game night in her dorm room.
They play until 3 a.m. Every evening at six she says 「帰る。」 on her way out of the office, so he knows that word. Now she asks 「……泊まってく？」 It sounds like 止まれ from the lift, and the gloss shows it once: staying over? Answering 帰る keeps them friends, and she nods. Answering うん opens the romance route.
   - Japanese: 泊まってく (とまってく, tomatteku): staying the night? (from 泊まる, to stay over); 帰る (かえる, kaeru): go home.
   - Play: a choice that tests two words.
   - Unlocks: stage 4 or friendship. mio-dorm, then mio-shirt the next morning.

## 3. Aoi: the intern who wants to stay

Aoi (24, decided by Jørgen 2026-10-05) is a graduate intern rotating through departments before placement, as many Japanese new hires do. She wants a full-time place and is loud about everything except that. She's his source of katakana: loanwords, brand names and arcade slang.

1. Colleague. The staff car park.
Sales has given the intern the executives' cars to wash. The hose barely dribbles, and she asks him to look at the tap. 「水を出せ」 turns it on full, and the hose whips across three cars and her. 「出せ」 alone gives a trickle.
   - Japanese: 水 (みず, mizu): water.
   - Play: a spell with a verb he owns and a new object.
   - Unlocks: stage 1. aoi-carwash.

2. Friend. The company beach day.
She can't blow up her flamingo float: 「ふくらませて！」 He tells the float 「膨らめ」 and it keeps swelling until it's the size of a house. 「少し膨らめ」 gives a normal float. On the giant one, Aoi rides the shallows while the whole of Sales watches, and it's the first time most of them learn her name.
   - Japanese: 膨らめ (ふくらめ, fukurame): swell; 少し (すこし, sukoshi): a little.
   - Play: a spell where the amount decides the result.
   - Unlocks: stage 2. aoi-float. Aoi is added to the Sales team chat.

3. Friend. The Sales karaoke, second party (二次会).
The intern sings first, by custom. Aoi picks a song and freezes when the lyrics come up in kanji. She pushes the second mic at him: 「一緒に歌って！」 They play the karaoke rhythm game together. A good score gets the room on its feet; a poor one still gets them through the song, to politer applause.
   - Japanese: 歌って (うたって, utatte): sing (a request); 一緒に (review).
   - Play: the karaoke minigame.
   - Unlocks: aoi-karaoke. A second song in the karaoke list.

4. Close. The rooftop, the last day of her rotation.
Sales has offered her a place. Planning 7 is short of people and on the closure list. She asks him 「どう思う？」 If he misreads it as a yes-or-no question and says うん, she says 「……うん、って何？」 and takes Sales without his advice. If he understood, he answers with a card: 「営業、いいよ」 (Sales is good) or 「地下に来て」 (come to the basement). In B2 she's on his team, and at risk with it. In Sales she becomes his contact upstairs.
   - Japanese: どう思う？ (どうおもう, dō omou): what do you think?; 来て (きて, kite): come.
   - Play: a choice with stakes on both sides.
   - Unlocks: stage 3. Aoi joins Planning 7 (one more pair of hands for requests) or becomes the Sales contact (early word on Sales plans).

5. Romance. The arcade on the shopping street.
She has spent 2,000 yen on a claw machine for one plush toy. 「取って！」 He tells the claw 「掴め」 and it grabs everything it can reach, until the prize chute jams with toys. 「一つ掴め」 gets her the one she wanted. In the pile of toys she makes her move, and he chooses whether to meet it or laugh it off.
   - Japanese: 掴め (つかめ, tsukame): grab; 一つ (ひとつ, hitotsu): one (thing).
   - Play: a spell with a counter, then a choice.
   - Unlocks: stage 4 or friendship. aoi-arcade.

## 4. Kaori: the last bath

Kaori (44) runs the canteen kitchen. She's patient with foreigners and speaks slow, soft casual Japanese, calling everyone あんた. The peony tattoo on her forearm keeps her out of most public baths in Japan, so she uses the dorm bath last, after everyone has gone to bed.

1. Colleague. The canteen, first lunch.
The menu board is in katakana. Once he can read a dish he can order it with a card: 「カレー、ください。」 She gives him a bigger portion than the salaryman before him and doesn't mention it. He can order a dish he can't read, and gets what she points at, which is natto.
   - Japanese: カレー (karē): curry; ください (kudasai): please (give me).
   - Play: reading a menu, then a phrase card.
   - Unlocks: stage 1. Lunch effects (+1 patience in the afternoon's talk).

2. Friend. The kitchen at 5 a.m. on sports day, then the dorm that night.
Kaori is making four hundred bento with her assistant off sick, and she puts him to work: 「お湯、沸かして。」 (boil some water). He fills and starts the big kettle. That night the dorm bath's boiler is broken again, and he tells it 「風呂を沸かせ」. 沸かす literally means bring to the boil, and it does. The steam sets off the fire alarm, and Kaori comes out into the corridor with a towel and a bottle of coffee milk: 「……あんたか。」 「温めろ」 gives a perfect bath and no scene.
   - Japanese: 沸かす (わかす, wakasu): heat or boil water, as 沸かして and 沸かせ.
   - Play: a request acted out, then a spell with the idiom as the trap.
   - Unlocks: stage 2. kaori-bath. Kaori keeps a table for him, so he can lunch with two people at once.

3. Close. The rooftop garden.
The canteen runs out of green onion in the lunch rush and Kaori shouts 「ネギがない！」 across the kitchen. Goro grows it on the roof. If he caught the word he goes up, and finds Kaori there at golden hour watering the beds, which she talked Goro into planting. She tells him why she left a Tokyo restaurant for a company canteen.
   - Japanese: ネギ (negi): green onion; ない (nai): there's none.
   - Play: overhearing, then acting on it.
   - Unlocks: stage 3. kaori-hose. Kitchen words (切れ, 焼け) that become spells in the izakaya evenings (Part B).

4. Close. The rooftop beer garden (屋上ビアガーデン).
The beer server jams on the first hot night of summer. 「注げ」 pours and doesn't stop. 「一杯注げ」 is the clean pass. The fountain version gets the whole garden laughing, and Kaori says 「……あんた、おごりね。」 (your treat, then).
   - Japanese: 注げ (つげ, tsuge): pour; 一杯 (いっぱい, ippai): one glass.
   - Play: a spell where the amount decides the result.
   - Unlocks: kaori-beergarden.

5. Romance. The canteen after closing.
She cooks one dish for him and holds out the spoon: 「味見して。」 He watched her salt it twice while she was talking. Saying 「おいしい」 makes her laugh and pour the pot away. The honest answer, 「しょっぱい」, gets it fixed and her sitting down across from him. This beat is only open if Kiyoko's story didn't end in the cellar (story 11).
   - Japanese: 味見して (あじみして, ajimi shite): taste this; しょっぱい (shoppai): salty.
   - Play: a choice where you say what you noticed.
   - Unlocks: stage 4 or friendship. new reward: Kaori after closing.

## 5. Yuzuki: 以上です

Yuzuki (31) is the company's PR spokeswoman, with a practised smile that never slips in public. She ends everything with 「以上です」 (that concludes my remarks), even at karaoke. Her story is about the few people who get to see her off duty.

1. Colleague. The station plaza, the new fountain.
She unveils the fountain for the press and it doesn't start. Her smile holds. From the crowd, he says 「もっと上がれ」, and all the water goes twenty metres up and comes down on the podium. 「ゆっくり上がれ」 gives a perfect show and a polite thank-you. Soaked, she laughs for real in public for the first time, then closes like a professional: 「……以上です！」
   - Japanese: 上がれ (あがれ, agare): go up; もっと (motto): more.
   - Play: a spell in front of a crowd, where suspicion grows with the size of the result.
   - Unlocks: stage 1. yuzuki-fountain. PR's event calendar on his phone, listing every set piece.

2. Friend. The Sales-and-PR karaoke night.
The karaoke chain lends costumes from a machine by the door, and the loser at rock-paper-scissors wears what it gives. Yuzuki loses. He tells the machine 「一番いいのを出せ」, and to a costume machine the best one is the most expensive. 「出せ」 alone gives a paper hat. She sings the company anthem in it and wears it like a work outfit.
   - Japanese: 一番 (いちばん, ichiban): the most, number one.
   - Play: a spell.
   - Unlocks: stage 2. yuzuki-bunny.

3. Close. A dressing room at a TV station on the mainland.
Twenty minutes before a live interview, her stockings ladder. She hands him her purse: 「ストッキング、買ってきて。」 The convenience store next door has three packs on the hook, labelled ストッキング, タイツ and ソックス, and he has to read them. With the wrong pack she goes on air as she is, and the clip is on the PR team chat by lunch.
   - Japanese: ストッキング (sutokkingu): stockings; 買ってきて (かってきて, katte kite): go and buy it.
   - Play: an errand decided by reading katakana.
   - Unlocks: stage 3. yuzuki-dressing. Perk: PR warns Planning 7 before any announcement about it.

4. Close. The year-end party (忘年会).
She's on duty all night with toasts, introductions and the bingo. He offers to run the bingo drum for her. A spell would be seen by two hundred people, so this one is by hand: he calls each number aloud in Japanese and the room checks its cards. Call them right and she gets to sit down for the first time that evening.
   - Japanese: 番 (ばん, ban): number (as in 七番); numbers to 75 (review).
   - Play: reading and saying numbers under pressure, no magic.
   - Unlocks: yuzuki-party.

5. Romance. The taxi rank after the party.
Everyone at work calls her by her surname with さん. Waiting for a taxi, she says 「名前で呼んで。」 Moving to a first name is a real step between Japanese colleagues. His next line is how he addresses her. Her surname with さん keeps them friends. Plain ゆずき opens the romance route. ゆずきさん makes her laugh and say close enough, and the choice comes round again next week.
   - Japanese: 名前 (なまえ, namae): name; 呼んで (よんで, yonde): call (me).
   - Play: a choice of address that tests what she asked for.
   - Unlocks: stage 4 or friendship. new reward: Yuzuki at the taxi rank.

## 6. Tsubasa: the watch

Tsubasa (27) runs on the company's ekiden team and works in General Affairs in the afternoons, which is how corporate athletes in Japan usually work. She measures everything with the stopwatch in her hand and talks in times and distances, so her beats drill numbers.

1. Colleague. The athletics track at 6 a.m.
Her usual lap counter is off sick. She hands him a clipboard and calls each split as she passes: 「七十二秒！」 He picks the number he heard. A wrong one shows up the next day, when her log says she ran impossibly fast and she asks who wrote it.
   - Japanese: 秒 (びょう, byō): seconds; numbers 60 to 80 (review).
   - Play: listening, with her log as the proof.
   - Unlocks: stage 1. tsubasa-track. Access to the company gym.

2. Friend. The gym sauna (mixed, swimwear only by the gym's rules).
It's 70 °C, which a Nordic player finds weak. 「もっと熱くなれ」 takes it to 110, and everyone leaves except Tsubasa, who does sauna after every run. 「熱くなれ」 alone reaches 80. She times it: 「……あと一分。」 Mio's 暑い is for weather and rooms. 熱い is for things you touch, and the stove is one.
   - Japanese: 熱い (あつい, atsui): hot (to the touch); あと (ato): … more, … left.
   - Play: a spell.
   - Unlocks: stage 2. tsubasa-sauna.

3. Close. The track, an overcast afternoon.
The team physio has told her two weeks without running. She hands him her sports watch so she won't be tempted: 「時計、持ってて。」 (hold on to it). Three days later she finds him at lunch: 「返して。」 If he understands, he keeps it and plays the card 「だめ」. If he gives it back, she runs that evening, the injury gets worse, and the ekiden beat moves back a month. She's angry with him for a day and grateful after.
   - Japanese: 返して (かえして, kaeshite): give it back; だめ (dame): no, not allowed.
   - Play: a choice where understanding means refusing.
   - Unlocks: stage 3. tsubasa-icebath, on her first day back.

4. Close. The company pool after closing.
She swims night laps when nobody's there and calls up to the gallery for the lights. 「光れ」, the word from the karaoke song, turns on every light in the building at once. 「点け」 turns on one row, the plain way. 「まぶしっ！」
   - Japanese: 光れ (ひかれ, hikare): shine; 点け (つけ, tsuke): switch on.
   - Play: a spell with a quiet version and a loud one.
   - Unlocks: tsubasa-nightpool.

5. Romance. The New Year ekiden, the finish line.
The team runs the relay on 1 January and she has the last leg, with forty seconds to make up. He waits at the finish with the team staff. She crosses the line and, before anyone else reaches her, asks him 「……何位？」 The results board flips fast and he has to read her place off it. Get it right and she hears it from him first. Then she asks 「一緒に帰る？」 in words he already has. うん opens the romance route. Going back with the team keeps them friends.
   - Japanese: 何位 (なんい, nan'i): what place?; 位 (い, i): place in a ranking, as in 三位.
   - Play: reading a board under pressure, then a choice.
   - Unlocks: stage 4 or friendship. tsubasa-morning.

## 7. Kanae: the new-hire page

Kanae (30) shoots the company newsletter (社内報) on film, because digital makes everyone look like a brochure. The newsletter is on the same cost list as Planning 7, and she's saving to go freelance. She's bossy with a camera in her hands and quiet without one.

1. Colleague. Planning 7, week 1.
Every issue has a page introducing new hires (新人紹介). She stands him against the wall and directs: 「こっち見て。」「笑って。」 Each instruction he follows improves the shot. Get them wrong and the issue pinned up in the canteen the next week shows him mid-blink, looking at the ceiling, and Mio uses it as her desktop background.
   - Japanese: 笑って (わらって, waratte): smile; こっち (kotchi): this way, here.
   - Play: acting on instructions, with a result everyone can see.
   - Unlocks: stage 1. The newsletter on the canteen board, a new thing to read each week.

2. Friend. The dorm roof on a windy morning.
A gust tears her laundry off the line and she shouts at him to do something. 「戻れ」 brings back every piece of laundry on the roof, the whole dorm's, in a flock she ignores so she can photograph it. 「止まれ」 stops her own washing in mid-air, where it stays.
   - Japanese: 戻れ (もどれ, modore): come back.
   - Play: a spell.
   - Unlocks: stage 2. kanae-laundry.

3. Close. The newsletter darkroom.
She asks him to shut the door properly: 「ドア、閉めて。」 He tells it 「閉まれ」 from across the room, it slams, and the old latch jams. 「開け」 would open it, but her paper is in the developer: 「開けるな！」 They spend twenty minutes in the red light. Near the end she raises her camera at him: 「動くな。」 The prints drying on the line behind her are all of him.
   - Japanese: 閉まれ (しまれ, shimare): shut; 〜な (na): don't, as in 動くな (うごくな, ugokuna): don't move.
   - Play: a spell, then a choice where 開け ruins her prints.
   - Unlocks: stage 3. kanae-darkroom. The negative command 〜な for every verb he owns.

4. Close. The photo studio.
For once she hands him the camera: 「撮って。」 It sounds exactly like Aoi's 取って, and the kanji show the difference. He sets up the shot from her directions given from the other side of the lens.
   - Japanese: 撮って (とって, totte): take (a photo).
   - Play: following directions, with the roles swapped.
   - Unlocks: kanae-shoot. Kanae's photos in the phone album.

5. Romance. The last train back from a photo walk on the mainland.
She shows him a letter from a Tokyo agency that wants her work. She gives him one of her earbuds, and as the train goes into a tunnel she says 「目、閉じて。」 If he understands, he closes his eyes and chooses whether to stay close or pull back. If he doesn't, he keeps looking at her, and she takes his photo instead and laughs. That keeps them friends until the next chance.
   - Japanese: 目 (め, me): eyes; 閉じて (とじて, tojite): close (them).
   - Play: an instruction acted on, then a choice.
   - Unlocks: stage 4 or friendship. kanae-train.

## 8. Sumi: lend me a hand

Sumi (24) is General Affairs' in-house calligrapher. She writes certificates, condolence envelopes, desk nameplates and the banner for every company event, and she's always behind. She's impatient and precise and there's ink on her fingers. Kanji come from her: tracing a character with her switches its words from kana to kanji across the whole game.

1. Colleague. Planning 7, week 1.
Every desk has a hand-written nameplate, and his has been blank since he arrived. She comes down to B2 with a brush: 「名前は？」 He answers with the katakana of his name, which he has seen on his ID card. She writes it and corrects how he reads one of the characters.
   - Japanese: 名前 (なまえ, namae): name.
   - Play: a question answered with a card.
   - Unlocks: stage 1. His nameplate.

2. Friend. The calligraphy room, the anniversary banner.
In the morning, carrying the banner poles, she says 「手、貸して」. It's an idiom for "help me". If he took her hand, she glared and carried the poles herself. In the afternoon her ink runs out halfway through the banner: 「墨、ちょうだい。」 He tells the big bottle 「墨を出せ」 and it gives all of it, over her. From the washroom she sends him to her locker: 「……シャツ。早く。」
   - Japanese: 手、貸して (て、かして, te, kashite): lend me a hand; 墨 (すみ, sumi): ink, and her name, which she's tired of hearing about.
   - Play: an idiom tested by acting, then a spell.
   - Unlocks: stage 2. sumi-ink.

3. Close. The company library, evenings.
She teaches him one character per session with a brush, and she keeps saying 「ゆっくり。」 Tracing is a short minigame in which stroke order counts and speed loses points. Each character he finishes switches its words to kanji everywhere, with the reading shown until he knows them.
   - Japanese: 字 (じ, ji): a written character; ゆっくり (yukkuri): slowly.
   - Play: the calligraphy minigame.

## 9. Saki: blue folders

Saki (28) heads Legal, and Planning 7 can't be closed without her department's seal. She notices everything and says about half of it. That makes her the most dangerous person in the company to be seen by.

1. Colleague. The copy room.
He comes in to cast on a jammed copier, and Saki is already there, bent over it and getting nowhere. He reads it, pulls, and clears the jam by hand.
   - Japanese: 引く (ひく, hiku): pull.
   - Play: reading a sign instead of casting.
   - Unlocks: stage 1. saki-copier. She remembers him as the one who reads manuals.

2. Friend. Legal, 1 a.m.
She has three hundred contracts to seal by morning and has fallen asleep at her desk. She wakes to a finished stack and him making coffee.
   - Japanese: 押せ (おせ, ose): press, stamp; 青い (あおい, aoi): blue.

4. Romance. A snowbound inn on Legal's winter trip.

5. Romance. Her floor's balcony at dawn.

# Part B: jobs

The player starts as a new hire (新入社員) in Planning 7. Rank follows a real Japanese ladder: 主任 (shunin, senior staff), 係長 (kakarichō, chief), 課長 (kachō, section manager), 部長 (buchō, general manager). Each promotion opens a floor or district, and from 係長 on he gets subordinates who take requests off his board. Planning 7 lends him out to other departments on secondment (出向), which is how he sees the whole company. Casual Japanese carries the jobs until 係長. Polite forms come with 課長, and keigo only on the executive floors.

## 1. Planning 7 (企画室7), B2

The work is odd jobs for anyone who will send them: copies, broken machines, data nobody else can find. It's where the magic starts, alone in the copy room, and where the player first learns that things take commands literally. Spells here are the basics: 動け, 出せ, 止まれ, with objects and counters (書類を十部出せ). The Japanese it drills is plain imperatives, を and に, and the counters 部 and 枚. Progression is the basement itself coming back to life room by room, as Stardew's Community Center does. Emi and Mio work here, and Aoi may join (story 3).

## 2. Sales (営業部), fifth floor

The work is chasing orders, preparing quotes and visiting clients on the mainland. Magic can't touch people directly, so here it helps with the paperwork around them: printers that finish a quote in seconds, a projector that works on the first try. Persuasion comes from Japanese: casual requests (〜て) with peers, and later 〜ませんか invitations to clients. It drills prices in yen, dates and counters. The first rank-up here is the business-card exchange (名刺交換), the first polite ritual the player has to get right. Rei runs the top team, and Yuzuki's PR works next door.

## 3. General Affairs (総務部)

General Affairs is the company's housekeeping: dorm rules, supplies, event setup, banners, certificates, and the annual sports day (運動会). Spells here move and count things: 並べ (line up) for chairs, 運べ (carry) for boxes, and 数えろ (count) for stock. It drills room names, days of the week and the dorm rules on the noticeboard (no guests after 23:00, rubbish by type on set days). Progression gives keys to the event halls and, later, the job of writing the dorm rules himself. Sumi and Tsubasa work here.

## 4. The canteen kitchen (社員食堂)

The work is prep before dawn, the lunch rush and the bento orders. Nobody watches the back of the kitchen, which makes it the safest place for magic: 切れ (cut), 焼け (grill), 沸かせ (boil). The Japanese is food words, the counters つ, 個 and 杯, and orders shouted across a counter at speed. Rank here is Kaori's trust, from washing pots to running a station. It links to direction 3's izakaya: once he runs a station, he can take over the evening shift in the izakaya under the dorms. Kaori runs it, and Goro supplies the vegetables.

## 5. PR and the newsletter (広報部・社内報)

The work covers press events, launches, the fountain and the festival stage, plus the newsletter's photos and interviews. Spells deal with event machinery (fountains, lights, microphones, curtains), in front of crowds, so the risk is high and a quieter version always exists. It drills reading headlines and katakana loanwords, and later the set phrases of announcements such as 以上です and お待たせしました. Progression moves from carrying the stepladder to running an event's stage, and access to the TV studio on the mainland. Yuzuki and Kanae work here.

## 6. Security and access (警備・入館管理)

The work is gate duty, the ID desk, night rounds and lost property. Doors, gates, lights and lifts are all things, so this job has the most spells: 開け, 閉まれ, 点け, 消えろ. The negative command matters most here (閉まるな, don't shut). It drills times, floors and giving directions to visitors with polite set phrases, which are learned as whole phrases and never taken apart. Progression is clearance: each level adds floors to his card, up to the executive floors. Ishibashi, Nanami and Kuro work here.

## 7. Facilities and the rooftop garden (施設管理)

The work is boilers, sprinklers, lifts, air conditioning and Goro's rooftop beds. This job produces most of the literal disasters: 沸かせ boils the bath, and 止まるな turns the sprinklers on for good. Requests arrive as fault slips from every floor, so the work is also reading: the slip says what's broken (壊れた, 動かない, 水が出ない) and where. It drills weather, temperatures, verbs of motion and 〜な. Progression unlocks the service tunnels under the island and the maintenance lift that reaches every roof, and at 係長 he gets two technicians who take the plain faults while he keeps the strange ones. Goro works here, and Kaori comes up for the onions.

## 8. Legal (法務部)

This job opens at 係長. The work is contracts, seals, the closure paperwork and the company's disputes. Stamps, shredders and printers are the spells (押せ, 消せ), but most of the job is reading documents and speaking correctly. It is the first job where polite forms are required: 〜てください, です and ます used consistently, and the first keigo nouns (ご確認, お名前). Progression brings Legal's calendar, which shows every step of the closure, and later a seat in the meetings where Planning 7's future is decided. Saki runs it.

## 9. The secretariat and the executive floors (秘書室)

This job opens at 課長. The work is executive schedules, entertaining clients (接待), the golf range and the anniversary banquet. The magic has almost no use here, since every room has witnesses and the executives notice everything. The job is keigo: respectful and humble forms (いらっしゃいます, 申します), seating order, and who pours for whom. Mistakes here are social: the wrong form to a director gets a silence, and the next invitation goes to someone else. Progression is a seat at the banquet table and a voice at the board vote on Planning 7. Kiyoko rules this floor, and her faction decides who gets invited up.

## 10. The izakaya under the dorms (evenings)

This is an evening side job that Planning 7 inherits (direction 3). The work is taking orders, cooking with kotodama in a kitchen nobody else enters, and serving the right table. It drills counters (二つ, 三本, 二杯), yen, and listening that starts at one slow customer and builds to a full table of colleagues talking over each other. Progression is the menu: a dish goes on the chalkboard only once he can read and say its name, and the bar's rating climbs on the company review app. The cast come as regulars: Rei after a bad quarter, Mio's game-night crowd, Emi hiding from a budget meeting.

## How jobs and relationships feed each other

Every department has cast members in it, so a job is where the player spends time with them. Each request done for someone in their department counts as a meeting (research-loops.md, section 11.5), and each person teaches the kind of Japanese their job uses: Tsubasa numbers, Sumi kanji, Nanami floors and times, Saki the polite forms. Their relationship ladders then ask for that Japanese back, which in turn makes him better at the job.

The perks run the other way. Close relationships take work off his board (Mio's weekly tech request, Emi's), open doors early (Nanami's archive access) or bring information before a decision (Yuzuki's warnings, Legal's calendar). The biggest perks decide the main plot: Rei's Sales requests pay rank, the red folder going missing buys time, and Kiyoko's faction can save Planning 7 at the board. Promotion also opens the places where later stories happen, such as the executive floor, the company trip and the winter inn, so relationship stages 3 and 4 tend to arrive together with rank.

Suspicion ties the two together. Every visible spell makes someone curious, and the people most likely to notice (Saki, Nanami, Kuro) are the ones whose jobs involve records. A player who reads the sign, uses the dull answer or casts only when alone keeps his secret longer. Kuro's story is the one where he decides to share it.
