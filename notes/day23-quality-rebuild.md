## Acceptance

Every required ticket is requested before the player performs it. The player must observe a problem, do something understandable, and see an outcome that advances learning or the plot. Travel is not the task's substance. New words are modelled in context, attempted through the existing typing interaction, then used again with a person or action. A count of definitions or optional hidden vocabulary does not establish a satisfying day's learning.

Each scene is checked as a cold player with only the words and relationships available on that branch. Speakers retain their language and voice. Comprehension comes from demonstrated action, a colleague's natural help and already-known words; `en` cannot bypass it. Replies must let the player get to know colleagues and use learned words in conversation, with believable reasons to stop when people are busy.

A staging check must show the same people, clothes, objects, eyelines and setting that the dialogue assumes. Required interiors must exist and have a sensible route in and out. A scene is not complete while those dependencies are placeholders.

Review entire branches, not just the default route: lunch histories, both report choices, both protagonists, prior meetings, skipped optional visits, repeated conversation and save/Continue during each substantial scene. Automated route checks establish reachability and state integrity; independent cold reading, normal-camera images and a learning inventory establish the content review. Cross-team cold review is requested before calling the rewrite complete. Voice generation follows settled text, with approved casting and checks of the actual exported clips.
