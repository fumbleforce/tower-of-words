# Scene editor: design and stages

Jørgen asked for this on 2026-10-10. The editor should let an author make whole scenes, or edit ours, the way a professional content tool does. It has to be easy to use and hide how the scenes are stored. Images are edited in place, with history and regeneration, and names, places and looks come from the bible. Every save keeps a revision. His words are in notes/feedback-log/. This page holds the design and the stage plan. How to run the editor and how its adapters work is in [tools/subplots/README.md](../../tools/subplots/README.md).

The URL is http://127.0.0.1:8771/tools/subplots/web/ (start the server with `./start`). It works only on this computer, and it works without an agent session.

His brief, word for word: "a fully blown scene editor like what a professional content editor would see, super easy to use, hiding implementation detail, making it easy to edit scenes and images in-place, change order, change and add narrative text, see it in context, re-generate and edit existing images, maybe generate videos for the images, set restrictions and place in story, fully integrated with data from the bible to avoid drift, keep revisions". On privacy: "this is private mode eligible so no restrictions, just labelling on what goes where".

The author is not technical. Show the actual scene images with the English and Japanese text together. Cast, locations, staging and conditions are readable choices from the bible and the image generator. Internal keys, helper calls and code expressions stay out of the normal editing view.

## Codex's version (judged 2026-10-10)

The backend is sound and stays. That covers the revision store, the conflict checks, the local adapter with its backups, rebuild and checks, the image service proxy and the bible catalog (`authoring.mjs`). The page itself did not work as an editor:

- It listed 1,090 code nodes ("D2 Limits", "Arrive") in one flat list. It had no labels for public and private content and no idea of where a scene sits in the story.
- Every beat was a large form card with two dropdowns and two text boxes. A 37-beat scene ran to about 30 screens, so nobody could read it as a scene.
- Beats could only be moved with ↑ ↓ buttons, one place at a time.
- Undo threw away every change since the scene was opened. There was no redo.
- Each line had a "spoken language" dropdown, and the other language was kept in hidden editor metadata. The author could not tell what the game would actually show.
- The preview was a plain text dialog, not the game's dialogue style.
- Saving a public scene rewrote the whole node with quoted keys (`{"say": "mio", ...}`), so a one-word fix showed up as a diff on every line.
- The front end was written as dense one-line code that is hard to maintain.

- **Scene list.** It is the story map's outline. The author picks a day (1 to 5, 6 on, or Any day for talk menus such as the conversations), and the list shows that day's places in the order the game plays them. Under each place are the scenes that start there, public and private side by side, a private one tagged Private. Parts that nothing in their own file starts are listed last, under "Started from elsewhere". Search looks across every day. The list is filtered by All, Public game or Private.
- **Timeline.** One compact row per beat: a drag handle, the beat number, a coloured type marker, the speaker's portrait and name, and the text edited in place. Japanese and English sit together on one line, in the order the game shows them. Pictures appear in the timeline whole, at the column's width and their full height. The timeline reads as one path through the scene (Jørgen, 2026-10-10: "I'm not able to easily follow when there is branching, like look vs leave. possible to select in the editor, and it "continues" below?"). A choice shows its options as a switch. The picked option's beats follow below it: an option that goes on to another part of the scene brings that part's beats in under an "After “Look”" band, and switching the pick swaps them. An "if" shows only the side the path takes. When a pick above decides it, the beat says which pick ("Because you picked …"). When nothing does, a Yes/No switch picks the side, and a switch for who plays sits in the path bar at the top, next to the picks so far. A line marks where the two sides of an "if" meet again. Beats in a part a choice leads to are edited, dragged and added like any other, and Save writes each changed part on its own. Preview plays the same path. Play in game passes who plays and the switched flags to a private scene's viewer; the choices stay the player's to make. A "+" between rows adds narration, a line, a choice or, in a private scene, a picture there. A new picture beat says "No picture yet" until one is composed for it or picked; Save refuses a picture beat with no picture. Rows drag to a new position, and Alt+↑ or Alt+↓ moves the selected row.
- **Pictures of a scene.** "This scene" in the Picture tab and the composer's library lists the pictures the scene already has and their alternates, worked out from what exists, with no records changed. That covers pictures made for it in the composer, pictures picked for it, the round folder each live picture was copied from (found by an identical file under rewards/), and rounds named after the scene or its pictures (copier-2 for d1-copier). Jørgen saw "No pictures generated for this scene yet" on a Mio scene, because only composer pictures counted. The rules are in `scene_runs` (tools/subplots/api.py).

## Hiding the source behind forms

The author never sees code. A beat is one of narration, line, choice, picture, branch, jump, word or direction. Each one is a form built from the step objects in game3d/story/FORMAT.md. A step the editor cannot express is still shown, in plain words ("Camera moves in on Mio"), and it can be moved and deleted. It keeps its exact source text, so nothing is lost on save. The private recipe helpers (`duo`, `cw`, `pic`, `gap` and the rest) are decoded the same way by the recipe adapter.

## One adapter, revisions and undo

- Every read and write goes through `/api/subplots/*` (tools/subplots/api.py). Public story modules are read by `source.mjs`, which parses them without running them. The private recipe sources are read through the provider command that `island/private/rewards/tools/subplot-editor.json` configures. The public repo holds only the generic adapters.
- A save checks the source's revision hash, so an outside change gives a conflict and never an overwrite. The previous scene goes into Revisions first. A private save also backs up the generated files and rebuilds them. If the rebuild or a check fails, everything is restored.
- Saves are minimal edits, public and private. A save that changes nothing writes the file back byte for byte. A beat that did not change keeps its exact source text, moved beats keep theirs, and the comments and blank lines between beats stay. An edited beat changes only the words that changed (an edited recipe call keeps its helper and its quote style; an edited public step keeps its other properties, and a beat inside a branch is edited inside it). New beats are written in the house style (`'mio: text'`, unquoted keys, single quotes).
- Undo and redo work in the browser over every edit since opening, with Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z. A save can be taken back by restoring the revision before it and saving again. That is one click in Revisions.

## Pickers from the bible

Speakers come from docs/game/cast.md, with portraits from game3d/js/ui/portrait-data.js and colours from narrative/speakers.js. Places come from places/definitions.js and docs/game/places.md. Times of day come from sim.js. Flags and their values are collected from every story condition. Words come from lang.js. Deliveries are the voice direction tags. All of them come from `/api/subplots/authoring`, which caches until a source changes. Free text is only for the words people say, narration, and choice labels. A value that is not in the bible yet (a new flag) can be typed in and gets a "new" marker.

## Checks on save

| Check | Public | Private |
|---|---|---|
| Parse and syntax of the written file | yes | yes |
| Generator rebuild (`*.src.mjs` writes sequences, plugins, line lists) | none | yes, restored on failure |
| The story checks (`story-check.mjs` and the day checks: unknown ids, missing nodes, bad conditions, missing deliveries) | yes, the save is undone when it finds something new | none |
| Reachability (`normal-entry-check`, then `normal-reach-browser` on request) | none | entry check now, browser on request |
| Blur check | none | yes |
| Voice manifest (changed lines go back to "needs voice", stale clips retired) | yes: the manifest is rewritten and lines with no clip are marked; old clips stay, keyed by their old text | yes |

A failed check shows in plain words next to the beat it concerns, in red ("Pick a delivery: on this day every line says how it is spoken"); one no beat matches goes in the status line. A saved line with no voice clip says so under it, and the status line counts them.

## Stages

Each stage ships on its own.

1. **Core editing loop (done).** Open any scene, public or private, labelled. Edit narration and lines inline, add beats, change the speaker from a picker, and drag beats into a new order. See each picture in place and replace it from the image gen history. Undo and redo. Save with a revision. Preview in the game's dialogue style, or Play in game in one click. Minimal public saves.
2. **Pictures (done 2026-10-11; Versions of a shot and Checks on save above).** Regenerate a picture from its own settings, edit and inpaint (mask a region and repaint it through the dashboard's repaint path), compare versions side by side, and add a picture beat with a new composition. GPU work goes only through the image gen queue, with ComfyUI's VRAM capped so the browser keeps headroom. story-check and the voice manifest run on public saves.
3. **Story map and placement (done 2026-10-11; private scenes from templates are #495).** A map of scenes per area and day with the routes between them (talk, event, choice and jump). Drag a scene's entry onto a place and time. Restrictions (day, period, flags, once only) become one sentence that reads like English. New scenes are made from a template. It covers the scene list as the map's outline (Layout, Scene list), when a scene plays as one sentence with pickers (Layout, When it plays), the map board with drag to a time, place or day (Layout, Story map), and new public scenes from templates (Layout, New scene). A placement made on the map was proved in a normal run on 2026-10-11: day 3's mailbox scene dragged to After work and saved, then `placement-reach.mjs` started it by tapping the mailboxes in the evening and not in the morning (tools/subplots/README.md); the change was not kept. A scene made from the short exchange template (Mori at the gym, day 3) started the same way in the afternoon, when he is there.
4. **Video.** Turn a picture into a short loop (image to video) on the local queue, preview it in place, and promote it through `tools/assets/live.mjs`.
5. **Polish.** Compare any two revisions side by side, comments for Jørgen on beats, and a whole-scene reading mode for writers.

### Appearance and clothing in one shot

The People frame exposes the actual clothing and appearance prompt words. A clothing preset supplies a starting point. The author can describe an altered garment directly, or change a hairstyle for the current shot. Appearance starts with the complete template description, including identifying details and build, so an old hairstyle cannot remain hidden in another template field. Changing either field affects only that shot. Reset buttons restore the clothing preset or appearance template. Exclusions are editable underneath the same person. “Edit shared template…” explicitly opens the template used by other scenes.

A local appearance replaces the group's short description too. Local words, including deliberately empty fields, survive saving, reopening and reusing generation settings. Older shots keep their existing template behaviour until edited.

### Other prompt templates and shot mode

Setting, light, pose, camera, framing, style and exclusion presets each have a “words in this shot” disclosure beside their controls. These fields start from the selected template and can be adapted or emptied for one picture. Reset to template restores the current template words. Shared edits are explicitly labelled “Edit shared template”. Switching presets keeps each preset’s local edits for this shot. The scene direction is already local text, with a scene template as its starting point.

The composer header’s Nonadult and Adult buttons change the current shot’s standing exclusion setting. They use the same saved flag as the former Hard scene checkbox. Nonadult applies the standing soft-scene exclusions at Mature and Adult content levels, while Adult leaves those standing words out. Every picture of a private scene is a reward (pictures belong to the private scenes): while its shot is Nonadult, the soft preset (the negative marked soft) is on and locked in Exclude at every content level, and Adult leaves it out. The default exclusion presets are locked on for a reward in both modes. Each section with prompt words (What happens, Pose, Setting, Light, Camera, More details) has an Avoid line under it for words to keep out of that part of the picture; they join the exclusion prompt after the people's exclusions, in that order, and never take out a locked preset. Other exclusion words, including words in a copied prompt, stay unchanged. The generated exclusion prompt updates immediately under Exclude. These shot buttons do not switch the browser’s library mode.

Place previews use the shot’s adapted setting, light, style and framing. Changing those words gives the preview a different identity, so a previous template preview cannot stand in for the adapted place. Shared template examples remain unchanged.

The picture library lists individual pictures newest first, including pictures from the same batch and jobs that have just finished. Loading older pictures continues that order without skipping pictures made at the same time. The lightbox follows the same order as the grid.
