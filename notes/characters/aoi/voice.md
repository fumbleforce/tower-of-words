Who Aoi is lives in her [bio](../../character-backgrounds/aoi.md), approved in Review character-backgrounds-5, and in [cast.md](../../../docs/game/cast.md#aoi-aoi). She is the woman Eric heard on the train telling a friend that her assignment could be anywhere but the basement. Her tennis route is in [cast.md](../../../docs/game/cast.md#aoi-choosing-an-assignment-she-can-stand-behind). The general dialogue rules are in [VOICE.md](../../../game3d/story/VOICE.md). This page is how she sounds, written against the approved bio. The example lines below are not in the game yet.

## How she talks

Aoi speaks only Japanese. She has no English beyond the loanwords everyone uses (テニス, ラケット, ナイス, サービス), so the player follows her through the words Eric has learned, her pointing and miming, and what happens next. When she sees he hasn't followed, she says it again more slowly and points at the thing. She doesn't switch to English, and she doesn't turn every misunderstanding into a lesson.

She talks fast and a little loud, the way she did on the phone on the train. She grew up the youngest of three, where you got your sentence out before your brother and sister had decided for you, and she still talks as if someone might cut in. With friends and people her own age she is casual and quick: 〜の！, 〜じゃん, えー, うそ, ほんと？, でしょ. With Eric at first she uses です and ます, because he is thirteen years older and a stranger, but it's light polite speech with the corners rounded off: 〜ちゃいました, 〜ですよね？, 〜しません？. With seniors at work and the club's older members her requests get short and careful (〜てもいいですか), and she says them too quickly to get them over with.

Her fillers are あ, えっと and あれ？. Almost every line she starts on impulse begins with あ. She says things out loud before she has decided whether she should, then covers them with a laugh or a すみません and carries on. She calls herself 私 and calls Eric {mc.name_jp}さん.

Half a year at a karaoke front desk left her with counter Japanese for anything about rooms and times: 空いてます, 何時からですか, 一時間でよろしいですか. She slips into it when she talks about bookings, catches herself and laughs. It is also the one place she sounds sure of herself, because she liked that job and was good at it.

She is cheeky. She winks, borrows things and keeps them longer than she should, and makes small jokes at the other person's expense, mostly about B2 being in the basement. The jokes are about something that just happened, and she lets Eric make one back. Older colleagues sometimes miss the wink and take the joke straight. When she sees that, she adds a quick 冗談です and laughs, and she doesn't apologise any further.

## What she wants and avoids

She wants to be trusted with a booking that people actually use, and to play tennis rather than carry the ball basket. She carried it for four years in her university circle, where she also booked the restaurants for the drinks, so she knows how a club works and what everything is called without having played much. She wanted a room with a window, and she got one upstairs. She likes being the one who knows where the courts are, what time the pool opens and which slot is free. She means to run the bookings for the island's big events one day, and when that comes up she talks about the summer festival stage as if it were already hers.

She avoids the basement, being handed the basket or a form because she is the newest, and asking a basic question where her seniors can hear it. When she has to ask one, she rehearses it under her breath first, starts with あの、すみません, and then says it too fast. Sometimes she has to ask again, and then she tells Eric about it afterwards with a laugh.

## Under stress

- She rehearses the sentence she has to say, sometimes to Eric, as if checking it with him.
- She corrects herself halfway: 「三時から……あ、じゃなくて、三時半から」.
- She talks faster and a little higher, and then stops dead and laughs at herself.
- When she is caught out, as with the phone call, she is embarrassed for one line and then changes the subject to something she can do, such as the pool tonight or the shoes she has to buy.
- When something she did goes wrong, such as a booking that clashes, she fixes it quietly on her own side and says nothing. If Eric asks straight out, she admits it with a laugh, and she doesn't make a confession of it.
- When someone asks for a thing she borrowed, she is honestly surprised for a second, then hands it over.

## Eric at the start and later

At first Eric is the man from the train who heard her phone call and works in the basement she was so glad to avoid. She is embarrassed for a moment, gives him her name and moves on quickly. She treats him as a polite stranger who is older than her, and she is pleased he can't follow her Japanese yet, because it means he missed most of the call. She points things out to him (the pool poster, the courts on the map) because she has just learned them herself and likes knowing.

After they have played together she calls him over first and keeps the racket between turns as if it settles who her partner is. Later she drops to casual Japanese with him, still with さん on his name, and she teases him about his serve and about B2. She tells him about bookings she made herself, and she asks him to stay beside her when the experienced players want the whole court. Further on she invites him to something because she wants to go, with no errand attached.

## Example lines

The English in brackets is for whoever reads this sheet. In the game her Japanese is overheard, blurred except for the words Eric has learned and the loanwords.

1. Day 3, the plaza board. Eric admits he didn't follow a word of her phone call.
   「よかった……。じゃあ、聞かなかったことにしてください。」 She winks. (Phew. Then let's say you didn't hear it.)
2. Lunch on the plaza. She holds up her new staff card.
   「施設課です。会議室の予約、全部私なんです。……まだ三つしか取ってないけど。」 (Facilities. All the meeting-room bookings are mine. ...I've only made three so far.)
3. On court, a member hands her the ball basket again.
   「あ、また私ですか。大学の四年間、ずっとこれ運んでたんですよ。」 (Oh, me again? I carried one of these for four years at university.)
4. Outside the training centre, before she asks her senior something. She says it to Eric first, quietly.
   「えっと、『三時から空いてますか』……で、いいですよね？」 (Um, "Is it free from three?"... That's right, isn't it?)
5. Eric asks whether a meeting room is free.
   「三番、空いてます。お時間は……あ、すみません。前のバイトの癖です。」 (Room three's free. And how long would you... Oh, sorry. Habit from my old job.)
6. Eric asks why she didn't want the basement.
   「だって、窓がないと、お昼かどうかも分からないじゃないですか。」 (Well, without a window you can't even tell when it's lunchtime.)
7. Later, after they have played together a few times. Casual now.
   「日曜、来るよね？ ラケット、まだ返してないから、一本余ってるよ。」 (You're coming on Sunday, right? I still haven't given the racket back, so there's a spare.)
8. A club member asks for the racket back.
   「え、返すんですか？ ……あ、そっか、借りてたんだ。」 (Huh, I have to give it back? ...Oh, right, I borrowed it.)
9. The experienced players want the whole booking.
   「最初の三十分は、初心者の時間です。終わったら、ちゃんと呼びますから。」 (The first thirty minutes are for beginners. I'll call you over when we're done, I promise.)

## Lines she would not say

- 「がんばりまーす！ よろしくお願いしまーす！」 This is the bubbly mascot voice Jørgen dislikes. Her energy shows in how fast she talks and in her jokes.
- "Sorry, I'll say it in English for you." She has no English, and she gets her meaning across by pointing and saying it again.
- 「私なんて、どうせ新人だから……」 (I'm only the new one anyway...) She never says what she is afraid of. She rehearses the question instead, or asks it too fast.
- 「末っ子だから、いつも後回しなんです。」 (I'm the youngest, so I always come last.) She doesn't explain herself through her family. Her brother and sister can come up in a story about something that happened, never as the reason for how she is.
