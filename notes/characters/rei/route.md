# Rei's relationship line

A plan for issue #357. It replaces the tennis plan in [cast.md](../../../docs/game/cast.md#rei-playing-a-game-she-could-lose), which was written before her current personality. Who she is lives in her [bio](../../character-backgrounds/rei.md) and how she talks in her [voice sheet](voice.md). How points and steps work is in [systems.md](../../../docs/game/systems.md#bonds). Kisses, dates and sex belong only on this line, gated on her step, never on a day. Her private scene plugs into step 2 and is written separately.

Rei wants a doubles partner who does what she says and is good enough to win, and somebody around to boss. She never admits a weakness or a mistake. Eric gets close to her by turning up on Sunday, standing where she tells him, calling her lines honestly, and not mentioning her ankle. She softens slowly, and it shows as attention, never as an apology. Her line runs from the tennis court to the Sales floor and ends at her own dining table. She leads it, so it moves on her schedule: she pulls Eric into her week with jobs, and each step sets up the next.

## How fast it moves

Jørgen, 2026-10-10, on the first plan: "progression is too slow when she is the one in control". With Sunday tennis as the only meeting and the cap of 3 points a day, the first plan needed about eight Sundays to reach step 4. Three changes make it about three weeks from her introduction to step 5:

- From step 1 her team's repair requests come to Eric by name, because she has decided he is the one who does them. They give the usual 2 points on weekdays, so Eric earns with her during the week as well as on Sunday.
- Her gates are lower than everyone else's: 4 points for step 2, 10 for step 3 and 16 for step 4 ([systems.md](../../../docs/game/systems.md#bonds)).
- Each step ends with her telling him when the next thing is. Step 3 ends with the date of the club match, and step 4 ends with her sending him to her place that same evening, so step 5 follows straight on.

## Step 1: the new foreigner from B2

Earned on day 4 at the tennis club, when she introduces herself and tells him and Aoi to take a spare racket each. She switches to English because she won't wait for his Japanese. She remembers whether he watched her serve or hit with Aoi. Before she leaves she hands him her team's repairs: "My team's laptops and printers are yours now. I'm putting your name on our requests, so check your list in the morning and come the same day."

## Step 2: she calls on him first

She reaches it at 4 points. Points come from her team's repair requests, Sunday tennis, doing a drill her way and polite Japanese. Joking with her earns nothing, because she doesn't follow the joke.

The scene is the built Sunday one, rewritten, and it plays at the first Sunday after he reaches 4 points, usually the Sunday after they meet. She doesn't offer to keep the explanation shorter. She tells him to serve again and throw the ball higher, counts his serves out loud and tells him which of them were in. From now on she calls on him before anyone else at the court.

**Private:** her terrace scene hangs on this step. It has no romance in it and is written separately.

## Step 3: the drill

At 10 points, the built doubles drill plays at Sunday tennis, rewritten. She puts him on the deuce side, then crosses into his half and takes a ball that was his. Eric can call "mine" on the next ball and stand his ground, or let her take it.

If he stands his ground, she makes it sound like her plan all along: "That's why I put you on that side. Now hit it properly, deep and at her feet." The next Sunday she runs the drill with his half left alone and doesn't mention it. If he lets her take it, she wins the point and tells him he was standing in the wrong place: "You were on the middle line again, so I took it. Stand out by the alley, where I put you." Both complete the step, and she remembers which one he did.

This replaces the built version. Its line where she admits she moved too soon is cut, along with every other line in it where she apologises or offers.

Before he leaves the court she gives him his next two jobs: "You're calling the lines at the match next Sunday, because the people here all call for their friends. And on Tuesday my biggest customer is coming in, so set up the laptop in our meeting room by ten and check that the projector works with it." The Tuesday job is a repair request, so it gives points that week.

The Japanese focus is court talk: the line calls イン and アウト, which the player reads in katakana, and もう一度, which she says to herself before a serve and the player types to ask for the ball again.

## Step 4: match point

At 16 points, at the Sunday club match against her old classmate's pair, the Sunday after step 3. Eric calls the lines from the bench, which she trusts nobody else with. Late in the set her right ankle goes. She says 大丈夫です before anyone asks and moves her weight onto her left foot.

Then her own shot lands close to the opponents' baseline. Eric can call it out, which is honest and costs her the game, or let it go. Either way she plays on and finishes the set. After the match she goes through it point by point and never praises him. If he called it, she states it as a fact (「あそこはアウト。ボール一個分。」) and moves straight on to her partner's mistakes. If he let it go, she tells him so: 「あのボール、アウトだったでしょ。次は、私のボールでもアウトって言いなさい。」 (That ball was out, wasn't it. Next time, call it out even when it's my ball.) Calling it honestly is what she wanted from him.

Then she gives Eric a nickname, which is how she marks him as one of hers. The writer of the scene chooses it, and one suggestion is ビーツー (B2), after his team, because "the new one from B2" is what she has called him from the start. Before she leaves the bench she tells him to come to her place that evening: "My printer at home has been broken for a week, and I've got customer files to print for Monday. Come round at seven and fix it. I'll send you the address."

The Japanese focus is her count of games. Points in Japanese tennis are called in katakana English (フィフティーン, サーティ, デュース), but she keeps the game count out loud in Japanese numbers (ゴー・ヨン), and the player follows the set by it.

## Step 5: a second corner of the table

Step 4 and this scene, the same evening. Her place is by the sports grounds. Her dining table is covered in customer files, with one corner cleared for her plate and a crime novel open near the end. Eric can fix the printer quietly or talk while she reads. If he talks, she tells him to wait until she has finished the chapter. Then she clears a second corner of the table for him without saying anything, and puts two plates on it.

This is where the friendship-or-romance choice falls. She tells him to keep Saturday free (「土曜、空けといて。」) and says she will tell him where they are going. Eric can answer as her regular partner, for tennis and errands, or ask her plainly whether that is a date. She doesn't look up from the plates, and she is short with him for asking: 「デートに決まってるでしょ。七時に寮の前で待ってて。遅れないでよ。どこに行くかは、そのとき言うから。」 Either answer completes the step. The answer is saved in a new flag, `romance_rei`, which only this choice sets. On the date path she still decides where they go and still gives the orders, and the warmth shows in what she tells him about her work. On the friendship path she keeps him as her doubles partner and the one she sends on errands, and she tells him why as well as what, now and then.

The Japanese focus is plain Japanese orders: 空けといて, 待ってて and 遅れないで, the forms she has used on her team all along and now uses on him.

No private scene hangs on steps 3 to 5 yet.

## After step 5

She still never admits a mistake. When she has got something wrong, she changes it the next week and is shorter with him if he brings it up. She still gets angry when he expects an equal say, and she still calls on him first.
