# Rei: voice

Who Rei is lives in her [bio](../../character-backgrounds/rei.md) and [cast.md](../../../docs/game/cast.md#rei-rei): forty-one, Sales, grew up on Amakawa, plays club tennis by the sports grounds. This page is how she talks. The general rules are in [VOICE.md](../../../game3d/story/VOICE.md).

## How she talks

Rei speaks in whole sentences, usually two at a time: the first says what she has decided and the second tells you what to do about it. She is brusque because she leaves out the cushioning. Her sentences are still complete. "You can stand by the line and tell me if these go in" is her. "Line. Watch it." is not.

She often asks a question and answers it before you can ("Can you see the T from there? You can, you're standing on it."). Once she thinks she knows where your sentence is going, she finishes it for you, and sometimes she gets it wrong and carries on anyway. She counts everything out loud: serves in, days until an order ships, how many times you've asked. Her lines get long only after a loss, when she walks through the whole match to show whose fault it was.

**Japanese.** Polite です/ます with strangers, customers and seniors, but without the softeners: 〜てください where others would say 〜ていただけますか, and no ちょっと or もしよければ in front. With her Sales juniors she switches to plain 〜て and 〜といて. With the classmates she grew up with she is casual and uses their old nicknames, even in a meeting. On court she talks to herself in Japanese, counting and saying もう一度 before the next serve.

**English.** Conversational and practical, learned on overseas customers. Short complete sentences joined with "and" and "so", no idioms. When she loses a word she uses a plainer one or shows you. Her tennis words (the T, the alley, the deuce side) run ahead of Eric, and she assumes he knows them. She phrases her orders as "You can...", so they sound like offers. When someone makes a joke she doesn't follow, she goes flat and steers back to the subject.

**Orders and criticism.** She says what went wrong and what to do next in the same breath, with the facts: "You were on the middle line again. I said take the alley." She doesn't ask whether you're willing to help. She assumes it and starts explaining the job.

**She never says** "would you mind", "if it's no trouble", "sorry, take your time", or "I was wrong" while the thing is still going on. She never uses Mio's internet fillers ("honestly", "basically", "like"), sales patter, or anything mysterious.

## Wants and avoids

She wants the management job, a doubles partner who is good enough to keep up, and her customers left in her hands. She avoids holidays, stopping a match for her right ankle, admitting a plan was wrong in front of the people she made follow it, and anyone who interrupts the last chapters of a crime novel.

## Under stress

- When her ankle hurts, she says 大丈夫です before anyone asks and moves her weight to her left foot. She walks to the ball basket slowly and pretends to be choosing balls.
- When her plan is failing, she gives her reasons for keeping it and gets more detailed about what everyone else should do differently.
- When she loses, she spends the evening explaining the match point by point and names whoever was at fault.
- When she is out of her depth in English, her sentences get shorter and she starts pointing.

## Eric, at the start and later

At first Eric is the new foreigner on B2 and a pair of hands. She uses him without asking, switches to English because she won't wait for his Japanese, and brushes off anything he learned in Norway. His dry jokes fall flat with her and she says so.

Later she tells him why as well as what. She trusts him with something she would normally keep, such as setting up the laptop for a customer visit or calling the lines on match point. She may give him a nickname, which is how she treats the people she counts as hers. She still argues when he expects an equal say on court. If she admits a mistake, it is to him alone, days later, and in passing.

## Example lines

1. Sales floor. A junior hesitates before calling a customer back. 「考えてる時間があるなら、先に電話して。断られたら、そのとき考えればいいでしょ。」 (If you have time to think, call first. If they say no, you can think then.)
2. A meeting. An old classmate, now in Logistics, presents the delivery numbers. 「タコちゃん、その数字、先週のと違うよね。」 (Tako-chan, those numbers are different from last week's, aren't they?) He hates the nickname.
3. Eric suggests a fix from his old job. "That probably worked in Norway. This customer has been ordering the same valves since 1998, and he wants the same person to call him about them."
4. An overseas customer makes a joke on a video call. "I don't understand the joke. Can we go back to the delivery date?"
5. Club doubles, Eric drifts to the middle. "You were standing on the middle line again. I told you to take the alley, so take the alley. If they hit it down the middle, that one's mine."
6. Her partner wants to stop because she's limping. 「大丈夫です。」 Then, to Eric, "It always does that. We're one game from the set, so you can serve."
7. Her apartment, Eric knocks while she's reading. "I've got eleven pages left and I already know who did it. Can it wait eleven pages?"
8. Weeks later, on the sea terrace. "The new toss was a mistake. I kept it for two weeks because I'd told the whole club about it. Don't repeat that on Sunday."

## Lines she would not say

- "Tennis is a war. Pick a side." She explains things practically. This is the clipped, quotable line the game bans, and she doesn't think in slogans.
- "Sorry, sorry, I always rush people! Take your time." She doesn't apologise for her pace. If she slows down, she just does it and keeps talking.
- "You were right all along, my plan was wrong." She doesn't concede while the match is going. The furthest she goes in the moment is "Fine, we'll try yours," and she will mention the score if it fails.
