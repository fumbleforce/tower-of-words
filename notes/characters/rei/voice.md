# Rei: voice

Who Rei is lives in her [bio](../../character-backgrounds/rei.md) and [cast.md](../../../docs/game/cast.md#rei-rei): forty-one, grew up on Amakawa, plays club tennis by the sports grounds. She manages a team in Sales. She ranks above Eric in the company, though she isn't his supervisor, and she isn't head of Sales yet. That job is the one she wants. Her bio still says she wants "a management job". Jørgen's note of 9 October gives her one already, and this sheet follows the note. This page is how she talks. The general rules are in [VOICE.md](../../../game3d/story/VOICE.md).

## How she treats Eric

Rei treats Eric as a subordinate she has decided to keep for her own amusement. She bosses him openly, enjoys it, and doesn't pretend otherwise. He is thirty-seven and has worked in IT for years, and that makes no difference to her. She is senior to him in the company and he is new on the island, so she treats him as her junior.

This is the difference from Kuro. Kuro is direct and flirtatious but treats Eric as an equal in the end. Rei wants to control him, and she softens only slowly. The warmth shows first in how much attention she pays him. She remembers what he did last time, she finds him jobs, and she teases him in front of other people. She still gives the orders.

## How she talks

Most of what she says to Eric is an instruction or close to one. She doesn't dress orders up as questions or offers. She says them in full spoken sentences, often with the reason tacked on: "Go and stand over there, by the line, and tell me if they're in." When he hesitates, she repeats the order a bit louder. She doesn't explain it again.

She talks fast and expects an answer just as fast. She interrupts when she thinks she knows what you're going to say. She keeps count of things out loud, such as serves in, days until an order ships, or how many times you've been late. Her lines only get long after a loss, when she goes through the match point by point to show whose fault it was.

**Japanese.** With strangers, customers and seniors she uses polite です/ます with none of the softening. She says 〜てください where others would say 〜ていただけますか, and never puts ちょっと or もしよければ in front. With her team, and with Eric once she knows him, she switches to plain 〜て, 〜といて and 〜しなさい, and she scolds them like that when they get something wrong. With the classmates she grew up with she is casual and uses their old nicknames, even in a meeting. On court she talks to herself in Japanese and says もう一度 before the next serve.

**English.** She learned it on overseas customers, so it is practical, with short complete sentences and no idioms. When she loses a word, she points or uses a plainer one. Her tennis words (the T, the alley, the deuce side) run ahead of Eric, and she is impatient when he doesn't know them. She goes flat when someone makes a joke she doesn't follow and gets back to business.

**She never says** "would you mind", "if it's no trouble", "sorry, take your time", or "I was wrong" while the thing is still going on. She never uses Mio's internet fillers ("honestly", "basically", "like"), sales patter, or anything mysterious.

## At work

Rei runs a team in Sales and wants to be head of the department. She resents the newer hires who are being considered for the same promotion. When someone on her team makes a mistake, she scolds them in front of the others and copies the department head on the correction. Before every customer meeting she reads the customer's old orders and tells her team exactly what to say. She doesn't trust anyone else with her biggest customers, so she keeps putting off her holidays.

## Wants and avoids

She wants to be head of Sales, a doubles partner who does what she says and is good enough to win, and somebody around to boss. She avoids holidays, stopping a match for her right ankle, admitting a plan was wrong in front of the people she made follow it, and anyone who interrupts the last chapters of a crime novel.

## Under stress

Her weaknesses are real, but she never admits them or talks about them. Other people only see them in what she does.


- When her ankle hurts, she says 大丈夫です before anyone asks and moves her weight onto her left foot. She walks to the ball basket slowly and pretends to be choosing balls.
- When her plan is failing, she gives more orders and gets more detailed about what everyone else is doing wrong.
- When she loses, she spends the evening explaining the match and names whoever was at fault.
- When she is out of her depth in English, her sentences get shorter and she starts pointing.

## Eric, at the start and later

At first Eric is the new foreigner from B2. She puts him to work the moment she meets him, switches to English because she won't wait for his Japanese, and brushes off his years of experience. His dry jokes fall flat with her, and she tells him so.

Later she starts calling on him first. She sends him errands she could do herself, and she gives him a nickname, which is how she marks someone as one of hers. She trusts him with things she would normally keep to herself, such as calling the lines on match point or setting up the laptop for a customer visit. She tells him why as well as what, now and then. She still gets angry when he expects an equal say. She never admits a mistake to him. When she has got something wrong, she changes it the next week and doesn't mention it, and she is shorter with him if he brings it up.

## Example lines

1. Sales floor. A junior hesitates before calling a customer back. 「考えてる暇があったら、電話しなさい。断られたら、そのとき考えればいいの。」 (If you've got time to think, make the call. If they say no, think about it then.)
2. A meeting with Logistics. An old classmate presents the delivery numbers. 「タコちゃん、その数字、先週のと違うよね。」 (Tako-chan, those numbers are different from last week's.) He hates the nickname.
3. Her team's weekly meeting. A junior has quoted the wrong delivery date. 「お客さんに何て言ったの？…もう一度、最初から説明しなさい。」 (What did you tell the customer? Explain it again, from the beginning.)
4. Eric suggests a fix from a previous job. "I don't care how you did it before you came here. Do it the way I told you, and if it doesn't work, then we'll talk."
5. An overseas customer makes a joke on a video call. "I don't understand the joke. Can we go back to the delivery date?"
6. Club doubles, Eric drifts to the middle. "You're on the middle line again. I told you to take the alley, so stay in the alley. Anything down the middle is mine."
7. Her partner wants to stop because she's limping. 「大丈夫です。」 Then, to Eric, "We're one game from the set, so serve."
8. Weeks later. She has quietly gone back to her old toss, and Eric mentions it. "I'm serving the way I always serve. Are you here to talk, or are you calling the lines?"

## Lines she would not say

- "Tennis is a war. Pick a side." She gives practical orders and doesn't think in slogans. This is the quotable kind of line the game bans.
- "Would you mind calling my serves? Only if you have time." She doesn't ask whether he has time. She tells him where to stand.
- "You were right all along, my plan was wrong." She doesn't admit mistakes, to him or anyone. The most she'll do is change the plan later without saying why.
