## The shape of these days

Eric begins to recognise people on his own routes. The office supplies work, the board supplies invitations, and a familiar face gives him a reason to stop somewhere. Each day offers one major scene and two small repair requests. A direct route through the major scene and one small job should fit a short play session; the rest is available for detours and later visits.

Day 1 is Thursday 1 October, so these are Saturday 3 October, Sunday 4 October and Monday 5 October. The weekend introduces freedom naturally. Nobody expects the whole B2 team at their desks on Saturday or Sunday. Working then is Eric's choice; requesters are at the place named in their ticket during its stated availability. That availability recurs and never becomes a deadline.

“Day 3”, “day 4” and “day 5” below mean the earliest offer. Sleeping advances the calendar without closing work or consuming an unplayed scene. The station confirmation can wait; a missed club introduction is offered at that club's next meeting. If several major scenes have accumulated, the player chooses one for the day and the others remain pending. Brief greetings and ticket responses fit around it. Leave room for #226's optional relationship scene without filling it here.

Use the existing periods for morning, lunch, afternoon and evening. Browsing the PC or board, walking, and talking briefly keep the current period. Choosing to spend a period on work, a club session or free time advances it, with the cost stated before starting. Going into room 203 during the day is safe; ending the day becomes an explicit sleep choice. This needs changing from day 2's automatic homecoming ending.

## Day 3: a job Eric can finish, an evening he can choose

### Routine and freedom

Morning starts in room 203 with the ticket list available and the station's confirmation ready. The player can go there, explore, or leave the work for later. B2 remains accessible for its computer, with weekend attendance written for each person rather than copied from Friday.

The recommended work route is the station confirmation below, then either small ticket. Lunch and afternoon are free. On the first plaza visit, Aoi is comparing the club posters. She points out the meeting place and takes her own slip while Eric chooses whether to take one. She has somewhere to go; she does not lead a tour of all four clubs.

The board is the day's new interaction. All four posters are readable from this first visit, each showing the next date, period and place in English with supported Japanese. Taking a slip joins that club immediately. Eric can join several, or none, without fees, prerequisites or a repair favour. Reading another poster later uses the same interaction. Aoi's introduction remains available if the morning was spent elsewhere; membership also works without meeting her.

Evening offers the swimming club's first social meeting in the gym, a walk, or home. Its short arrival scene puts Eric beside someone who is sorting borrowed equipment; he can help put it away, stay to talk, or leave. This first meeting is deliberately small while the station carries the day's major scene. The club's personal threads wait for #226.

### Major scene: signing off the station doors

Place: the empty, stationary carriage and its doorway. Cast: Eric and Mr. Ishibashi. Trigger: the day-2 report has been submitted and Eric chooses the confirmation visit. The guard explains that the station needs a witnessed check to accept the report; he wants to get the train back into normal use.

1. Show which door is being checked, the guard's portable test control and the empty threshold. The report determines the setup. If Eric requested a replacement, the replacement is now fitted by station maintenance. If he reported a pass, the original sensor remains. Neither branch accuses him of lying or assumes Mio accompanied yesterday's check.
2. Eric starts one ordinary open-and-close cycle. The guard checks the sensor and accepts it. Then, while Eric is still looking at the door, it begins to close before the guard has collected the test control.
3. Eric can use the already-known `matte` to hold it, with the established visible and audible effect, or press the ordinary hold button the guard points out. The guard retrieves the control and completes the check in either case. Nobody is trapped, and there is no timed input.
4. The station accepts the repair and closes T-0002, paying its existing ¥5,000 once. The guard notices which method Eric chose, then returns to work. He does not launch an investigation or explain the magic.

The ordinary controls make the visit understandable even on a history where Eric has kept the magic quiet. The magical choice lets him decide whether to use it in front of this person. The scene ends at sign-off; the walk to the plaza is under player control.

### Routine and freedom

The day's major scene takes place during that session. A player who skips it still has a complete free Sunday and can meet the club on its next Sunday. Other encounters here stay brief so there is room for one personal scene from #226.

### Major scene: the other side of the net

Place: the tennis court and its adjoining bench, with no scene change. Proposed participants: Eric, Rei and Aoi, plus background members. Their presence here is a casting proposal for #226, not a settled membership fact.

Rei wants to get back to a proper doubles game. Aoi has come to play but keeps being handed the ball basket because she is new. Eric arrives as another beginner just as the experienced pair call Rei back onto court.

1. Establish the two occupied sides of the net and Aoi at the basket before anyone asks Eric to choose. Rei offers him a spare racket. Aoi asks whether he will hit with her; she wants an actual partner, not reassurance about being new.
2. Eric chooses to pair with Aoi for a short practice rally or asks Rei to show him a serve. Both choices are spoken, concrete requests. A short staged exchange of balls follows; there is no aiming, scoring or reaction test.
3. With Aoi, their first ball escapes under the fence and she gets the next one herself, leaving Eric the racket. With Rei, she starts explaining the serve, notices she has used three different terms he cannot follow, and demonstrates once instead. Aoi then asks for her own turn with the racket.
4. They sit down for a drink or Eric says goodbye. The next meeting is mentioned in the ordinary conversation. A later greeting can recall his choice; neither answer earns a special bond step or shuts the other person out.

The turn is that Eric and Aoi get to participate. Rei has her own game to return to, and the club continues when Eric walks away. If this is his first meeting with Rei or Aoi, the opening supplies their names without assuming he found their day-2 encounters.

## Integration and pacing handoff

Build sequentially after Claude's cold read: day 3's board and sports access first, day 4's court and session next, then day 5's two small interiors and Kotodama entrance. Finish and check each playable route before expanding the next. This outline commissions no art or voices.

For tickets, add the two requests on their first eligible day, preserve unread and open work, and close only through the scene's stated check. The existing T-0002 report branch must survive into its confirmation, including older day-2 saves. Re-entering a scene or loading after payment cannot pay twice. Ticket closure can move its own story forward without controlling club membership or unrelated visits.

The board needs saved memberships, meeting dates and a next meeting after a missed session. The existing fixed day loader needs free-period choices and pending scene offers. A day that skips all work must still reach evening and sleep. Returning to a visited location must restore its people and objects without replaying introductions.

Kotodama is currently standalone. Integration needs an entry from B2, a return to the same world state, an exit after the first guided delivery and English retained for that introduction. Its internal command-count patience remains inside the game. Do not wire the older giving, comparison or wanting prototypes into these days; C-0442 identifies Kotodama as the approved game. New Japanese elsewhere is reintroduced when first encountered, since the player can defer days or take activities in another order.

For the cold read, check both station reports, an Eric who met few optional characters, a weekend with no work, joining all four clubs without attending, and taking a later scene before an earlier pending one. The major scenes must still explain who wants what and leave an ordinary way forward. Voice drafting follows VOICE.md: the guard's precision, Rei's interest in her own game, Aoi's wish to participate, and Mio's concern for the work on her desk should survive without a narrator explaining them.

The first ideas discarded were a four-club guided tour, a malfunction that strands everyone, a compulsory sports contest, a prodigy reveal and a universal welcome speech. Each would make the clubs revolve around Eric. The selected scenes give the other people an activity they continue without him.
