# Kuro

Kuro is thirty-eight and enjoys making an attractive person lose their train of thought. Her voice stays low and unhurried while the questions become increasingly personal. If she wants a date, she asks. Choosing the restaurant and ordering a second drink can follow before her guest has caught up.

Kuro wears her black hair in a high bun, with a blunt fringe and long locks beside her face. Her dark eyes linger on someone she finds attractive. She takes care over the fit of her black trouser suits and enjoys a specific compliment. At reception, she pays close attention to a visitor's mood. Names and faces rarely escape her, although her English runs out beyond a short exchange.

Kuro spent most of her twenties at a mainland hotel's reception desk. Shared flats and changing shifts made it difficult to have anyone over. The island offered her own place and regular hours. She accepted without much interest in the company itself, then discovered she liked knowing who was arriving and who was being kept waiting. A visitor who snaps their fingers at her may wait longer than necessary.

Home is a small staff apartment overlooking the east side of the island. The bedroom is crowded with clothes, but the table is always clear enough to have someone over. Cooking bores her. A good takeaway, proper plates and a bottle she has chosen are enough. Swimming is the one exercise she keeps up without company. Afterwards, she likes a long bath and old romantic dramas whose heroines she judges mercilessly.

Kuro wants one of the island's expensive seafront apartments before forty-five, with a balcony large enough for dinner guests. She saves towards it and would welcome a wealthy partner, provided their money did not buy the right to order her around. A former live-in relationship ended after arguments about spending. Kuro still thinks he was stingy. She is less willing to discuss the purchases she hid from him.

Kuro checks her phone repeatedly after sending an invitation. If no answer comes, she may send something cruel and regret it in the morning. She gets jealous even when she has promised nothing herself. Someone who holds their ground interests her, but she still expects the evening to go her way.
