# Kiyoko

Proposed in play: Kiyoko likes influence and good company, and is tired of pretending she never enjoys either for its own sake. An old friend has offered her a life with fewer obligations, just as somebody at Amakawa has finally begun asking for the judgement she most wants to give.

## Established

The `kiyoko` entry in [the bible](../../bible/facts.yaml) places her on the executive floors and establishes her approved appearance. [Art direction](../../art/PROMPTS.md) describes a woman in her fifties and uses 56 in a prompt; this set leaves exact biographical age unsettled. Her title is not established. Legacy links to Kaori or Mori are not current facts.

## Proposed history

Kiyoko began with ordinary office work and learned who made decisions because she wanted more interesting work herself. She asked for responsibility, accepted opportunities and sometimes benefited when another person hesitated. The executive floors are the result of a career she pursued. She does not need an unseen sacrifice to justify being there. Her precise title remains undecided.

She has been helpful to junior colleagues and has also remembered who was useful to her. One former assistant dislikes her. Kiyoko considers the judgement unfair but can name the afternoon on which she probably earned it: she let the assistant absorb a senior person's anger rather than interrupting a meeting that mattered to her. She later supported their transfer. That help did not settle the earlier hurt.

Outside work, she has a friend who left Amakawa years ago and runs a quite ordinary life on the mainland. They enjoy novels, long lunches and making sharp assessments of people neither will ever meet again. Kiyoko is funny in this company, particularly when telling an unflattering story about herself. She decides which part of the story is safe to make funny.

She has loved more than once and had one long partnership that ended by the other person's choice. She found being left humiliating before she found it sad. For a while she avoided mutual friends, then became tired of giving away evenings she wanted. She now attends those gatherings and sometimes enjoys them. The old partner is not an unresolved obstacle every new relationship must defeat. She can remain attracted to somebody and still dislike how they treated her.

Her house is less immaculate than visitors expect. Books accumulate beside the bed, and she has three nearly identical pairs of shoes because a comfortable one became hard to find. She likes a large breakfast when travelling and dislikes itineraries built around proving how much ground was covered.

## A possible move

The mainland friend is considering a shared house with separate living space and asks whether Kiyoko would be interested later. Kiyoko is tempted by the company, the garden and having a door nobody at work knows. At the same time, an executive has begun asking for her view before a decision is made, which is exactly the influence she has wanted.

She does not owe the story a retirement lesson. Staying could be an honest choice. So could leaving, after arranging her own future without claiming the organisation cannot survive it. The interesting pressure is that her friend's offer has a real timetable and may go ahead with somebody else. Kiyoko can lose that particular possibility through delay.

## Getting to know her

Introductions and access to the executive floors must be written before these encounters. Early personal talk can be about a novel in which Kiyoko likes the character everyone else dislikes. Let the player disagree without making the answer a test of sophistication.

A later `ikitai` question can return to a place she has mentioned visiting. A subsequent conversation reveals why she keeps returning and what the friend has offered. Her account of the former assistant belongs only after the player has heard her claim to be a good judge of people. She can reconsider that claim in one concrete case and remain proud of her work.

Her proposed voice is unhurried, with complete questions and an occasional unexpectedly blunt judgement. She rarely raises her voice. When she wants something personally, she can take longer to ask than when requesting a difficult professional favour. No relationship with Mori or Kaori is imported from their legacy mini-stories.
