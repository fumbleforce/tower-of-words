# Rei

Rei is forty-one and works in Sales. When people are unsure what to do, she usually starts telling them without waiting to be asked. She is impatient with anyone who takes too long to answer, and she argues openly with colleagues who question her instructions.

Rei has steel-grey eyes and wears her silver-grey hair in a ponytail. She dresses in a carefully fitted light suit and sits upright even when she is resting. She plays tennis with much less restraint, serving hard and complaining loudly when a match goes badly. Her right ankle still aches after long matches because of an old injury. Sometimes she tries to hide her limp so her partner will not suggest stopping.

Rei grew up on Amakawa, where her mother worked in payroll and her father maintained the lifts. After school, she joined the company in order processing and worked her way into Sales. Several former classmates still work nearby. She knows their old nicknames and sometimes uses one in a meeting, even when it embarrasses them.

Before meeting a customer, Rei reads their previous orders and prepares for the objections they usually raise. She speaks conversational English with overseas clients but can be unfriendly when they make jokes she does not understand. She wants a management job and resents having to compete for one with people who have only recently joined the company. She has worked nowhere else and often dismisses what they learned in other jobs. She also finds it difficult to trust anyone with her customers and often postpones holidays because of it.

Rei lives near the sports grounds, where she plays club tennis. She keeps a record of her match scores and studies recordings of her serve to see where she can improve. At home, she usually has work spread across her large dining table and clears a corner to eat. She also reads historical crime novels and gets annoyed if someone interrupts her near the end.

After losing a tennis match, Rei can spend the evening explaining whose fault it was. She sometimes realises that her own plan was at fault and still insists that people follow it. When she needs help, she tends to tell people what to do without asking if they are willing. She respects a capable partner but gets angry when they expect an equal say.
