# Rei

Proposed in play: Rei understands what happens after a sale because she began by following orders through fulfilment. On court she can turn that useful decisiveness into choosing a partner's shot for them.

## Established

[Cast](../../docs/game/cast.md#rei-rei): 41, Sales, native Japanese and practical conversational English used with overseas customers. Her first proper introduction belongs at tennis. [Current route](../../docs/game/cast.md#rei-playing-a-game-she-could-lose) retains her other doubles partners and work commitments.

## Proposed history

Rei began in sales administration, following an order after the exciting part of the pitch was over. She learned how a small ambiguity in a customer's request became somebody else's late afternoon. Later she moved into customer-facing sales and found she enjoyed the live negotiation: working out what mattered, proposing something concrete and hearing a decision. She knows enough about fulfilment to dislike promises that leave the delivery staff trapped.

She acquired English through repeated customer calls and visits, first by preparing likely questions and later by tolerating the embarrassment of a sentence she could not finish. Tennis vocabulary developed more quickly because she played with some of the same people. She can explain a cross-court drill confidently and still need a much simpler word for a family situation. Her language gaps should be ordinary and consistent.

A parent was good at teaching practical skills and bad at letting the learner finish slowly. Rei enjoyed the attention and became competent. She also remembers being annoyed when a half-finished job was taken out of her hands. Her tendency to do the same on court is an inherited habit she can notice in a particular moment without discovering that her whole childhood was false.

For several years Rei and a partner spent spare afternoons at inexpensive film screenings, often choosing by the poster. The partner liked sweeping adventure films; Rei claimed to prefer comedies and was usually the one still talking about the adventure on the walk home. They kept a small running argument about a film with an absurdly long escape in a rowing boat. Their relationship ended, and for a while she avoided the cinema they had used. She has started going again with a friend, sometimes alone, and still buys the salted caramel popcorn her former partner introduced her to. She occasionally misses their arguments and does not want to resume the relationship. On a date she likes hearing what the other person actually enjoyed, even if she thought it was terrible.

Her closest existing tennis friendship is with a doubles partner who likes to attempt improbable shots. Rei complains, adjusts her position and enjoys the match. She would be bored playing only with someone who followed instructions perfectly.

## What this gives play

She can identify the actual decision inside a vague conversation and help someone make it. She can also mistake hesitation for a request to decide on their behalf. She respects preparation, but enjoys improvisation more than she admits at work. Her independent goals are to improve her weaker return and keep a regular non-work match on the calendar.

Incidental tells: checking the court rather than a person's expression after a bad bounce; admitting that a customer had a better suggestion; keeping an inexpensive racket grip she already likes instead of replacing it because somebody else has a new one. Her possessions do not all have to signify status.

## Discoveries across play

1. Her existing `mouichido` connection supports another rally. Once the player has seen her weaker return, she can say why she wants that shot repeated rather than presenting herself as a perfect coach.
2. When a work conversation naturally reaches deliveries, she can mention her first desk job. If the player has met Hamada, a neutral question about an expense form may connect their professional competence; neither shares customer or payroll details.
3. After the player has watched her with another partner, she can tell how that friendship began. An `isshoni` invitation refers to a match actually discussed, not an assumed exclusive partnership.
4. A terrace conversation about a film she saw can lead, when she chooses, to the old cinema afternoons. A later greeting may ask what she went to see next. She can defend the ridiculous rowing-boat film with more energy than she gives its actual recommendation; the player can disagree or ask to hear more.

Her preference for disagreement does not authorise rudeness as a winning tactic. Keep the route's first deliberate shared call unspent; these histories explain her interest without completing her change beforehand.
