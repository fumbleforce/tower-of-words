# Rei

Proposed in play: Rei likes winning, loves a bad adventure film and is saving for a small boat she has nowhere to keep. Sales gave her money and mobility after an earlier business failed; she has no interest in pretending that failure was secretly a good decision.

## Established

[Cast](../../docs/game/cast.md#rei-rei): 41, Sales, native Japanese and practical conversational English used with overseas customers. Her first proper introduction belongs at tennis. [Current route](../../docs/game/cast.md#rei-playing-a-game-she-could-lose) retains her other doubles partners and work commitments.

## Proposed history

Rei once helped run a small import business with a friend. She liked the buying trips and the customers. They ordered too much of something that sold brilliantly once and then barely moved. The business closed after a long, unpleasant attempt to reduce the losses. Rei took a sales job because she needed a wage and already knew how to talk to customers. She found she enjoyed the work more when the entire business was not her responsibility.

The friendship did not survive immediately. They argued over who had insisted on the order and avoided one another for years. They have since resumed an occasional meal. Rei can now say which decision was hers, but still remembers every sentence that made it seem reasonable at the time. An apology has not erased her interest in being accurate about the argument.

Her English grew through customers and travel. She is comfortable beginning a conversation she cannot finish elegantly. At home she used to telephone a friend to practise a sentence before an important call; that friend would deliberately answer with something she had not prepared. She found this infuriating and useful.

A past long relationship included regular trips to the cinema. The other person liked spectacular adventure films. Rei pretended to be indulging them until it became obvious she remembered every ridiculous escape. They separated because the relationship had become a comfortable arrangement neither was willing to enlarge. She now goes with different friends or alone and buys the same popcorn. She can miss the company without wanting the arrangement back.

During a coastal holiday she hired a small rowing boat, found the effort satisfying and decided she would like one. Since then she has looked at second-hand boats, where to store them and whether she is imagining a hobby she would actually do. She has bought nothing. The attraction is having an afternoon in which going somewhere slowly is the whole plan.

## Company she chooses

Her usual doubles partner attempts improbable shots. Rei complains about them and would be bored if they stopped. She likes someone who gives her a serious game without treating every correction as a personal insult. The current tennis route tests her habit of deciding the next shot for somebody else. She should remain competitive after learning to share that decision.

An off-court thread concerns the boat. A friend offers access to one on the mainland, provided Rei helps clean it and uses it on the friend's schedule. Rei first rejects the arrangement because it is not quite the one she pictured. Accepting it would let her discover whether she likes the ordinary part of the hobby. Refusing and continuing to save is also a credible choice. The player may be invited on a built, safe outing later; the dossier does not create a boat or a new water route.

She is impatient with reluctant recommendations. If someone hated a film, she wants them to say so. She is much less relaxed about a criticism of a purchase she has just made. Her confidence includes blind spots, and her friendships include people who know them.

## Getting to know her

The first personal conversations can be about the film she saw or an expensive item she decided against. Let her defend something the player thinks is silly. After an earlier remark about going together, learning `isshoni` can open which friend she meant and why they have started meeting again. The failed business then has a present reason to be mentioned.

Her speech gives a question, hears enough of the answer to make a suggestion and sometimes needs to be stopped before making a second. English remains practical and less softened than her Japanese. A later greeting can ask whether she tried the borrowed boat, after the outing has actually happened offscreen. Tennis and her work continue even when the player spends the week elsewhere.
