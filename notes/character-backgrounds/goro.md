# Goro

Proposed in play: Goro is enjoying retirement more than he expected and has begun saying yes to plans before remembering that his spouse has a life of their own. He grows things, enjoys bad jokes and has never quite recovered from being the less adventurous brother.

## Established

The `goro` entry in [the bible](../../bible/facts.yaml) establishes a gentle retired engineer, 61, who keeps the rooftop garden. [Cast](../../docs/game/cast.md#the-others) reserves him for later play. He does not appear on day one.

## Proposed history

Goro became an engineer because he liked finding out how things worked and had an opportunity to train. He stayed at Amakawa because he liked enough of the work and the people. His career included sound decisions, forgotten mistakes and long stretches with little worth retelling. Retirement has given him relief as well as pride. He enjoys being able to leave a task for tomorrow.

His older brother moved often, changed work several times and sent photographs from places Goro had never considered visiting. In family stories the brother became the adventurous one. Goro liked the photographs and was privately annoyed by the comparison. He had also chosen things, including staying somewhere he wanted to be. He has never found a way to say this without sounding defensive.

They get along well on the telephone, where the brother talks and Goro asks a question that reveals something omitted from the story. The brother is now less eager to travel and has suggested coming to the island instead. Goro has begun planning an exhausting visit for a man who mostly wants to see him and sit down.

Goro has a long, affectionate marriage. His spouse had interests and friends during all the years he was at work and has kept them. He was surprised after retirement to discover that a free Tuesday did not mean they were both free. They sometimes have to negotiate the use of their own home: Goro likes company arriving without ceremony; his spouse likes knowing how many people will want lunch.

Gardening began with surplus seedlings from a neighbour. Goro killed some, kept others alive and liked having something to look at each morning. He prefers plants that give him something to eat, though he has become attached to a flowering pot somebody gave him. He is sentimental about individual objects and will deny this while explaining why a cracked pot must stay.

## His brother's visit

Goro wants to show that he has had an interesting life here. He proposes a tour involving every person who might remember him being useful. His spouse asks whether the brother has requested any of this. Goro trims the itinerary and then starts adding things back.

The visit can eventually include one short tour, an ordinary meal and a long, slightly awkward conversation. The brother may envy how settled Goro is. That does not cancel Goro's envy of the photographs. Their relationship gets another afternoon in which both accounts can be true.

The rooftop garden remains somewhere Goro wants to be. He may send the player away because company is coming, or invite them to stay because there is too much food. He can give useful technical advice and become impatient when a former colleague treats retirement as free on-call support.

## Getting to know him

Goro appears only in later play. An early conversation about the garden can reveal what he plans to cook with a particular plant. Later, the same `isshoni` word used for the player's club invitations helps clarify who is coming to the meal he mentioned. The player can return after the visit to ask what they actually did.

His proposed voice is gentle and concrete. When he is proud of something he may explain its history longer than anyone asked. His jokes occasionally arrive after the conversation has moved on. Let his spouse or brother interrupt affectionately without making them the writer's correction mechanism. Their housing and employment arrangements need a separate setting decision before implementation.
