# Goro

Proposed in play: Goro enjoys patient observation and a small practical improvement. Retirement lets him choose when to stop, while a successor's successful changes still tempt him to explain his original method.

## Established

The `goro` entry in [the bible](../../bible/facts.yaml) establishes a gentle retired engineer, 61, who keeps the rooftop garden. [Cast](../../docs/game/cast.md#the-others) reserves him for later play. He does not appear on day one.

## Proposed history

Goro continues living on Amakawa after retirement and tends the company's rooftop garden by arrangement. The proposal leaves his employment and residential terms open.

Goro spent much of his working life on systems whose good performance was easy to overlook. He liked comparing a real installation with the assumptions in its documentation and asking the person who used it most what had become troublesome. Over time he became especially good at distinguishing a tolerable imperfection from a fault that would consume somebody's whole afternoon.

He also grew used to being consulted. Retirement brought the pleasant discovery that he could stop halfway through an optional task and the less pleasant discovery that a successor could choose a method he disliked. The successor's method sometimes worked better. Goro can tell that story with enjoyment, though he still wants to add exactly why his original method made sense at the time.

Gardening began well before retirement. A neighbour gave him more seedlings than he needed; he kept them alive imperfectly, discovered that he liked tending them and began exchanging practical observations with other residents. He is capable of trying advice because it sounds interesting and forgetting to write down what changed. He can spend a morning comparing two pots and then lose track of which received the extra compost.

He has a long, affectionate marriage. His spouse likes the garden's produce and dislikes having every windowsill recruited for it. Their compromise is a limited amount of indoor space and a periodic conversation about what counts as a container. They walk together sometimes and have separate friends. The spouse's identity remains undecided; neither person is introduced as the other's caretaker.

One former colleague still visits to talk about work. Goro enjoys the visit more when the colleague eventually starts talking about something else. He would like to stay in touch without becoming the unofficial help desk for his old job.

## What this gives play

He is good at patient observation and at accepting a partial improvement. He is less good at noticing when a suggestion from him sounds like an instruction to a younger person. He may be right about a machine and wrong about how another person wants to spend a morning.

He wants enough small sweet peppers to blister a panful for supper without counting how many are left for tomorrow. A neighbouring gardener wants more flowers, and they are deciding which pots to give up. He and his spouse also want two nights at a mainland lakeside inn in late autumn. His spouse has chosen it for the baths; Goro wants to hire a rowing boat and eat the breakfast he remembers from their first stay.

Incidental tells include judging a pot by its drainage before its appearance, leaving a task unfinished to sit with somebody, and remembering a colleague's preferred lunch more readily than a component number.

## Discoveries across play

1. At his eventual introduction, he asks the player to look at a pepper hidden behind the leaves that he nearly missed. Known `mitai` can support looking closer. While checking for another, he mentions how he wants to cook them.
2. A return visit shows that he tried somebody else's suggestion. He explains the result, including the possibility that it made no clear difference. A casual question can reveal the neighbour who first gave him seedlings.
3. A heard `yasumi` remark can later open the lakeside trip. `isshoni` helps clarify who is going if the earlier conversation left that unclear. After the trip, the same topic can become a question about the rowing boat or breakfast; the opportunity survives a missed visit.
4. Once familiar, a conversation about a changed work method lets him tell the successor story. Mori may have his own opinion if both men are present for an independently motivated visit; no lifelong friendship between them is assumed.

He is pleased to answer a technical question on a garden visit, but may leave a longer conversation for another day if he and his spouse already have plans.
