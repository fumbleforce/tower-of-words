# Emi

Emi is thirty-two and leads the B2 team. She talks quickly and in complete sentences, as if she has a meeting in ten minutes, which she often does. She gives the good news first and the problem afterwards, sometimes so late that her team learns what she has agreed to after the meeting is over. Often she says yes before a question has finished and only then works out what she has promised.

Emi has brown eyes, fair skin and a reddish auburn bob. She pushes her tortoiseshell glasses up with one finger when she is thinking. She is curvy and dresses for meetings upstairs in a charcoal blazer and cream blouse. Long days at a desk leave her shoulders stiff, and a meeting that runs past two hours gives her a headache. A physiotherapist told her to swim twice a week. She manages about once.

Emi grew up in Reading, in England, the only child of an English father and a Japanese mother who taught at the town's Saturday Japanese school. Until she was sixteen she spent every Saturday morning in her mother's class while her friends were at swimming galas. She resented it and swam for a county club anyway, training before school in the dark. After a degree in Japanese and business, she joined Amakawa at twenty-four to translate contracts and manuals for imported equipment. Within three years she had moved into purchasing, and three years ago she took over B2.

Emi lives in a one-bedroom flat in a staff block on the east side, where her swimsuit dries on the balcony rail. Her kitchen calendar is filled in with three colours of pen: work, the club and things she wants to do. The third colour gets used least. She drinks strong tea with milk and brings it back from the mainland by the box. On Sunday mornings she does a cryptic crossword while her mother phones, switching between Japanese and English mid-sentence.

Emi wants a department head's job upstairs within five years, and B2 is her proof that she can keep something difficult running. When she promised head office ten more years from the old machines, she was thinking partly of her own career. She fought for a hardware engineer for the same reason, as well as because the servers were failing. She has not told her team this.

Asking for something for herself is hard for her. When she tries, it comes out like an item on a meeting agenda, and she notices and laughs. She joined the swimming club to have one evening a week when nobody handed her a list. Within a fortnight she knew who had the keys and was keeping the booking sheet. She would rather do a job herself than watch someone do it slowly, and the people around her have learned to let her.
