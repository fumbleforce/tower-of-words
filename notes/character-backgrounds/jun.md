# Jun

Proposed in play: Jun is good at making other people's evenings easy and deliberately makes his own more complicated. He took over the bar because he wanted it, likes a small risk and is considering bringing an old friend into a business arrangement that once damaged their friendship.

## Established

The `jun` entry in [the bible](../../bible/facts.yaml) establishes age 38, payroll work by day and running the company bar in the evenings. The former-jazz-drummer alternative was rejected and stays rejected. His exact rota and the bar's staffing are not yet established; the proposal does not require him to work two complete shifts every day.

## Proposed history

Jun's first job at Amakawa was routine administration. Payroll had a vacancy, he was good with the work and he moved across. He likes a result that can be checked and a day that has an end. Nothing about the role required him to become a patient listener, although he often is one.

The bar began as occasional cover for a person he liked. Jun enjoyed serving, choosing what to stock and hearing a room become lively. When that person wanted to stop running it, Jun asked to take responsibility. He wanted somewhere on the island that felt partly his. A viable rota with other staff is necessary; the proposal does not make him work two full shifts every day.

Before Amakawa, he and a friend tried selling second-hand furniture. They enjoyed buying it, particularly the pieces nobody else wanted. Storage and transport cost more than they had planned. Jun kept one ugly table and a resentment about which of them paid the last bill. The friend remembers doing most of the lifting. They reconciled slowly and still argue over whose version is more generous.

Jun has been with his current partner for several years. They like each other's company and differ sharply about risk. The partner works elsewhere and has friends who have never been to the company bar. Jun sometimes wants to join those evenings, then finds he has arranged something at his own place instead. He is capable of making himself indispensable and complaining that he cannot get away.

He likes late films, cheap furniture with one excellent feature and taking a route he has not used before. He is poor at leaving a profitable arrangement alone if he sees a way to make it more interesting. The bar's books are accurate. His account to his partner of how much time a new idea will take is less reliable.

## The table offer

The old furniture friend has found a small set of chairs that would suit the bar and suggests another occasional buying partnership. Jun is immediately interested. His partner remembers how the earlier one ended. The friend also remembers and wants an agreement about storage and costs before buying anything together.

Jun initially feels insulted by the formality, despite being the person who still complains about the old bill. He can agree to a modest arrangement, decide against it or buy one set of chairs for the bar alone. The friendship continues in different ways depending on whether he treats the caution as a practical request or a personal slight. The player can have an opinion without becoming financier or bookkeeper.

## Getting to know him

Introduce the bar as a place where Jun is sometimes working and sometimes sitting with his own drink. The furniture story can begin with somebody disliking the ugly table. Jun will defend it and tell a version of the buying trip that makes the failure sound enjoyable.

When Jun later mentions the possible partnership, the player can return to the earlier story and ask about the final bill he skipped over. He can answer candidly and still disagree with his friend. His proposed voice is relaxed, with practical questions at the counter and longer speculative sentences when an idea interests him. Payroll information stays at work; a friendly drink never exposes someone else's salary.
