# Saki

Proposed in play: Saki chose Legal because she liked the work and has no wish to make her evenings resemble it. She sings badly with enthusiasm, can be careless with an unimportant promise and has begun wondering whether an old friendship still includes room for the person she is now.

## Established

The `saki` entry in [the bible](../../bible/facts.yaml) establishes Legal and her approved design. It does not establish professional licence, precise rank, age or personal relationships. This proposal concerns company work and personality, and makes no claims about Japanese legal procedure.

## Proposed history

Saki studied with a fairly clear idea of the field she wanted. A placement confirmed that she liked reading carefully, identifying an unresolved question and getting somebody to answer it. She applied to Amakawa and has largely had the career she intended. Her exact qualifications and rank remain unsettled; the proposal gives her no unapproved professional licence.

At home, her family remembered her as the sensible one long after the description became inconvenient. She could make a ridiculous decision and still be asked to explain why it was wise. In response she began keeping parts of her life very separate. Colleagues hear little about the weekends on which she travels to see performers she likes. Her old friends know less than they think about what her actual work involves.

A long friendship survives from school. The friend is proud of Saki and introduces her by her impressive-sounding department, then retells stories in which Saki was the one who knew better. Saki used to enjoy this. Now she sometimes behaves worse in that friend's company simply to prevent the expected story. The friend is understandably confused.

Saki has a partner who enjoys her least dignified tastes. They watch extravagant television competitions, argue about contestants and sing along badly. The relationship has lasted partly because neither needs the other to be consistent. They live separately by preference and sometimes forget to tell one another about an invitation that matters. The partner's identity is open; the proposal does not reserve Saki for a player romance.

She likes a long lunch, bitter drinks and frightening rides she will regret halfway through. She is willing to be wrong about a film. She is much less willing to be wrong about whether somebody was making fun of her. A joke at her expense can stay in her head long after everyone else has forgotten it.

## A weekend with old friends

The old friend asks her to organise a group reunion because she is so dependable. Saki agrees and then resents the task. She chooses a place she wants to visit, sends the details late and is hurt when several people cannot come. For once, the criticism is deserved.

An interesting continuation gives her room to say she did not want the job while still owning the poor invitation. Someone else can organise the next gathering. Saki may attend and enjoy having no responsibility, then find she misses being consulted. Her partner can tease her about that without delivering a final verdict on her character.

## Getting to know her

An early conversation outside work can concern a singer she likes and has no wish to justify. The player can ask whether she wants to sing rather than assuming she is only there to listen. `utaitai` is proposed future vocabulary here, absent from the current word set; its first understandable use and a later reuse still need to be designed before it becomes a known-word reply. She may be loud and off key. Any performed scene requires the physical staging and appropriate audio before implementation.

A later mention of the reunion lets `yoyaku` clarify whether the plan is actually booked. Afterward, the player can ask who came and hear the part of the story Saki initially omitted. Her proposed speech is usually complete and exact, but becomes less careful when she is enjoying an argument about entertainment. Avoid legal metaphors for meals, dates or games. She can tell the player plainly that she has had enough of paperwork for one day.
