# Saki

Proposed in play: Saki hears the missing condition in an agreement. Her enjoyment of clear rules comes partly from games that were more fun once the argument was settled; her own competitive streak sometimes prolongs it.

## Established

The `saki` entry in [the bible](../../bible/facts.yaml) establishes Legal and her approved design. It does not establish professional licence, precise rank, age or personal relationships. This proposal concerns company work and personality, and makes no claims about Japanese legal procedure.

## Proposed history

Saki entered Legal through work assembling files, comparing versions and following up missing information. She learned that a sentence can be accurate and still leave two people expecting different things. Her later responsibility involves asking those people what they are actually agreeing to, with senior specialists handling matters beyond her remit. The exact qualification and title can be decided when her job is built.

An early mentor was exacting about wording and generous about mistakes caught in a draft. Saki became comfortable challenging a sentence before it was final. She is less comfortable when people hear that challenge as an attack on them. She sometimes gives a technically useful correction before acknowledging the effort that produced the draft, then wonders why nobody wants the second correction.

Her family enjoyed tabletop games and interpreted disputed rules with more seriousness than the occasion deserved. The best evenings ended with an agreed house rule and another round. Saki enjoys a clean rule because it lets the game continue, not because she enjoys denying someone a turn. She also likes winning and has occasionally argued a point longer when it favoured her.

A longstanding friend is casual about plans and very reliable in a real difficulty. Saki took years to stop treating those traits as a contradiction that needed correcting. Their friendship works because the friend accepts a precise meeting time when it matters and Saki sometimes accepts an afternoon without an itinerary. They have a familiar disagreement about spoilers that remains unresolved.

Her romantic history is modest and not central to her self-understanding. She has declined a second date with someone she liked because the conversation felt like another meeting, and accepted another because they could sit through a disappointing film and laugh about it. She is currently unattached.

## What this gives play

Saki can hear the missing condition in an apparently settled arrangement and prevent an ordinary disappointment. She may ask for more certainty than a leisure plan can reasonably offer. She is funny when she describes a small procedural absurdity in complete seriousness, but should not deliver legal-themed punchlines on demand.

She wants a regular game evening whose rules are agreed before the food arrives, and a work handover that gives a junior colleague enough context to act without asking her about every page. Those aims can coexist without making the game evening a metaphor in her dialogue.

## Discoveries across play

1. On an introduced work request, she asks a concrete question about what the player means. Known `daijoubu` may prompt her to check what exactly is okay, while ordinary replies preserve understanding.
2. A later conversation about a booking can use `yoyaku` to distinguish an intention from a confirmed plan. Her next response remembers which one the player actually chose.
3. Jun may mention an open game evening after Saki has agreed it is open. Asking her reveals the family habit of disputed rules and one rule she lost an argument about.
4. With familiarity, she can mention the friend whose imprecise plans are usually enjoyable. A shared afternoon may remain unplanned without becoming a test she needs to pass.

Personal trust never unlocks somebody else's file, and the player is not made her legal adviser. She can keep her precision and find more ways to enjoy it.
