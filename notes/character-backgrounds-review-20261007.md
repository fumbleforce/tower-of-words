## Should fix before presenting the rewrite

### 1. Three central personal threads are the same offer to share a home

`notes/character-backgrounds/emi.md:23`, `aoi.md:23`, `kiyoko.md:23`.

An old friend offers shared housing; the character weighs company and a desired future against a comfortable present. Emi measures furniture, Aoi imagines her kitchen and lamp, and Kiyoko imagines a garden and a door away from work. Their motives differ, but the promised recurring conversations have the same external question: will she move in with the friend? For a rewrite specifically requested because the cast felt interchangeable, this is too prominent a repeated premise.

Keep at most two and change the actual pressure in the third. Aoi is the best candidate because her strongest material is the course she abandoned, her affection and envy toward the friend who stayed, and her wish to be treated as someone whose life is progressing. Those already offer a different source of decisions. Do not merely exchange the flat for a house or put a different date on the offer. Preserve the room and lamp as tastes if useful.

### 2. Kanae and Sumi share too much of the unsuccessful artist biography

`notes/character-backgrounds/kanae.md:11`, `:19`; `sumi.md:11`, `:13`, `:15`.

Both try to earn through their art, dislike or fail at obtaining customers, enter ordinary office work for security, continue the art privately and resent a more successful maker with whom they once had an intimate connection. Different media and a former partner versus classmate do not sufficiently separate that causal chain. Sumi's dimly hung rival's work and returned gift are excellent particular actions, but the background producing them remains close to Kanae's.

Give one a different route into paid work or a different relationship between ambition and art. Sumi could retain her artistic rivalry while acquiring her office career independently of failed artistic self-employment. The task is to break the repeated causal chain, not remove either woman's ambition or make one a secret prodigy.

Ishibashi, Rei and Jun also have unsuccessful businesses, but those are more distinct in present effect: a shared marriage history, a costly decision and estranged business friend, and temptation to repeat a partnership. I would not mechanically remove all business failures. The Kanae/Sumi pair is the clearest repetition to fix.

### 3. A cluster of former relationships still ends over wanting less shared life

`notes/character-backgrounds/emi.md:15`, `nanami.md:17`, `cctv.md:21`, `rei.md:17`.

Emi ends a long relationship to live alone. Nanami ends one because she prefers the evenings apart and admits it too late. CCTV resists moving in and wishes they had said so earlier. Rei's relationship stays comfortable until neither will enlarge it. The new draft has genuine variation elsewhere, including Kuro being refused, Mio's public arguments, Aoi's adopted tastes and Kiyoko's humiliation at being left. This four-person group still repeats the earlier draft's compatible adults with incompatible degrees of togetherness.

Revise one or two of Nanami/CCTV/Rei. Emi's history currently does useful work in making her new housing decision difficult. The other revised relationship should leave a different present effect, not just a different object from the old partner. A modest, funny, selfish or unresolved history would all work; betrayal or trauma is not required. Preserve the existing cake/scarf or film pleasures if they fit the new history.

### 4. Saki presents an unregistered word as an available reply

`notes/character-backgrounds/saki.md:29` says, "If the player knows `utaitai`". That word has no match in the current `game3d` content or `docs/game` sources checked. The index promises that vocabulary outside the existing set is explicitly identified as proposed (`index.md:45`).

Mark this as proposed future vocabulary with an introduction and later reuse still to be designed, or use an existing reply that fits the encounter. There is no need to author a new lesson during this background pass.

### 5. Jun's reservation word is attached to an agreement that contains no reservation

`notes/character-backgrounds/jun.md:31`, referring back to the buying partnership at `:23` and `:25`.

The new plan concerns buying chairs, sharing storage and agreeing costs. No venue, delivery slot or reserved purchase is described. Yet the discovery hook proposes `yoyaku`, the game's booking/reservation word, to clarify which part of the agreement is settled. This repeats the old temptation to attach a familiar word to a vaguely related situation. The optional wording "or another established word" defers the problem rather than solving it.

Give the actual story a reservation only if it naturally needs one, select a relevant existing phrase, or leave the word to be chosen and labelled as future writing work. Do not equate an ordinary business agreement with a booking. The concrete financial disagreement can stand without a vocabulary hook on every detail.

## Small prose cleanup

- `cctv.md:9` repeats the immediately preceding Established paragraph as Established scope. Merge or remove the duplicate paragraph; it gives no new character information.
- `cctv.md:15` ends by denying that the operator is a tragic all-seeing watcher. `emi.md:15` announces that the separation has no convenient villain. These are remnants of instructions to the writer. The positive histories already make the intended point. Similar denials are less frequent than in the original draft, but the final humanizer pass should remove the obvious leftovers rather than append reassurances.
- `index.md:47` broadly prohibits a photograph, gift or word from revealing an ex-partner's identity, even though the preceding sentence concerns voluntary disclosure. That is an unnecessary restriction on fictional ordinary relationships and conflicts with the aim of letting lives overlap naturally. Confidential workplace information and unchosen disclosure are different matters. Narrow the sentence; a character choosing to name an ex or show a holiday photograph is not inherently a privacy failure.
