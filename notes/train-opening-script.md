# Train opening: first playable draft

Draft for review, 2026-09-25. Covers New Game through stepping onto the island platform. Does not modify the playable game. Supersedes the phone-argument opening in day1-opening-proposal.md: the user asked to start with easy Japanese, teach tapping in play, and adapt subsequent text.

The dramatic movement: help a stranger, find her unexpectedly easy to talk to, discover she knows your new boss, arrive with a connection and a small question about their relationship. Rei is working during the journey and selectively avoiding more work. She is neither a tour guide nor a tutorial character.

English translations in this document are writer/developer reference. They are hidden in play until requested. Visual descriptions are staging instructions, not narration for the player to read. Short English interface instructions and physical-action labels are visible. No introductory exposition paragraphs.

## Shared presentation and functionality

- Start directly after New Game. No name entry, level selection, placement test, or onboarding app.
- Japanese is voiced with synchronised Japanese subtitles. Start with short, easy utterances even for an unknown player. Natural orthography does not imply dense language.
- One subtitle at a time. The player advances it. Inspecting a word, choosing a response, or looking at a prop holds the scene; nothing expires.
- Tappable words in subtitles and spoken options: kanji word -> kana reading above -> contextual English below. Already-kana words go directly to meaning. Optional romanisation is reachable in the word explanation, including for katakana.
- The first tap cue appears on ありがとう. The first two-stage reading cue appears on 初日. Tutorial-requested taps are not evidence of a language gap.
- A compact Meaning control explains the whole current line. Replay repeats it slightly more slowly. Backlog remains available. Explain each control only when needed; do not present a controls list at startup.
- Tapping an option's words never chooses it. A separate speak/confirm target commits a spoken reply. Choosing speaks the protagonist's line. Physical gestures have plain English labels.
- A reading voluntarily requested is retained on that word's next appearance. If help is needed across several lines, select the supported versions below for later beats. Do not rewrite a line already on screen. One lookup does not lower the whole scene's language level.
- Whole-line Meaning is an escape hatch, not an instruction to mark every word unknown. No-help exposure does not establish mastery. Gestures, preference choices and friendly/rude choices are not scored as grammar answers.
- All essential plot information remains recoverable with help. No penalties for replay, lookups, gestures or time spent reading.

## Shot continuity

Use the shot-staging skill when making boards or requesting art. These are proposed shots; new art needs the existing asset review process.

Interior: a company monorail carriage with a side-facing bench. Rei sits beside an empty seat occupied by her closed document folder and a lidded takeaway coffee. Her small open laptop is on her lap. The player stands in the aisle beside this seat with a backpack, one hand on a pole. The first shot looks down from the player's standing eye level at the seat and Rei's hands. Later conversation uses a seated oblique side view, placing Rei and the side window in frame. The player places the backpack between their own feet after sitting.

Maintain the side-window rule: sea and sky only, water well below the carriage; no forward track or approaching island in these interior views. Morning light enters consistently from the sea-facing window. Train movement and a changing motor sound establish travel without a camera constantly floating around.

The island reveal is an exterior establishing shot, not the player's literal side-window view. Camera outside and behind the train, offset to one side, looking along its direction of travel toward the island. Show its last car, continuous elevated beam, supporting piers and island ahead. The train travels away from the camera toward the island. Return to the same interior positions afterwards.

At arrival, use a separate close shot facing the side doors. The platform is on that side and visible only once the train has entered the station. Audio and a stopped view establish arrival before anyone stands.

## 1. The cup

**See/hear:** New Game fades into the carriage. Wheel/motor hum, a keyboard, a soft clack at a track joint. No speech yet. Rei's folder shifts on the seat beside her. The cup perched on it tilts toward her laptop.

Rei looks down.

**Woman:** 「あ。」

*Ah.*

The action settles into a held anticipation pose. Two clear actions appear beside the objects:

- **Catch the cup.** The player steadies it. Rei catches the folder with her free hand.
- **Warn her.** The player says 「あ、コーヒー。」 Rei catches the cup and closes the laptop with her other hand.

Both succeed. Use a quick hand/cup insert for the result. No spill, score, timer, slow-motion spectacle, or exaggerated reaction.

She checks the lid, then looks at you properly.

**Woman:** 「ありがとう。」

*Thanks.*

**Functionality:** First action is achievable without any Japanese. The warning line is an assisted spoken action, not a production test. Now place a small cue beside ありがとう: **Tap a word for help.** A tap reveals *thanks / thank you* beneath this kana expression. Do not force a lookup; Continue is still available. No third tutorial panel appears.

**Change:** You have interrupted her work by helping. She makes room for you.

## 2. A seat

**See:** She closes the laptop, puts it in her bag, and stacks the folder on her own lap. The coffee is now firmly in her hand. She indicates the cleared seat.

**Woman:** 「どうぞ。」

*Go ahead.*

The seat is the available action: **Sit.** It is visually clear what she is offering. Sit; put the backpack between your feet. The camera settles at seated height. Let the carriage sound and view breathe for a moment.

**Functionality:** どうぞ is tappable, with the contextual explanation *Go ahead; here, offering you the seat*. No comprehension question. No choice asking whether to sit or stand for hidden points. This expression returns with a different gesture at the end of the journey.

## 3. New badge

**See:** The player takes a company badge from its clear sleeve. There is still protective film across its face. The AMAKAWA mark matches the mark beside the carriage doors. The visible department is 企画室７. Avoid tiny decorative Japanese text.

She glances at the badge, then at you.

**Woman:** 「初日？」

*First day?*

**Functionality:** If the player taps 初日, show しょにち above it. Brief cue: **Tap again for the meaning.** A second tap shows *first day*. This explains the reading/meaning distinction on an expression with immediate personal relevance. Forced or tutorial-prompted exploration is excluded from adaptation decisions. Use later voluntary requests to infer support needs.

Reply:

- **「うん。」** *Yeah.*
- **Nod.** No spoken line.

Both communicate the same thing and continue. She gives a small acknowledging nod. No praise for managing an easy answer.

Optional extra appears as a small conversation choice, alongside Continue:

- **「そっちは？」** *And you?*

If asked:

**Woman:** 「三年目。」

*Third year.*

She presses down a worn corner of her own badge sleeve with her thumb. Leave this detail uncaptioned.

**Functionality:** The badge is inspectable, not a form to fill out. Tapping its department explains *Planning Office 7; your assigned team*. Proper names and organisation labels are available context, not mandatory learning targets. Do not branch personality or relationship scores on nodding versus speaking.

**Change:** The player now has a concrete role in this place, and a more experienced colleague beside them.

## 4. The company gets bigger

**See/hear:** The motor pitch changes. Cut outside behind the train: the guideway reaches a dense island of offices and residential towers, with smaller streets and shops between them. Show the company mark on one major building, not every surface. Let the image hold without speech first.

Over the establishing shot:

**Woman:** 「全部、会社。」

*All of it belongs to the company.*

This is an amused observation to a newcomer, delivered casually. No company history follows it.

Return inside. Reply:

- **「全部？」** *All of it?*
  - **Woman:** 「うん。」 *Yeah.*
- **「すごいね。」** *That's impressive.*
  - **Woman:** 「最初はね。」 *At first.*
- **Keep looking.** She leaves you to it; hold the exterior a little longer before returning.

If substantial help was requested earlier, use **「大きいね。」** (*It's big.*) instead of すごいね if 大きい is already understood; otherwise retain the original with help available. The supported reaction to either spoken response is simply 「うん。」 Avoid selecting a synonym solely because it is tagged as lower level.

**See:** The player returns the badge sleeve to the backpack. A second card is visible with it: a bed symbol, **A · 203**, and 寮. She notices.

**Woman:** 「寮？」

*The dorm?*

The player can confirm with **「うん。」** or a nod. They briefly open the housing card on the phone: a delivery photograph shows taped moving boxes outside door 203. One box has an AMAKAWA delivery sticker; another has a recognisable hand-drawn mug on it. No paragraph explains the delivery.

**Woman:** 「今日から？」

*Starting today?*

**You:** 「うん。」

She moves the folder away from the edge of your shared seat, leaving you a little more space.

**Functionality:** Confirmation opens the relevant housing photo automatically as the protagonist shows it to her. It does not open a multi-page app. 寮 is a single contextual word with help available. Together, the card, boxes and conversation establish that the player is moving in today. Close the insert back to the conversation with the same Continue control.

**Change:** This place is where you will live as well as work. Rei's small accommodation keeps her from becoming a stream of cynical remarks.

## 5. Emi

**See/hear:** A different vibration: the player's company phone. Contact photo and name **エミ / Emi**; one voice-message play button. The button is the next action, not an unreadable Japanese menu.

Tap Play.

**Emi, voice message:** 「おはよう！」

*Morning!*

**Emi:** 「もうすぐ？」

*Nearly here?*

The train is approaching the station. Reply choices:

- **「もうすぐ。」** *Nearly there.*
- **「ちょっと、緊張してる。」** *I'm a little nervous.*

The second is optional expression, not a harder answer worth more points. A visible **Help me reply** action can reveal the intentions in English. Reply inspection uses the already-taught taps, and a separate Send action confirms the choice. The protagonist records the selected line; do not invent keyboard typing or a live AI conversation here.

Emi's response arrives after a short typing/recording indicator:

If Nearly there:

**Emi:** 「わかった。」

*Got it.*

If Nervous:

**Emi:** 「大丈夫。」

*You'll be fine.*

Both continue:

**Emi:** 「会社で待ってるね。」

*I'll be waiting at the office.*

Supported version of that last line: **「待ってるね。」** *I'll be waiting.* The company context has already been established. In the full line, retain the reading on 会社 if the player requested it during the island exchange. This is the first obvious same-scene display adaptation.

Rei recognises the voice. She glances from the phone to your department badge.

**Woman:** 「上司、エミ？」

*Emi's your boss?*

Confirm with **「うん。」** or a nod. 上司 is tappable; its contextual meaning is *boss / team lead*. Show the relationship through this exchange, not a narration card.

She looks as if she might add something, then takes a sip of coffee instead.

**Functionality:** Every essential reply has help. Sending either answer works. The game does not need to infer whether the player is actually nervous. Adapt the language support, not the underlying personality decision.

**Change:** Someone at the destination is expecting you. The stranger knows her, and you have noticed that there is more to their relationship.

## 6. Ask, or leave it

**See:** Rei opens her folder again. She is checking a short table of figures, not reading a lore document. Its contents need not be legible. The player can continue the conversation or let her work.

Optional spoken question:

**You:** 「エミ、どんな人？」

*What's Emi like?*

**Woman:** 「優しいよ。」

*She's nice.*

She puts one sheet back into the folder.

**Woman:** 「頼みごと、多いけど。」

*Asks a lot of favours, though.*

If the player has needed substantial explanation, use **「仕事、多いよ。」** (*There's a lot of work.*), with reading support on 仕事 if needed. It keeps her reservation while reducing the phrase complexity. Keep the whole-line escape hatch: a shorter sentence is not necessarily understood. Do not define 'easy' by character count alone.

Alternative action: **Let her work.** A few quiet seconds: she crosses out one entry, checks another, and closes the folder. Same main progression; no relationship penalty for choosing silence.

Her phone lights up beside the folder. Caller: **エミ**. The name was just introduced. She sees it and silences the ringing. She does not look confused or guilty.

If the player asked about Emi, Rei catches their glance.

**Woman:** 「あとで。」

*Later.*

If the player let her work, she simply puts the phone away. Do not add a question forcing the player to intervene.

**Functionality:** Optional conversation is a source of extra voiced Japanese. Choosing silence preserves progress. The caller name can be tapped during the held shot; prior requested reading support remains. No quiz asks the player to identify the caller. Record this as a witnessed story event for later dialogue, not a language success.

**Change:** Rei is helpful to you but resistant to your boss. This is ordinary workplace friction; no ominous music or mystery framing. A future request to visit Sales now has a personal context.

## 7. Arrival

**See/hear:** Arrival chime. A station roof enters the outside view; the train slows and comes fully to rest. Music thins beneath the announcement.

**Announcement:** 「天川シティ、天川シティです。」

*Amakawa City. Amakawa City.*

Keep the opening announcement this short. It supplies authentic station rhythm without requiring a formal multi-clause speech. No left/right comprehension test.

The doors open on the platform side. People further along the carriage stand. Rei closes her bag, tucks the folder under her arm, and keeps the coffee in her other hand. The player picks up their backpack.

Her badge turns face outward as she stands: **黒田 レイ / 営業部**. Pause long enough to inspect it, but do not demand it. Speaking establishes the name for players who ignore the badge.

**Woman:** 「レイ。営業。」

*Rei. Sales.*

She indicates herself with the hand holding the cup. The speaker label now changes from Woman to Rei. Her name is not an assessed vocabulary item. 営業 has the contextual gloss *Sales; her department*.

Reply:

- **「よろしく。」** *Nice to meet you.*
- **Smile and nod.**

She nods back. Keep it small.

**Rei:** 「エミによろしく。」

*Say hi to Emi.*

The phrase explanation covers the whole expression. Do not imply that the two uses of よろしく have an identical word-for-word English translation.

## 8. The way out

**See:** Both are in the short aisle approaching the open door. Rei steps aside to let you pass; she does not physically hold automatic train doors. The platform is visible and safe.

**Rei:** 「どうぞ。」

*Go ahead.*

It is the expression from the offered seat, now accompanying a different gesture. Preserve help if the player requested it; do not deliberately hide it to create a test.

Action: **Step onto the platform.** If the player chooses to replay or inspect instead, everyone waits naturally. No departing-train pressure.

Exterior platform shot: the protagonist exits, Rei follows a pace behind. She turns toward the main pedestrian flow and glances back just enough to check you are coming. Beyond the station canopy are the lower levels of the company city. For the first time the destination is near enough to walk into.

The phone vibrates once. Emi has sent a photograph of the entrance gates, with a short message:

**Emi, text:** 「入口は、ここ。」

*Here's the entrance.*

The photograph supplies the destination. The message and its translation are reopenable. Emi remains at the office waiting for the newcomer; the next scene must teach entering the building before introducing the internal route to her.

The player saves the photograph to the phone's current objective with **Keep this photo**. The next playable goal is **Find the entrance in Emi's photo.** No kanji directory yet.

Autosave. End train sequence.

## Authoring and adaptation notes

The first required spoken exchanges are あ, ありがとう, どうぞ, 初日 and うん, separated by physical actions. The phone argument is gone. A beginner can follow every essential step using gesture, visuals and readily available explanation. Later optional lines provide more listening without making them progression requirements.

Do not set a vocabulary target for the entire script. Every word is accessible, but only a few requested words should be selected for deliberate recurrence. どうぞ returns naturally; 会社 returns when suitable; the name エミ becomes a recognisable social connection. Reuse 初日 in a later office welcome instead of forcing it into this conversation twice.

There are no branch loops or wrong-answer dead ends. Catch/warn merges at thanks; nod/speak merges at the same social acknowledgement; island responses merge at housing; phone replies merge at Emi waiting; optional questions merge before arrival. All branches retain cup ownership, laptop location, baggage, speakers and destinations.

Rei's voice is brief and matter-of-fact, sometimes dry. Her kindness is in making room, answering, and letting the player go first. Emi sounds warmer and uses softening endings naturally. Neither discusses language levels or congratulates the player for vocabulary.

Pacing alternates action, a short exchange, visual space, curiosity, connection and arrival. The relationship changes: a distracted stranger becomes someone the player can approach later, with evidence that her interests differ from the boss's. The unresolved question concerns how those people will get along at work. Magic remains private and unrevealed until the copy room as currently intended.

Review risks: the coffee interaction needs a legible hand/prop insert; the exterior reveal needs correctly staged art; mandatory housing and contact interactions must remain brief on a phone. Meeting Rei here changes her Sales greeting. Those are proposed story changes, not already implemented behaviour.
