# The island beyond B2

Design doc from the relationships agent, 2026-09-28. Nothing here is approved or built. Day 1 comes first (GUIDE, Scope). This is where the game goes once day 1 is solid: how Eric's work takes him out of the basement, what opens when, and what a day and a week feel like. The people and their arcs are in notes/RELATIONSHIPS.md.

## The idea in one paragraph

Eric's IT tickets are the reason he goes everywhere. A ticket is both a job and a day pass: it gets him into a place he can't enter yet, puts him in front of the people who work there, and gives him a problem to solve by hand or with a command. The island's old systems are what answer to his voice, and his contract is to replace them. So the oldest districts are the richest in magic, and the newer, more polite districts are where Japanese matters more than commands. That ordering also gives GUIDE's rule (casual first, polite and keigo later) a place on the map.

### The internal parties

The staff council (社員会) runs the island's small budgets: the canteen, the dorms, events, which department gets migrated first. It has three groups, and they matter in RELATIONSHIPS.md (Kenji, Kaori, Emi, Kiyoko):

| Group | Wants | Leader |
|---|---|---|
| The old guard | Nothing changed that doesn't have to be; seniority respected | Kiyoko's faction of general managers |
| The reformers | The upgrade done fast; everything measurable | Head office's people, Emi's audience |
| The basement | Free drinks from the B2 vending machine (a joke platform that wins a seat) | Kenji |

The council votes once a month, on things like a budget line for Kaori's guests, the migration order and Emi's promotion. It's where bonds change things for the whole island.

## How places open up

Five keys, all things the player earns by playing, and no hard walls. GUIDE's rule holds: no gate needs you to recall a word.

| Key | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Tickets | A ticket is a day pass to where the job is. | A fax ticket gets him onto the fifth floor before Sales knows his name. |
| Badge lines | After his first ticket in a district and one person there at bond step 2, Nanami adds the district to his card. | The press building after Kanae's newsletter photo. |
| People | Invitations and keys from people at step 3. | Goro's roof door, Kiyoko's floor after the lessons, the bar after hours with Jun. |
| Commands | Old doors, lifts and gates answer to him. The service tunnels, which connect every district, open to 開けて and are the fast way around. | Taking the tunnel from B2 to the canteen in a minute. |
| Standing | Migrations toward the contract quota (30 of 45, RELATIONSHIPS.md) move him from contractor to trusted contractor, then to a staff offer (正社員 as 主任) around New Year. Legal and the executive floors need that standing. | The staff offer brings polite forms with it, which Legal and the council expect. |

Japanese doesn't lock any door. What it changes is how much a place gives you: the production floor is noise until he has some words, and the bar's overheard talk gives up more each month.

## Kinds of IT tickets

The B2 whiteboard shows three to five tickets each morning, and more turn up in the world: someone flags him down in a corridor, a phone rings in the canteen, a note is left on his tray. He picks what to do. Nothing fails if it waits: unfinished tickets carry over, and a few quietly expire with a small reaction from whoever sent them.

| Kind | What it is | The choice inside it |
|---|---|---|
| Broken old machine | Copier, meal ticket machine, fax, timing board | By hand (slow, safe) or by command (fast, someone might see) |
| Migration | Replace an old system with the new one | Job progress and access, against one fewer thing that answers him. A waiver keeps it, if someone signs. |
| Accounts and access | Cards, logins, a manager who has never logged in | Mostly people and words, not machines |
| Installs and moves | New terminals, screens, the server room | Where it goes can cost someone (Kanae's darkroom, Mio's nap room) |
| Events | The festival PA, council microphones, the ekiden board | Dated and public: big crowds, big risk |
| Night calls | A server down, an alarm, a fax at 23:00 | Night places and night people (Kuro, Rei at her desk) |
| Favours | The small personal ticket someone sends at bond step 2 | Usually small, always about them |

## A day

About 10 to 15 minutes of play, one train ride. The periods are the ones in game3d/js/sim.js.

| Period | What happens |
|---|---|
| Commute | Monorail or the walk from the dorm. Who's on the train changes by weekday (Mio on Thursdays). One overheard exchange. |
| Morning | Greetings at the gate and in B2, then the ticket board. One or two tickets. |
| Lunch | The day's choice of company: canteen with Kaori's regulars, the machine room with Mio, the roof with Goro, or wherever someone invited him. |
| Afternoon | Tickets across the island, which is where new places come from. People at their open times. |
| Evening | One social place: the bar, the konbini, the university class, someone's club, or straight home. |
| Night | Dorm and save. Sometimes a night call, the laundry at 2 a.m., or the night lobby. Optional, never required. |

A normal day has one ticket somewhere new or unfinished, at most one big personal beat, and small things to notice (a need said out loud, a person with their headphones off).

## A week

| Day | What's different |
|---|---|
| Monday | Biggest ticket board. The newsletter comes out and prints the week's hooks: events, who's moved where, a photo that points somewhere new. |
| Tuesday | Council day, when there's a vote that month. |
| Wednesday | Mio leaves for her mother's after work. B2 is quieter. |
| Thursday | Mio on the morning train with pickles. Kaori's bento goes up to the executive floor. |
| Friday | The team drinks (飲み会). The bar is full, and so is the overheard talk. |
| Saturday | No tickets unless there's a night call. Beach, arcade, sports ground, the university's weekend classes, shopping street. |
| Sunday | Quiet island. Laundry, the dorm, one person at a time. Later, trips off the island. |

## The half-year

Eric's contract runs from 1 October to 31 March, which is the second half of the Japanese fiscal year. Each month has one or two fixed dates, and the personal arcs hang off them.

| Month | On the island |
|---|---|
| October | Day 1 is 1 October, also the 内定式 for next year's hires (Aoi's first beat). Sports day. First council vote. |
| November | The anniversary festival on the plaza (Yuzuki's switch-over weekend). Culture week at the university. |
| December | Year-end parties (忘年会). Bonus day. The production halls open. |
| January | The New Year ekiden on the 1st (Tsubasa). The staff offer, if his standing is there. |
| February | The quiet month for work: long evenings, the executive floors, Kanae's exhibition. The romance beats land by the end of it. |
| March | Transfers posted, contracts end, Kiyoko retires, Mio's fifth year ends. Everything comes due, and each arc ends its own way. |

## Register by place, over the game

Casual carries the first half, as GUIDE says, and the order the districts open is the order the register rises. B2, the canteen, the dorm, the roof and the bar are casual. The gate and Sales use set polite phrases, learned whole. Legal and the council use polite forms from the new year. The executive floors use keigo. No UI announces the shift. The player hears it as he moves into new places.

## What this asks of the build, later

Only once day 1 is stable:
- The places are flat-shaded rooms like the day-1 ones, joined by continuous transitions (GUIDE): the tunnels and the monorail do a lot of that joining.
- Schedules, open times, gifts, bonds and taught commands already exist in sim.js; the new pieces are the ticket board, badge lines, waivers, the calendar and the council vote.
- Repeated spaces (dorm corridors, office floors) are where the parked room generator in TODO.md would help, if it's ever unparked.
