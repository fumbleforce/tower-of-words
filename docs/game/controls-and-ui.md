# Controls and UI

The controls on desktop and phone (walk, run, use, Next, Say with Q, the mic, Give, pause) and when each is first taught; the HUD (goal box, clock, counts, markers, the Say button); the dialogue box; the panels and screens (title, pause, quick save and the saves screen, settings, day end, the build id); the camera; and how the phone and desktop layouts differ. Last checked against the game on 2026-09-29.

Elsewhere: the rules every screen has to meet (no timers, one menu per target, separate phone and desktop layouts, the design language) are in GUIDE, Visual design (with "First screen"). What the systems behind the panels do is in [systems.md](systems.md).

## Controls

| Action | Desktop | Phone |
|---|---|---|
| Walk | W A S D or the arrow keys, or click the floor | Tap the floor |
| Steer | Hold the left button on the floor and move the mouse | Hold a finger on the floor and drag |
| Run | Hold Shift while walking, or turn Caps Lock on to run all the time (Shift runs even with it off) | Double-tap the floor (that walk is run) |
| Use or talk to the target | E, Space or Enter; or click it or its marker | Tap it or its marker |
| Open the target's action menu | Click it or its marker, when it has more than one thing to do (its action and Say) | Tap it or its marker, the same |
| Next target, when several are in reach | Tab (picks the next and opens its menu), or the Next row in an open menu | The Next row in an open menu |
| Say a word | Q (can be rebound in Settings), or Say a word in the menu | Say a word in the target's menu |
| Say it into the microphone | Hold V, or the mic button at the prompt | The mic button |
| Move the story on | Click anywhere on the dialogue area, Space or Enter | Tap the dialogue area |
| The backlog | L, PageUp, the mouse wheel up, or Log on the dialogue box | Swipe down on the dialogue area, or Log |
| Auto, on or off | Y, or Auto on the dialogue box | Auto |
| Skip lines already seen | Hold Ctrl, or Skip on the dialogue box (on until it stops) | Skip |
| Hide the text and HUD | H, or Hide on the dialogue box; any click or key brings them back | Press and hold the dialogue area, or Hide; any tap brings them back |
| Hear the line again | R, or Replay on the dialogue box | Replay |
| Give a drink | The Give button next to someone, or tap the drink in the Bag | The same |
| Pause, or close a panel | Esc, or the menu button top right | The menu button |
| The map, open or close | M, or click the minimap; Esc or the close button closes it | Tap the minimap; the close button closes it |
| Quick save | F5, or Quick save in the pause menu | The save button top right, beside the menu button, or the pause menu |
| Quick load | F9, or Quick load in the pause menu | Quick load in the pause menu |
| Send feedback (local builds only) | F8, or the note button top right | The note button |
| Performance numbers on or off | F3, or Settings | Settings |

Running (Jørgen, 2026-09-30: "I'd like to have Shift button to run, and caps lock to toggle running") works for the keys, a click on the floor, steering and a click on something to use. Eric walks at 1.43 m/s and runs at 2.07 times that, about 2.96 m/s; Carina, with ?mc=carina, the same. Both default speeds are 10% faster than the previous 1.3 m/s walk and 2.691 m/s run (Jørgen, 2026-10-06). Caps Lock is read from the keyboard itself, so running is on exactly when its light is. While a text field has focus (a word to type, the feedback note), Shift and Caps Lock only change the text; a word typed with Caps Lock on still counts. When the field closes with Caps Lock still on, he runs again. On the phone a double tap on the floor runs that one walk; a double click does the same on desktop. Authored glides and NPC walks keep their own pace; scenes that use the player’s ordinary walker inherit its base speed. The walking hint on the train doesn't mention running.

Steering (Jørgen, 2026-10-04: "I want to be able to hold down mouse and guide the character along, as alternative to wasd and click"; code in game3d/js/movement/steer.js): a press on the floor first walks there like a click. Held for a moment (0.22 s) or moved a little (10 px, a finger 16 px), it becomes steering: Eric walks toward the point under the cursor or finger and follows it as it moves, at walking pace, running with Shift or Caps Lock, or when the press was the second of a double tap. He goes straight where the way is clear and the short way round where it isn't (the fountain, a desk, a doorway). Steered along a wall he turns and walks along it; steered straight into one he stands. He goes round people as on any walk, stops when he reaches the point, turns on the spot when it swings round behind him, and sets off again when it moves away. A faint teal ring sits on the floor under the cursor while steering. Letting go stops him. A press on a person, a thing or a pin uses it as a click does and never steers. The keys win while both are used. Steering ends when a scene starts, the same as the keys stop. game3d/tools/steer-check.mjs steers round the plaza fountain and across the office with a held mouse or finger, and checks that letting go stops him, that a quick click still walks, that a click or a held press on Mio still targets her, and that his feet don't slide.

A click on something he can use walks him there and uses it. If a scene starts on the way (he walks into a room with its own moment), he carries on to it and uses it when the scene ends.

Clicking while the story is busy (a walk, a door) shows that the game is waiting and hurries the scripted move along. Every attempt does something visible.

People are soft to each other and never stand inside each other or the furniture (Jørgen: "we need much better soft collision so models push gently at each other without locking up in tight spaces"). Someone walking behind another person follows at their pace instead of walking into them, and a walk goes round whoever stands in the way. People lean in and push apart gently, and slip past in a spot too tight to share. A seated person's knees and feet count as taken floor. Nobody stands on the cat, and she hops off Eric's chair before he sits. Someone left standing with a shoulder in a desk or a machine steps clear. In the lift, everyone stands inside the car's walls. The fast test fails when any of these happen.

While Eric is saying a word (its practice prompt, his voice, the answer), clicks and taps on people, things and markers, and E, are ignored, so the word isn't lost to a new talk. They work again as soon as the word is done or the prompt is cancelled. A scene that would start meanwhile (he walks into the doorway with the prompt still open) waits until the word is done (issue #81).

## When each control is taught

On the train (game3d/js/onboard.js, notes/ONBOARDING.md for the design):

- At the start the screen shows only one line on walking ("W A S D, or click or hold the floor to walk" / "Tap or hold the floor to walk"). It steps aside while a line or caption is up, goes once Eric has walked a few steps and never comes back.
- No goal and no story hints until he has talked to a person. The first passenger he talks to points at the seat beside Mio, and that gives the first goal.
- Continuing a save outside the introductory train releases any goal held by onboarding, including when the save is loaded into fresh browser storage.
- On desktop, the pin of the target in reach carries an E key cap beside it, level with the pin, until E has been used five times (Jørgen, 2026-10-04: "the E button indicator is almost to small to read"): 24 px tall at the default interface size and growing with it, a light face with a dark rim and bold letter so it reads on dark and light scenes, on the pin's left near the right edge of the screen. Phones show no key caps; that's where E is taught, since the action menu only opens when asked for. The menu's rows drop their key caps after the same five uses. Its action word (Talk, Look, Pet, Push to your desk...) always stays: a box with only the name read as having no action (Jørgen, 2026-09-30: "interaction windows but no actions").
- Say first shows only at the goal target (the cat), after Mio teaches おはようございます; after one use it shows wherever a word works. The tip that comes with the first word says "Press Q to say a word you know." on desktop and "Tap who you want to talk to, then Say a word." on the phone.
- No Next, clock, People count, mute chip or minimap on the train.
- Onboarding state is kept in the browser, so a reload doesn't teach it all again; Start on the title resets it.

Every control is introduced the first time it's needed, and ambient people never block the player's path or clicks (Jørgen).

## The HUD

- The goal box, top left: the current goal on one line, always there until the player acts, re-readable. Tips (the Say tip, story hints) are a second line inside the goal box, never a separate box (Jørgen, 2026-09-29: "Put tips inside the goal box top left so we dont get so many places for this"). A tip has a close button; tapping the goal line brings a closed hint back. A side line under the goal is used for small side goals ("The vending machine is stuck.").
- Top right: the clock (date and period), People (count), Bag (count), Words (count), Photos (found of all, "2/5"), a mute button, on the phone a quick save button, and the menu button. Each appears once it has something in it; none on the train. On a local build served by ./start, a note button for feedback sits before the menu button everywhere, including the train and in the middle of a conversation; the public build doesn't have it.
- Markers: every interactable thing on screen, not just people, carries a pin: a circle with a symbol (speech for people, a paw for the cat, an eye for things) on a short line down to it (Jørgen, 2026-09-29: "the microscopic dots are not enough to indicate interactive elements... earlier you had a line and a circle with a symbol which was better"). A pin that takes Eric to another place has its own symbol in place of the eye (Jørgen, 2026-10-05: "navigation points that take you between areas should not have an eye icon, something else"): a door for doors and building entrances (a shut shop's too), stairs, the lift's doors with up and down for the lift, an arrow for the ways out of a place ("To the plaza"), and a place marker for a spot to walk to (the train's "Free seat"). Its action is its own verb: Go, Go in, Go out, Take the stairs, Take the lift, Get off (the train's doors). Which pins are ways out, and of what kind, is one table in game3d/js/gameplay/pin-kinds.js: a thing whose verb is Go, Go in, Go out or Go to is one by its verb, the table names the others. No labels on pins; the action menu has the name. A tooltip says what a pin does (Jørgen, 2026-10-05: "when I hover an interactive eye / heart element it should have a description"): the verb and the name, as the action menu has them ("Look: Vending machine", "Talk: Mr. Mori", "Pet: Tama"), and for a way out where it goes ("Go to the plaza", "Take the lift down to B2", "Go into room 203"). It shows while the mouse is on the pin, and on a phone after a long press on it (about half a second); a tap still uses the pin as before, and the press that showed a tooltip doesn't use it. The phone's tooltip stays until the next tap anywhere. It sits over the pin, or beside it with no room above, on the action menu's slate with the verb in teal, and doesn't show while the action menu is open on the same target. The goal's pin is teal and larger. Its off-screen direction arrow stays below the rendered top HUD, including enlarged UI and wrapped hints, and keeps clear of the minimap and performance overlay. Pins further from Eric are smaller and paler, and where two would overlap only the one nearer to Eric shows. With no room above a target (under the HUD) the pin sits beside the head, never over the face. Every person with a body always has a pin and Talk (Jørgen, 2026-09-30: "All people should always be interactable, but dont necessarily need to say that much interesting"); when the story has nothing for them right now they say their idle line, or turn to Eric and nod. Things (Jørgen, 2026-09-30: "a hundred 'interactive' things in this room with symbols that dont really do anything interesting"): one with its own talk line or action, or the goal, has its pin; one whose talk is a flat one-liner, or whose only use is a word (its own joke, or the stock reply any thing gives), shows its pin only once Eric is within 2.5 m, and using one that only takes a word opens the Say menu; one with nothing to do yet (no word known) has no pin and can't be picked. Put generally (issue #128): a thing can be picked only while its menu has a row that does something now: its talk line, act or look, the goal, or Say where a word Eric knows works (before Say is first used, only at the goal). A talk entry that falls back to an empty node (the chair before the machine room is found) is no E row. game3d/tools/menu-day-check.mjs plays the day and fails on a pickable thing with no such row, or an E row that does nothing. A close-only pin never hides a full one. Things marked "no marker" in the engine show one only while they're the goal, or close by once a word of their own works on them. Which thing gets which: notes/interaction-audit.md. A goal that points at a seat or a spot (the train's "Sit by the lunchbox.") gets a goal pin of its own there ("Free seat"); tapping it walks Eric there. Tapping or clicking a visible pin does what tapping its target does; a pin hidden by a nearer one takes no taps. A private scene's pin (private mode only) wears a heart in place of the eye, on the same disc: purple for a soft scene, red for a hard one (Jørgen, 2026-10-04); the symbol's name and the pin's label come from the local plugin, so the public game never shows one. With private mode off no heart shows, and a pin a plugin marked private (a heart, or `private: true`) says only its plain verb in the tooltip and the action menu ("Look"; a person "Talk" and their name), never the plugin's label.
- The goal off screen: a teal arrow button on the screen edge toward it, with the goal's name ("To head office", "To the dorms"); tapping it walks Eric there. It never covers Eric, the people and things he can use (the plaza's fountain basin as a whole), their pins or the action menu: where the goal's own place on the edge is taken, it slides along that edge to the nearest clear spot (issue #84; game3d/tools/goal-arrow-check.mjs checks the outdoor places at phone, 1366 and 1920 sizes).
- The target in reach: its pin lights up and its line becomes a small bobbing pointer, and the model itself gets an outline; the model under the mouse gets the same outline on desktop. With several things in reach the target is the nearest, except that the current goal, when it is in reach, always wins, so E uses what the scene needs (Jørgen, 2026-10-04: in the machine room "you will always either interact with mio or the cat"); Tab still picks another. Nothing is drawn on the floor around the goal or the target, and no ring comes around the person being talked to (Jørgen, 2026-09-29: "the ring comes around any character that you are currently selecting / interacting with"; earlier the floor ring was "brutally ugly and overlaps the whole screen"). The pin and the outline are the only marks. The outline takes only the model's own meshes, never the shadow blob under a person or other helper meshes (Jørgen, 2026-09-29: it drew a big square under Mio).
- The action menu: it opens only when the player asks for it (Jørgen, 2026-10-02, on his phone: "The interaction menu opens automatically everywhere and is very disruptive, taking a lot of screen space"). Being in reach of something only lights up its pin and outlines it. A tap or click on a target (or its pin) that has more than one thing to do, its action and Say, walks Eric there and opens its menu; one with only its action is used straight away, and E, Space and Enter always use the target in reach straight away. Tab picks the next thing in reach and opens its menu. The menu closes when the target changes, on any tap outside it, on a walk key or Esc, and when a row is used. It sits beside the target's pin, never over Eric, the target or the pin: right of the pin, else left, else above it, else under the target, clamped to the screen, under the HUD row and clear of the goal box. Inside: the name on top, then one row per action, with a key cap and the label in one type style, and a small pointer toward the pin (Jørgen: "the interaction box is also not very pretty, not well designed"). Phone rows are compact (40 px, no key caps). One menu per target, with interact and Say stacked. game3d/tools/act-menu-check.mjs stands Eric at every pinned thing in the train, office and plaza and fails if a menu shows, then taps a pin and checks the menu that opens. Say is in the menu whenever a word Eric knows does something there now: a word the story reacts to for that person or thing, or any word at all on a thing that has nothing else to do (the office fridge answers every word with a line of its own; Jørgen, 2026-09-30: "you just see 'Fridge' with no attached action even though you CAN say something to it"). Such a thing shows its name and Say, with no E row. Say in the menu, and Q, speak to the thing in reach (the one the menu is on) (game3d/tools/say-menu-check.mjs lists every place's things and their rows).
- The Say button: lights up when the goal answers to a word Eric knows. The Say menu lists his phrases and commands with their icon, Japanese, reading and English, and the practice dots ([systems.md](systems.md)).
- The minimap, bottom left (Jørgen picked 5b and north up on Review minimap-plan-1, 2026-10-05; code in game3d/js/ui/minimap.js, css/map.css): a rounded square, 180 px on desktop at interface size 1 (it grows with the interface size) and 88 px on the phone, showing the same pale paths, blue-grey roofs, sage planting and blue coast as the full map, with an N marker for north and his teal arrow turning with him; every place has a small outlined dot (dark: been there, grey: not yet, muted: not open today) and the goal as a teal dot, held on the minimap's edge when the goal is further away. A small tag in its corner says "Map", with an M key cap on desktop. A tap or a click on it opens the map. It hides on day 1's train, in the train's first minutes, while a line or a prompt is up (on the phone the dialogue box comes up there, on desktop the portraits), with the HUD (H), and at the title and the day's end. The goal arrow on the screen edge keeps clear of it.
- The LED board at the top of the train car shows the station announcement.
- Emotes over heads (!, ?, …, ♪, heart, sweat, zzz) are big (Jørgen, playtest: "emotes much bigger").
- What is clicked can't be selected as text (Jørgen, 2026-10-05: "the text in the interface should not be highlightable, it is clickable gui. Other text inside modals etc should still be highlightable"): every button and tab, the HUD row and goal box, the pins and their tooltips, the action menu, the LED board and the lift's floor display. Text inside panels and dialogs can be selected.

## The dialogue box

- Ambient captions use the space above an open conversation and any portrait that crosses their width. They keep clear of the player when there is room. If a close view leaves no clear row, keeping the caption on screen takes priority. They move with growing choices and screen resizing, then return to the bottom when the conversation closes.
- Direction 1, "faded" (Stage light, game3d/design/DIALOGUE-OVERLAY.md), approved by Jørgen, with a more solid grey band at the bottom on phone so the portraits' cut edge sits on solid colour.
- The protagonist (Eric, or Carina) stands on the left facing right, and the person they talk with stands on the right facing left, both turned towards the text (Jørgen, 2026-10-09: "put MC on the left, facing towards the chat, with opposing character on the right facing left to the chat"). A portrait whose picture looks the other way is shown mirrored; which way each one looks is `FACING` in game3d/js/ui/portrait-data.js. On desktop both stand on the bottom edge either side of the text and the listener dims; on a phone only the speaker shows, smaller, above the box on their own side, moved in from the edge when needed so the whole picture stays on screen. With three or more people, whoever spoke last besides the protagonist holds the right. game3d/tools/dialogue-sides.mjs checks the sides and mirroring for the whole cast. Narration has no portrait. While a word to type or a choice waits on the player, no portrait covers Eric, or the thing he's saying the word to: a portrait that would goes to the other side, else shows smaller, else fades out, and it comes back when the lines go on (issue #76; game3d/tools/prompt-shots.mjs shoots and checks every prompt of the day). The faces are in [cast.md](cast.md).
- A new speaker's portrait never opens on the last person talked to (Jørgen, 2026-10-02, Galaxy S23 in Chrome: "Whenever i talk to someone new, i first initially see the last person i talked to"). Their side stays empty until their picture has loaded, then it fades in; a new expression of the person already showing swaps in once it has loaded. Everyone's neutral picture loads a few seconds after start, and each scene loads the pictures of everyone who speaks in it as it begins. game3d/tools/portrait-swap-check.mjs checks it with the picture files slowed down.
- The name plate and role sit above the text. Japanese words show with reading and English; taught words can be clicked to hear them.
- The whole dialogue area moves the story on, including the empty space below the text; "Click to continue" shows for the first few lines. The HUD buttons (Words, People, Bag, sound, the menu) stay usable while a line is up, and tapping them doesn't move the story on. Eric's spoken lines finish before anyone replies (Jørgen, playtest).
- Choices: the buttons name their object ("Lift the briefcase over the gate"). Word tokens in replies show the same Japanese, reading and meaning as spoken lines. Learning a word is typing it, not clicking a choice. On the phone the buttons come up dimmed and take taps only after a moment, so a tap meant for the line before doesn't pick one.
- Mio's texts show as phone messages on their own dark card, with her portrait beside it as on her spoken lines, looking at her phone (Jørgen, 2026-10-04: "when showing mios message, should have her portrait too"). Rei's texts would show hers the same way. When the messages end (the box closes after them) her card, name and portrait go with it, and when a scene ends the box is emptied, so replies that open the next scene never show the last speaker (Jørgen, 2026-10-05, day 3 at the desk: "mio is still showing in the dialogue after she sent messages before"). Within a scene, replies with no line of their own keep the line before them, as Mori's line stays over his ski-jump replies.
- A shut door's card: the shop doors face across their streets, so from the play camera their cards are edge-on. When Eric tries a door with a card, the narration line that reads it has a picture of the card beside the box (desktop: right of the box; phone and narrow windows: above it on the right): the card as it hangs now (準備中, 本日休業, the onsen's notice, the izakaya's 本日貸切), the kana large, the English small. It goes with the line. Code: game3d/js/ui/door-card.js, the cards by door in game3d/js/scenes/door-cards.js.
- Something a person holds up for Eric to see (a phone's photo, video or reminder; pages side by side) shows large, top centre, in a phone frame or as sheets, clear of the text and portraits, while the lines about it are up. It never takes a tap.
- Japanese spoken with an English subtitle ([systems.md](systems.md), Subtitled Japanese): the name plate says "in Japanese" and the line is the English.
- The row on the box (Jørgen agreed to these on 2026-10-04): five small buttons at the top right of the dialogue box, Log, Auto, Skip, Hide and Replay, in the HUD's slate with Mio's teal for what is on (code: game3d/js/ui/vn-controls.js, css/vn.css). They come in after the first few lines of a player's first time (the first screen stays clear), then show on every line from then on, and not over a word to type. On a phone each is a 44 px target. The speaker name and role stack in a separate column beside the controls; long roles are shortened with an ellipsis while names can wrap. Their keys and a controller's buttons are in the table above; a controller uses A to move on, B for Esc, X for Auto, Y for the backlog, LB to replay, RB held to skip and Back to hide.
- The backlog keeps the most recent 400 conversation entries across days and Continue, oldest first, opening at the newest. Each spoken line retains its raw text, original day, period, place and story node, plus the vocabulary known when heard; older saves keep their recorded day without inventing missing provenance. Reopening renders Japanese with the player’s current vocabulary, so a word learned later can reveal part of an earlier conversation. The list shows each line’s original day and period, speaker, role and face; texts are marked as messages, narration is italic, and picked replies remain under “You chose”. Voice replay and word playback work as in the dialogue box. Esc, the close button, a tap outside, or scrolling past the end closes it. Loading a different save replaces the list with that save’s conversations; starting a new game clears it. The oldest entries leave when the limit is reached: this is a rolling conversation history, not permanent storage for story clues. Code: game3d/js/ui/backlog.js and backlog-records.js.
- Remembered remarks survive the rolling log limit. When learning changes a remark’s readable words, its log entry offers “What I understood then”: an optional text comparison using the vocabulary recorded at the original hearing. Names and original contextual glosses remain readable in both versions; unlearned words remain obscured. The ordinary reading and voice replay use current knowledge. An older save without a vocabulary snapshot has no historical comparison. Opening this view never teaches a word or changes a conversation’s eligibility. In the log, Tab/Shift-Tab moves between controls and Enter/Space activates one. Controller left/right selects controls, A activates, up/down scrolls, and B closes.
- Auto: off at every start, and only ever on when the player turns it on (GUIDE, No text on timers). While on, the Auto button is teal with its name written out and the continue arrow is teal. A line moves on once it's written out and its voice has finished, or, with no voice, after a reading time from Settings, Auto speed. It lasts through learning scenes: it waits at replies and word prompts (it never answers them) and carries on after them, and the taps and keys that answer there (picking a reply, typing the word, tapping the field, the mic or the word's play button) don't stop it (Jørgen, 2026-10-05: "the autoplay should survive learning scenes"). Any other tap or key (except its own) stops it at once, and the tap that stops it doesn't move the line on. game3d/tools/auto-learn-check.mjs plays the train scene that teaches gaijin with Auto on, at a size, with the setting below off or on. It ends with the conversation, and doesn't move on while the text is hidden or the backlog is open.
- Skip: runs through lines already seen (on any earlier play: which lines have been seen is kept in the browser, across saves), each shown for a moment and not spoken, and hurries the walks and moves between them. It stops at replies, word prompts and the first line not seen before (Settings, Skip unread too, lets it run through those as well, still stopping at replies and prompts), and ends with the conversation. It holds while the game is paused, and a line it was about to pass waits for a tap if Skip was turned off meanwhile. Ctrl held does the same while held. While on, the Skip button is teal with its name written out.
- Hide: the dialogue box, the portraits and the HUD go so the scene can be seen; a small note at the bottom says how to bring them back, and any tap, click or key does, without moving the line on.
- Replay: plays the current line's voice again; dimmed on a line with no voice.

## Panels and screens

Panels open above everything in the HUD, the goal arrow and the Say button included.

- People: everyone Eric has met (nobody he hasn't), with their bond step as five hearts filled up to it, its name (Known, Friendly...), a short line on where things stand ("You've met. Still getting to know each other.", "Friendly. Glad to see you around."), what they remember and what he has learned about them ([systems.md](systems.md), Bonds). It shows the step only, never what the next one needs or opens. Each card leads with the person's face in a circle ringed in their colour (Jørgen, 2026-10-05: "people panel should have portraits"), the same face crop of their neutral portrait the dialogue log uses, so a private-mode stand-in shows there too; their initial shows while it loads and for anyone without a portrait yet. The pictures load only when the panel opens. Code: game3d/js/ui/people-view.js.
- Bag: his yen and the drinks he's carrying. With someone in reach, tapping a drink gives it to them; otherwise the panel says to walk up to someone.
- Photos: the album on Eric's phone: the day's photos as prints (empty frames for the ones not found) and the papers he kept; tapping one shows it up close ([systems.md](systems.md), Finds).
- The ticket app (Jørgen, 2026-10-03, first "the Windows 95 simple ticketing system", then "I regret pure win 95, think japanese interfaces, corporate bad but simple design. Some japanese here, and learnable"): when the story sits Eric at a computer, the screen becomes that computer showing Amakawa's in-house repair ticket system, a plain company web page in the style of a big Japanese firm's internal tools. On desktop a browser's address strip (http://intra.amakawa.co.jp/shuri/list.do) runs across the top over a grey backdrop, with the page centred under it. The page has a blue header with the red 天川 company mark, AMAKAWA GROUP, "Repair Tickets Ver.2.03" and a 閉じる Close button; an info strip with "B2 IT · Eric", "Paid to you" (what the closed tickets have paid) and the date; a breadcrumb (Top › Repair tickets › T-0002); then the list under a heading with a blue bar on its left ("Tickets", with the count of tickets and open ones). ### Time controls

Click the date/period chip in the top HUD, on desktop or phone, to open the time menu. Its clock icon and text share a centre line. From day 3, after the morning messages, choose a named later period (one, two or three periods ahead). The menu pauses play; Tab/arrows move between buttons, Enter or Space chooses, and Escape or a tap outside closes without changing time. Closing returns focus to the clock. A conversation or action must finish first. Days 1 and 2 explain that time follows the current goal and repeat that goal; evening directs the player to Sleep in their room. Waiting uses the same schedules, light and autosave as the room desk, without a fee or a new day.
