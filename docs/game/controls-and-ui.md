# Controls and UI

The controls on desktop and phone (walk, run, use, Next, Say with Q, the mic, Give, pause) and when each is first taught; the HUD (goal box, clock, counts, markers, the Say button); the dialogue box; the panels and screens (title, pause, quick save and the saves screen, settings, day end, the build id); the camera; and how the phone and desktop layouts differ. Last checked against the game on 2026-09-29.

Elsewhere: the rules every screen has to meet (no timers, one menu per target, separate phone and desktop layouts, the design language) are in GUIDE, Visual design (with "First screen"). What the systems behind the panels do is in [systems.md](systems.md).

## Controls

| Action | Desktop | Phone |
|---|---|---|
| Walk | W A S D or the arrow keys, or click the floor | Tap the floor |
| Steer | Hold the left button on the floor and move the mouse | Hold a finger on the floor and drag |
| Run | Hold Shift while walking, or turn Caps Lock on to run all the time (Shift runs even with it off) | Double-tap the floor (that walk is run) |
| Use or talk to the target | E, Space or Enter; or click it or its marker | Tap it or its marker |
| Open the target's action menu | Click it or its marker, when it has more than one thing to do (its action and Say) | Tap it or its marker, the same |
| Next target, when several are in reach | Tab (picks the next and opens its menu), or the Next row in an open menu | The Next row in an open menu |
| Say a word | Q (can be rebound in Settings), or Say a word in the menu | Say a word in the target's menu |
| Say it into the microphone | Hold V, or the mic button at the prompt | The mic button |
| Move the story on | Click anywhere on the dialogue area, Space or Enter | Tap the dialogue area |
| The backlog | L, PageUp, the mouse wheel up, or Log on the dialogue box | Swipe down on the dialogue area, or Log |
| Auto, on or off | Y, or Auto on the dialogue box | Auto |
| Skip lines already seen | Hold Ctrl, or Skip on the dialogue box (on until it stops) | Skip |
| Hide the text and HUD | H, or Hide on the dialogue box; any click or key brings them back | Press and hold the dialogue area, or Hide; any tap brings them back |
| Hear the line again | R, or Replay on the dialogue box | Replay |
| Give a drink | The Give button next to someone, or tap the drink in the Bag | The same |
| Pause, or close a panel | Esc, or the menu button top right | The menu button |
| Quick save | F5, or Quick save in the pause menu | The save button top right, beside the menu button, or the pause menu |
| Quick load | F9, or Quick load in the pause menu | Quick load in the pause menu |
| Send feedback (local builds only) | F8, or the note button top right | The note button |
| Performance numbers on or off | F3, or Settings | Settings |

Running (Jørgen, 2026-09-30: "I'd like to have Shift button to run, and caps lock to toggle running") works for the keys, a click on the floor, steering and a click on something to use. Eric walks at 1.3 m/s and runs at 2.07 times that, about 2.7 m/s (Jørgen, 2026-10-05: "running should be a bit faster"; it was 1.8 times, 2.3 m/s); Carina, with ?mc=carina, the same. Caps Lock is read from the keyboard itself, so running is on exactly when its light is. While a text field has focus (a word to type, the feedback note), Shift and Caps Lock only change the text; a word typed with Caps Lock on still counts. When the field closes with Caps Lock still on, he runs again. On the phone a double tap on the floor runs that one walk; a double click does the same on desktop. Walks the story makes (trips, scenes) keep their own pace. The walking hint on the train doesn't mention running.

Steering (Jørgen, 2026-10-04: "I want to be able to hold down mouse and guide the character along, as alternative to wasd and click"; code in game3d/js/movement/steer.js): a press on the floor first walks there like a click. Held for a moment (0.22 s) or moved a little (10 px, a finger 16 px), it becomes steering: Eric walks toward the point under the cursor or finger and follows it as it moves, at walking pace, running with Shift or Caps Lock, or when the press was the second of a double tap. He goes straight where the way is clear and the short way round where it isn't (the fountain, a desk, a doorway). Steered along a wall he turns and walks along it; steered straight into one he stands. He goes round people as on any walk, stops when he reaches the point, turns on the spot when it swings round behind him, and sets off again when it moves away. A faint teal ring sits on the floor under the cursor while steering. Letting go stops him. A press on a person, a thing or a pin uses it as a click does and never steers. The keys win while both are used. Steering ends when a scene starts, the same as the keys stop. game3d/tools/steer-check.mjs steers round the plaza fountain and across the office with a held mouse or finger, and checks that letting go stops him, that a quick click still walks, that a click or a held press on Mio still targets her, and that his feet don't slide.

A click on something he can use walks him there and uses it. If a scene starts on the way (he walks into a room with its own moment), he carries on to it and uses it when the scene ends.

Clicking while the story is busy (a walk, a door) shows that the game is waiting and hurries the scripted move along. Every attempt does something visible.

People are soft to each other and never stand inside each other or the furniture (Jørgen: "we need much better soft collision so models push gently at each other without locking up in tight spaces"). Someone walking behind another person follows at their pace instead of walking into them, and a walk goes round whoever stands in the way. People lean in and push apart gently, and slip past in a spot too tight to share. A seated person's knees and feet count as taken floor. Nobody stands on the cat, and she hops off Eric's chair before he sits. Someone left standing with a shoulder in a desk or a machine steps clear. In the lift, everyone stands inside the car's walls. The fast test fails when any of these happen.

While Eric is saying a word (its practice prompt, his voice, the answer), clicks and taps on people, things and markers, and E, are ignored, so the word isn't lost to a new talk. They work again as soon as the word is done or the prompt is cancelled. A scene that would start meanwhile (he walks into the doorway with the prompt still open) waits until the word is done (issue #81).

## When each control is taught

On the train (game3d/js/onboard.js, notes/ONBOARDING.md for the design):

- At the start the screen shows only one line on walking ("W A S D, or click or hold the floor to walk" / "Tap or hold the floor to walk"). It steps aside while a line or caption is up, goes once Eric has walked a few steps and never comes back.
- No goal and no story hints until he has talked to a person. The first passenger he talks to points at the seat beside Mio, and that gives the first goal.
- On desktop, the pin of the target in reach carries an E key cap beside it, level with the pin, until E has been used five times (Jørgen, 2026-10-04: "the E button indicator is almost to small to read"): 24 px tall at the default interface size and growing with it, a light face with a dark rim and bold letter so it reads on dark and light scenes, on the pin's left near the right edge of the screen. Phones show no key caps; that's where E is taught, since the action menu only opens when asked for. The menu's rows drop their key caps after the same five uses. Its action word (Talk, Look, Pet, Push to your desk...) always stays: a box with only the name read as having no action (Jørgen, 2026-09-30: "interaction windows but no actions").
- Say first shows only at the goal target (the cat), after Mio teaches おはようございます; after one use it shows wherever a word works. The tip that comes with the first word says "Press Q to say a word you know." on desktop and "Tap who you want to talk to, then Say a word." on the phone.
- No Next, clock, People count or mute chip on the train.
- Onboarding state is kept in the browser, so a reload doesn't teach it all again; Start on the title resets it.

Every control is introduced the first time it's needed, and ambient people never block the player's path or clicks (Jørgen).

## The HUD

- The goal box, top left: the current goal on one line, always there until the player acts, re-readable. Tips (the Say tip, story hints) are a second line inside the goal box, never a separate box (Jørgen, 2026-09-29: "Put tips inside the goal box top left so we dont get so many places for this"). A tip has a close button; tapping the goal line brings a closed hint back. A side line under the goal is used for small side goals ("The vending machine is stuck.").
- Top right: the clock (date and period), People (count), Bag (count), Words (count), Photos (found of all, "2/5"), a mute button, on the phone a quick save button, and the menu button. Each appears once it has something in it; none on the train. On a local build served by ./start, a note button for feedback sits before the menu button everywhere, including the train and in the middle of a conversation; the public build doesn't have it.
- Markers: every interactable thing on screen, not just people, carries a pin: a circle with a symbol (speech for people, a paw for the cat, an eye for things) on a short line down to it (Jørgen, 2026-09-29: "the microscopic dots are not enough to indicate interactive elements... earlier you had a line and a circle with a symbol which was better"). No labels on pins; the action menu has the name. The goal's pin is teal and larger. Pins further from Eric are smaller and paler, and where two would overlap only the one nearer to Eric shows. With no room above a target (under the HUD) the pin sits beside the head, never over the face. Every person with a body always has a pin and Talk (Jørgen, 2026-09-30: "All people should always be interactable, but dont necessarily need to say that much interesting"); when the story has nothing for them right now they say their idle line, or turn to Eric and nod. Things (Jørgen, 2026-09-30: "a hundred 'interactive' things in this room with symbols that dont really do anything interesting"): one with its own talk line or action, or the goal, has its pin; one whose talk is a flat one-liner, or whose only use is a word (its own joke, or the stock reply any thing gives), shows its pin only once Eric is within 2.5 m, and using one that only takes a word opens the Say menu; one with nothing to do yet (no word known) has no pin and can't be picked. Put generally (issue #128): a thing can be picked only while its menu has a row that does something now: its talk line, act or look, the goal, or Say where a word Eric knows works (before Say is first used, only at the goal). A talk entry that falls back to an empty node (the chair before the machine room is found) is no E row. game3d/tools/menu-day-check.mjs plays the day and fails on a pickable thing with no such row, or an E row that does nothing. A close-only pin never hides a full one. Things marked "no marker" in the engine show one only while they're the goal, or close by once a word of their own works on them. Which thing gets which: notes/interaction-audit.md. A goal that points at a seat or a spot (the train's "Sit by the lunchbox.") gets a goal pin of its own there ("Free seat"); tapping it walks Eric there. Tapping or clicking a visible pin does what tapping its target does; a pin hidden by a nearer one takes no taps. A private scene's pin (private mode only) wears a heart in place of the eye, on the same disc: purple for a soft scene, red for a hard one (Jørgen, 2026-10-04); the symbol's name comes from the local plugin, so the public game never shows one.
- The goal off screen: a teal arrow button on the screen edge toward it, with the goal's name ("To head office", "To the dorms"); tapping it walks Eric there. It never covers Eric, the people and things he can use (the plaza's fountain basin as a whole), their pins or the action menu: where the goal's own place on the edge is taken, it slides along that edge to the nearest clear spot (issue #84; game3d/tools/goal-arrow-check.mjs checks the outdoor places at phone, 1366 and 1920 sizes).
- The target in reach: its pin lights up and its line becomes a small bobbing pointer, and the model itself gets an outline; the model under the mouse gets the same outline on desktop. With several things in reach the target is the nearest, except that the current goal, when it is in reach, always wins, so E uses what the scene needs (Jørgen, 2026-10-04: in the machine room "you will always either interact with mio or the cat"); Tab still picks another. Nothing is drawn on the floor around the goal or the target, and no ring comes around the person being talked to (Jørgen, 2026-09-29: "the ring comes around any character that you are currently selecting / interacting with"; earlier the floor ring was "brutally ugly and overlaps the whole screen"). The pin and the outline are the only marks. The outline takes only the model's own meshes, never the shadow blob under a person or other helper meshes (Jørgen, 2026-09-29: it drew a big square under Mio).
- The action menu: it opens only when the player asks for it (Jørgen, 2026-10-02, on his phone: "The interaction menu opens automatically everywhere and is very disruptive, taking a lot of screen space"). Being in reach of something only lights up its pin and outlines it. A tap or click on a target (or its pin) that has more than one thing to do, its action and Say, walks Eric there and opens its menu; one with only its action is used straight away, and E, Space and Enter always use the target in reach straight away. Tab picks the next thing in reach and opens its menu. The menu closes when the target changes, on any tap outside it, on a walk key or Esc, and when a row is used. It sits beside the target's pin, never over Eric, the target or the pin: right of the pin, else left, else above it, else under the target, clamped to the screen, under the HUD row and clear of the goal box. Inside: the name on top, then one row per action, with a key cap and the label in one type style, and a small pointer toward the pin (Jørgen: "the interaction box is also not very pretty, not well designed"). Phone rows are compact (40 px, no key caps). One menu per target, with interact and Say stacked. game3d/tools/act-menu-check.mjs stands Eric at every pinned thing in the train, office and plaza and fails if a menu shows, then taps a pin and checks the menu that opens. Say is in the menu whenever a word Eric knows does something there now: a word the story reacts to for that person or thing, or any word at all on a thing that has nothing else to do (the office fridge answers every word with a line of its own; Jørgen, 2026-09-30: "you just see 'Fridge' with no attached action even though you CAN say something to it"). Such a thing shows its name and Say, with no E row. Say in the menu, and Q, speak to the thing in reach (the one the menu is on) (game3d/tools/say-menu-check.mjs lists every place's things and their rows).
- The Say button: lights up when the goal answers to a word Eric knows. The Say menu lists his phrases and commands with their icon, Japanese, reading and English, and the practice dots ([systems.md](systems.md)).
- The LED board at the top of the train car shows the station announcement.
- Emotes over heads (!, ?, …, ♪, heart, sweat, zzz) are big (Jørgen, playtest: "emotes much bigger").

## The dialogue box

- Direction 1, "faded" (Stage light, game3d/design/DIALOGUE-OVERLAY.md), approved by Jørgen, with a more solid grey band at the bottom on phone so the portraits' cut edge sits on solid colour.
- The speaker's portrait stands beside the box (desktop: large on the left; phone: smaller, above the box, and moved in from the side when needed so the whole picture stays on screen). Eric's shows smaller on the right on his lines, and the listener dims. Narration has no portrait. While a word to type or a choice waits on the player, no portrait covers Eric, or the thing he's saying the word to: a portrait that would goes to the other side, else shows smaller, else fades out, and it comes back when the lines go on (issue #76; game3d/tools/prompt-shots.mjs shoots and checks every prompt of the day). The faces are in [cast.md](cast.md).
- A new speaker's portrait never opens on the last person talked to (Jørgen, 2026-10-02, Galaxy S23 in Chrome: "Whenever i talk to someone new, i first initially see the last person i talked to"). Their side stays empty until their picture has loaded, then it fades in; a new expression of the person already showing swaps in once it has loaded. Everyone's neutral picture loads a few seconds after start, and each scene loads the pictures of everyone who speaks in it as it begins. game3d/tools/portrait-swap-check.mjs checks it with the picture files slowed down.
- The name plate and role sit above the text. Japanese words show with reading and English; taught words can be clicked to hear them.
- The whole dialogue area moves the story on, including the empty space below the text; "Click to continue" shows for the first few lines. The HUD buttons (Words, People, Bag, sound, the menu) stay usable while a line is up, and tapping them doesn't move the story on. Eric's spoken lines finish before anyone replies (Jørgen, playtest).
- Choices: the buttons name their object ("Lift the briefcase over the gate"). Learning a word is typing it, not clicking a choice. On the phone the buttons come up dimmed and take taps only after a moment, so a tap meant for the line before doesn't pick one.
- Mio's texts show as phone messages on their own dark card, with her portrait beside it as on her spoken lines, looking at her phone (Jørgen, 2026-10-04: "when showing mios message, should have her portrait too"). Rei's texts would show hers the same way. When the messages end (the box closes after them) her card, name and portrait go with it, and when a scene ends the box is emptied, so replies that open the next scene never show the last speaker (Jørgen, 2026-10-05, day 3 at the desk: "mio is still showing in the dialogue after she sent messages before"). Within a scene, replies with no line of their own keep the line before them, as Mori's line stays over his ski-jump replies.
- A shut door's card: the shop doors face across their streets, so from the play camera their cards are edge-on. When Eric tries a door with a card, the narration line that reads it has a picture of the card beside the box (desktop: right of the box; phone and narrow windows: above it on the right): the card as it hangs now (準備中, 本日休業, the onsen's notice, the izakaya's 本日貸切), the kana large, the English small. It goes with the line. Code: game3d/js/ui/door-card.js, the cards by door in game3d/js/scenes/door-cards.js.
- Something a person holds up for Eric to see (a phone's photo, video or reminder; pages side by side) shows large, top centre, in a phone frame or as sheets, clear of the text and portraits, while the lines about it are up. It never takes a tap.
- Japanese spoken with an English subtitle ([systems.md](systems.md), Subtitled Japanese): the name plate says "in Japanese" and the line is the English.
- The row on the box (Jørgen agreed to these on 2026-10-04): five small buttons at the top right of the dialogue box, Log, Auto, Skip, Hide and Replay, in the HUD's slate with Mio's teal for what is on (code: game3d/js/ui/vn-controls.js, css/vn.css). They come in after the first few lines of a player's first time (the first screen stays clear), then show on every line from then on, and not over a word to type. On a phone each is a 44 px target. Their keys and a controller's buttons are in the table above; a controller uses A to move on, B for Esc, X for Auto, Y for the backlog, LB to replay, RB held to skip and Back to hide.
- The backlog: every line said today, oldest at the top and the newest at the bottom where it opens: the speaker's name, role and a small round face, the line as the box showed it (Japanese with its reading and English, overheard Japanese blurred as it was), Mio's and Rei's texts marked as messages, narration in italics, and each reply Eric picked under "You chose". A line with a voice has a play button on its right that plays it again, and taught words play when tapped, as in the box. Lines he had already seen on an earlier play are a little quieter. It closes with Esc, the close button, a tap outside the list, or scrolling down past its end. It comes back with a save or Continue for the same day, and starts empty on a new day. Code: game3d/js/ui/backlog.js.
- Auto: off at every start, and only ever on when the player turns it on (GUIDE, No text on timers). While on, the Auto button is teal with its name written out and the continue arrow is teal. A line moves on once it's written out and its voice has finished, or, with no voice, after a reading time from Settings, Auto speed. It lasts through learning scenes: it waits at replies and word prompts (it never answers them) and carries on after them, and the taps and keys that answer there (picking a reply, typing the word, tapping the field, the mic or the word's play button) don't stop it (Jørgen, 2026-10-05: "the autoplay should survive learning scenes"). Any other tap or key (except its own) stops it at once, and the tap that stops it doesn't move the line on. game3d/tools/auto-learn-check.mjs plays the train scene that teaches gaijin with Auto on, at a size, with the setting below off or on. It ends with the conversation, and doesn't move on while the text is hidden or the backlog is open.
- Skip: runs through lines already seen (on any earlier play: which lines have been seen is kept in the browser, across saves), each shown for a moment and not spoken, and hurries the walks and moves between them. It stops at replies, word prompts and the first line not seen before (Settings, Skip unread too, lets it run through those as well, still stopping at replies and prompts), and ends with the conversation. It holds while the game is paused, and a line it was about to pass waits for a tap if Skip was turned off meanwhile. Ctrl held does the same while held. While on, the Skip button is teal with its name written out.
- Hide: the dialogue box, the portraits and the HUD go so the scene can be seen; a small note at the bottom says how to bring them back, and any tap, click or key does, without moving the line on.
- Replay: plays the current line's voice again; dimmed on a line with no voice.

## Panels and screens

Panels open above everything in the HUD, the goal arrow and the Say button included.

- People: everyone Eric has met (nobody he hasn't), with their bond step as five hearts filled up to it, its name (Known, Friendly...), a short line on where things stand ("You've met. Still getting to know each other.", "Friendly. Glad to see you around."), what they remember and what he has learned about them ([systems.md](systems.md), Bonds). It shows the step only, never what the next one needs or opens. Code: game3d/js/ui/people-view.js.
- Bag: his yen and the drinks he's carrying. With someone in reach, tapping a drink gives it to them; otherwise the panel says to walk up to someone.
- Photos: the album on Eric's phone: the day's photos as prints (empty frames for the ones not found) and the papers he kept; tapping one shows it up close ([systems.md](systems.md), Finds).
- The ticket app (Jørgen, 2026-10-03, first "the Windows 95 simple ticketing system", then "I regret pure win 95, think japanese interfaces, corporate bad but simple design. Some japanese here, and learnable"): when the story sits Eric at a computer, the screen becomes that computer showing Amakawa's in-house repair ticket system, a plain company web page in the style of a big Japanese firm's internal tools. On desktop a browser's address strip (http://intra.amakawa.co.jp/shuri/list.do) runs across the top over a grey backdrop, with the page centred under it. The page has a blue header with the red 天川 company mark, AMAKAWA GROUP, "Repair Tickets Ver.2.03" and a 閉じる Close button; an info strip with "B2 IT · Eric", "Paid to you" (what the closed tickets have paid) and the date; a breadcrumb (Top › Repair tickets › T-0002); then the list under a heading with a blue bar on its left ("Tickets", with the count of tickets and open ones). The list is a thin-bordered table with zebra rows and the columns No., 件名 Subject, 依頼者 From and 状態 Status; unread tickets are bold, the chosen one pale yellow with an amber edge, and each status is a small coloured badge: 未対応 New in red, 対応中 In progress in blue, 完了 Done in grey. The ticket shows under the list as a form table (labels in a blue-grey column on the left): 件名, 依頼者, 状態, 報酬 Pay (with a green Paid once it's closed), Details with its text, and under it a blue 担当する Take ticket button while it's new. A copyright line ends the page. The Japanese labels and how tapping them teaches them: [words.md](words.md), Labels on the ticket system. On the phone it shows one pane at a time, without the address strip: the list as rows of number and badge, subject, and who sent it, then the ticket alone with 戻る Back in the breadcrumb; every row, button and tappable label is at least 44 px tall. The first time, a pale yellow notice over the list says "Tap a ticket to read it." (desktop: "Click"), and on a new ticket one by 担当する says "Take ticket puts it on your list. It doesn't have to be done today." Each goes once used. 閉じる and Esc close it; closing gives the screen back and Eric stands up. Arrow keys move through the list; picking a ticket keeps the keyboard in the app (on the chosen row, or on the phone's ticket, its Take ticket or Back button). What tickets are: [systems.md](systems.md), Tickets.
- Close looks: a photo or paper when he picks it up or taps it in the album, and a notice board's posts pinned on the board when he reads it. They cover the view with the dark scrim; any tap or Esc closes them.
- Words: the words he can say, each with its dictionary form and practice dots, and a note on the -te form ([words.md](words.md)). When he learns a word, the Words chip pops once (it grows a little and settles in about a third of a second) and its border lights up in the accent teal and fades over a second; the count goes up with it.
- Title: the train seen from outside at dawn, with no train rattle under it, drawn at up to 30 frames a second (play runs at the screen's rate). On Start the camera flies into the car. Continue when there's a save. Continue, a loaded slot, quick load and Days never fly into the car: they go straight to the saved place with the loading chip and a crossfade (after a reload, behind the boot loader). Days, under them, opens a short list in place of the menu ("Start from another day." with Day 2, Day 3 and Back); a day starts the way ?day=N does: from the player's own finished day before if the autosave is one, otherwise from a plain one. If the autosave is a game still in progress, the title first asks, "Start day 3? It replaces your game in progress." (for the day picked), with Start day 3 and Back. The save slots are never touched.
- Pause (Esc): Resume, Quick save, Quick load, Save, Load, Settings, Quit to title. On desktop the two quick rows carry their keys (F5, F9). Quick load is dimmed until there is a quick save, Load until there is any save. What loading restores: [systems.md](systems.md), Saving.
- Quick save (Jørgen agreed 2026-10-04): F5 on desktop, the save button in the phone's HUD (top right, before the menu button; desktop has no button), or the pause menu. It works anywhere Eric is in a place, in a conversation too, and puts up the notice "Quick saved". In the middle of a conversation or a scene the notice adds "Loading it starts this conversation again from the beginning." (or "this scene"), because a save restores a running scene from its start (systems.md, Saving). From the pause menu it closes the menu. Quick load (F9 or the pause menu) loads the one quick slot; at the title F9 loads it straight away.
- Load confirm: loading a quick save or a slot in play first asks, in a small dialog in the game's style, "Load your quick save?" (or "Load Slot 5?") with the place, day, period and when it was saved, "Anything you've done since your last save is lost.", and Load and Keep playing (focus on Keep playing). It asks only when the game has progress no slot holds (story, words, people, money, Bag, the clock, the place, the goal); at the title there is nothing to lose and it loads at once. Esc or a tap outside is Keep playing.
- Saves (Save and Load in the pause menu; Continue on the title; code game3d/js/saves/, css/saves.css): one screen for both. In play its header has Save and Load tabs; at the title it is Continue, load only. Save lists the quick slot, the autosave (dimmed: "Saves by itself at each new place") and slots 1 to 12; Load lists the saves there are. Each card has the picture taken when it was saved, the slot's name (Quick save, Autosave, Slot 5) and the real date and time ("Today 14:05"), the place, the day and period ("Day 1 · Lunch") and the goal, or the line on screen when there was no goal. Saving over a save asks first ("Replace Slot 5?", Replace and Cancel); the card then says "Saved to Slot 5." under the list and flashes once. Desktop: a centred panel, two columns of cards, at most 880 × 600 px at desktop scale and never bigger than the screen at any interface size; phone: a sheet from the bottom, one column, at most 88 % of the screen. The list scrolls inside, under the header. Keys: arrows move through the cards (Up and Down by row), Left and Right on the tabs or PageUp and PageDown change tab, Tab stays inside, Esc closes. game3d/tools/saves-check.mjs plays it at a size.
- Settings (Jørgen, 2026-10-04: "rework the settings panel it is larger than my screen, cutting off. Remake to look great"; code in game3d/js/ui/settings-view.js and css/settings.css): one panel in six sections. Text: text speed (default fast), Auto speed (slow, normal or fast; normal; how long Auto waits on a line nobody speaks) and Skip unread too (off). Sound: spoken lines on or off, then volumes for master, music, voices and ambience (the voices slider dims while spoken lines are off). Graphics: graphics quality (auto, with a note on what auto picks here), surface detail, chibi cast (off; from the next load, [art-and-sound.md](art-and-sound.md)) and performance numbers (off). Controls: the Say key (desktop only), interface size and reduce motion. Voice & learning: voice input (device, browser or off, with a note on what each does) how many tries a word needs before it's a click (3) and Skip skill checks (off; Jørgen, 2026-10-05: "add a setting to skip the skill checks"; what it does: [systems.md](systems.md), Typing a word). Private: private mode (on at a desk, off on a phone until turned on). Each setting is a row with its name, a short note under it where the name doesn't say enough, and its control on the right: segmented buttons for choices, a switch for on and off, a slider with its number for a volume, and a key cap for the Say key (click it, then press a key; Esc cancels, and a key the game uses is refused with a note saying so).
  - Desktop: a centred panel with the sections in a rail on the left; phone: a sheet from the bottom with the sections as a row of tabs under the header (it scrolls sideways). The header with the close button stays put. The panel is never bigger than the screen, at any interface size: it is at most 460 px tall at desktop scale (560 px and 78 % of the screen on a phone), and a section that doesn't fit scrolls inside it.
  - Keys (and a controller later, through the same keys): Tab moves through everything and stays inside; on the rail Up and Down change section (Left and Right on the phone strip), Right goes into the section; in a section Up and Down go from row to row (Up from the first row goes back to the section's tab), Left and Right change the focused choice, slider or switch; PageUp and PageDown change section from anywhere; Esc closes and gives focus back to where it was. Settings opens again on the section it was left on (until the page reloads).
- Performance numbers (F3 or the Settings switch; kept between visits): a small dark box top right, under the menu button, with the frame rate, average frame time, 1% low, draw calls, triangles, geometries and textures in memory, the JS heap (Chrome only), the place and the graphics tier. It sits under the menus and takes no clicks. How to read the numbers: notes/PERF.md.
- Feedback (F8 or the note button; Jørgen, 2026-09-29: "just a modal with text field and send, that also takes a screenshot there and then"): a small window with a text field, a thumbnail of the screenshot and Send. The game pauses behind it and no key reaches the game while it's open. The screenshot of the world and the HUD is taken before the window shows. Send saves the text, the screenshot and where the game is (build, place, period, story node, goal, the line on screen, player position, viewport) through tools/review_server.py to notes/feedback-game/<time>/, logs the text in notes/feedback-log/, and says "Sent" with a button back to the game. Send is the game's teal outline button, full width; while it sends it reads "Sending…", dimmed (Jørgen, 2026-10-05: "the button to send has gone all white"). Ctrl+Enter sends and Esc closes; an unsent text is kept for next time. If the server doesn't answer within 10 seconds the window says so and the player can send again; closing it while it sends gives up on the send, so a stalled server never leaves the game paused. The screenshot shows what is typed in a text field, such as a romaji answer half typed, and the context lists it too. Only on a local address where the server answers; tests and captures leave it out unless the URL has ?feedback.
- A loading chip between places, and a dimmed frame, only when the walk out is over and the next place is still being built. A watched walk, the lift ride, or a trip to a place already built never shows it or dims the screen.
- The end of the day: the places (a frame of every place Eric was in, in the order he got there, including the walk home and his room), the people met and the words he can use; the story's closing line; back to the title, after everything else (it never covers the list). Eric's room's photo is only taken once he's inside it, never on the corridor, and is refreshed when the day ends there (issue #92). No quiz and no score.
- The build id shows in a corner (game3d/build.json).

## Camera

- Top-down 3D, looking down at each place with its near walls cut away. Conversations zoom in on the person who matters, then back.
- The player character is always in view (Jørgen: the lift ride showed only the outside of the lift during a long sequence).
- Wherever a place's framing would leave part of Eric off screen (the end of its follow range, a fixed whole-room view, a close-up still up while he can walk, such as the office greeting holding on Mori), the camera slides along the ground just far enough to bring his whole body back in, about 4 % in from the edges. Where he's already in view, the place's framing is unchanged (issue #121). game3d/tools/cam-coverage.mjs stands him on every reachable spot of every place and checks him against the frame at 390 × 844, 1366 × 860 and 1920 × 1080; run it after changing a camera fit, clamp or cam.js, or a place's walk grid.
- After a walk between two outdoor places, the close framing he arrives on moves with him as he walks in onto the lane, then lets go to the place's own framing; it never stays on the edge while he walks off the screen (a phone lost him for a few seconds at the office quarter and the east coast). game3d/tools/loop-check.mjs walks every trip between the outdoor places and fails if his feet or head leave the frame while he walks in and the camera lets go.
- While a word to type or replies to pick wait on the player, the camera pulls back a little, holding the top edge of the view, until Eric and the thing he's saying the word to are on screen and clear of the dialogue box; it eases back when the prompt is answered (issue #80; game3d/tools/prompt-shots.mjs checks every prompt of the day).
- Ceiling light fixtures are not shown in the top-down scenes (they get in the way); their light stays (Jørgen).

## Phone and desktop

- The phone layout is used when the screen is taller than it is wide (width to height under 0.8) or narrower than 640 px. Phone and desktop have separate layouts (GUIDE, Visual design).
- The UI scales with the screen: 1 at 1366 × 860 and up to 2 on large screens (QHD and 4K get a bigger UI by default), times the interface size setting. Phones keep 1.
- The phone keyboard (Jørgen, 2026-10-02, Galaxy S23 in Chrome: "On phone the full panel doesn't show"): while the word field has focus and the keyboard is up, the whole typing prompt (the line, the word card, the field, the mic, the hint and "Never mind") sits just above the keyboard and the browser bar, a little tighter, and the portraits step out until the keyboard closes. The 3D view keeps its size. game3d/tools/keyboard-fit.mjs checks it with the visible area cut to keyboard heights at phone sizes.
- The game targets desktop first; Android comes later.
